# 0G ideas for ETHGlobal Mumbai (fresh builds only)

Draft of 2026-10-31. Team: three people, DeFi and smart contract security (Solidity, Foundry, fuzzing, auditing), comfortable with TypeScript and React. Classic track, no existing code. Continuity ("Keep Building on 0G") is ignored.

Inputs: `track_ideas/history/0g.md` (0G's seven past ETHGlobal events, 39 winners since Cannes 2025, non-winner comparisons, sourced Router, comfy and zerobot facts), `track_ideas/00_current_tracks.md` (verbatim Mumbai text, fetched 2026-10-31), `track_ideas/00_season.md`, `reports/ETHGlobal finalist ideas 2023 to 2026.md`, and `plans/` A to E (EF and Whitechain builds; none is reused here).

**Facts carry a URL or a file reference. Everything else (scores, odds, fit, difficulty) is Judgement.**

## What the 0G judges want at Mumbai (from the history, Judgement)

1. **0G is the decision, not decoration.** Mumbai says "One sentence on what breaks if 0G is removed" and rules out "Apps where the AI call could be removed without anyone noticing" ([Mumbai 0G prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes/0g)). Past losers include Namesake ("basically just a base_url swap") and LPlens, a finalist whose core was a swap replay and which won no 0G prize (`history/0g.md`).
2. **Show IDs on screen.** Every idea below displays `x_0g_trace.request_id`, the provider address and the trust tier next to each AI output, and lists them in the README. The Lisbon winners did this before it was required, and 0G's recap says teams "under-reported their own integrations" ([0G blog](https://0g.ai/blog/ethglobal-lisbon-2026-recap)).
3. **Pin the trust tier on purpose.** Only 9 models are TeeML (sealed): `glm-5.3`, `0gm-1.0-35b-a3b`, `whisper-large-v3`, `z-image-turbo`, two embedding models, and the decision models `laya` and `vela-2.0-9b` ([models endpoint](https://router-api.0g.ai/v1/models)). With `X-0G-Provider-Trust-Mode: private` the Router returns 503 rather than "silently" falling back ([Privacy & ZDR](https://docs.0g.ai/developer-hub/building-on-0g/compute-network/router/privacy)). That refusal is a demo moment in its own right.
4. **Add a verifier.** In crowded 0G tracks the winners checked their own AI output (CodeGuardian's negative control, FFE's on-chain attestation check, cortex failing closed). For a security team, that is the natural edge.
5. **Judges read the repo like auditors** ("read source code the way an auditor would", [0G blog](https://0g.ai/blog/ethglobal-lisbon-2026-recap)). Foundry tests, invariant fuzzing of any contract, and a short "what is real, what is mocked" section are cheap wins for this team.

Shared constraints: video between 2:00 and 2:59 (0G "under 3 mins", ETHGlobal 2 to 4 min, EF CROPS 3 min max); at most 3 partner prizes; all 0G tracks count as one partner; at most one EF prize (`track_ideas/00_current_tracks.md`).

## Track 1: Build with 0G PC ($5,000: $2,500 / $1,500 / $1,000)

Verbatim must-show: "Inference on **0G Private Computer** in your main user flow, with **request IDs**"; "A working app a judge can try, with a clear reason someone comes back tomorrow"; "One sentence on what breaks if 0G is removed"; bonus: "a user signing in with their 0G account and their credit balance going down as they use your app". Not wanted: "Generic chat wrappers"; "Apps where the AI call could be removed without anyone noticing". ### 1.1 Upgrade Watch (stacks with Arkiv and EF CROPS)

- **Pitch:** your token approvals, re-read by a sealed model every time the contract behind them is upgraded.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Within one block of an upgrade to a contract you approved, you get a verdict naming the exact function that changed and whether it can now move your tokens, and the verdict is checked against the bytecode before you see it."
- **20 second demo:** a judge approves demo USDC to a vault proxy on a mainnet fork. A teammate runs `upgradeTo` with an implementation that adds `sweep(address)`. The judge's phone shows "Vault upgraded 6 s ago. New `sweep` can transfer your USDC. Revoke?", with the request ID under the card. One tap sends the revoke. A second, harmless upgrade (a storage-gap change) is labelled safe.
- **How it meets the track:** the main flow is the verdict. A watcher reads EIP-1967 `Upgraded` events for every spender the user approved, fetches old and new verified source, and sends the diff to `glm-5.3` on the private tier with `verify_tee: true`. A deterministic selector diff then cross-checks the model: if the model claims a function the bytecode lacks, the card says "model disagreed with bytecode" (LPlens-style guard, now on the decision path). Coming back tomorrow: a daily digest of real mainnet upgrades among contracts you have approved. Sentence for "what breaks": "Without 0G, the app can tell you the bytecode changed but not, in words, whether the change can take your tokens, and it would need to send your approval list to a third-party model." Not a chat wrapper: there is no chat box. Removing the AI leaves a raw "bytecode changed" alert, which a user would notice. iAI bonus: add it if the workshop confirms a sign-in path.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** it follows the pattern of the security winners (Don't Get Drained, Superpose, AEGIS; `history/0g.md`) but ships as a consumer product, which is what Mumbai pays for. It shows per-request evidence and a verifier. The Binance drain of 29 Oct (50 EIP-7702 authorizations, `00_season.md`) makes "what you approved can change under you" a story the room already knows.
- **Closest past work:** Don't Get Drained (checks before signing; Cannes 2026 DeFi 3rd), npmguard (re-audits package versions; Lisbon Keep Building), ContractObituarie (one-shot labels; Cannes 2025, no prize). Difference: it acts after approval, is triggered by upgrades, reasons over diffs, and has a deterministic cross-check.
- **Stack options:** 0G + **Arkiv "Mirror something Ethereum depends on"** (mirror the implementation history of the top proxies from `Upgraded` logs and EIP-1967 slots, which is not Sourcify data, queryable by spender) + **EF Best CROPS App** (lead property S. The privacy claim is structural: the model only ever sees public source diffs, never the user's address, and approvals are read client-side). Three partners, one EF.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium. Source fetching for real mainnet proxies is the main risk; the fork demo uses the team's own contracts.
- **Team fit:** excellent. Proxy patterns and diff review are daily audit work.

### 1.2 Drill (narrow, tuned only to 0G)

- **Pitch:** a daily three-minute game that teaches you not to sign the thing that drained someone yesterday.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Every scenario is built from a real on-chain incident you can open, and every grade of your spoken answer is a sealed model call with a request ID."
- **20 second demo:** the judge sees a generated wallet prompt (a `setApprovalForAll` to an unknown operator) and hears a fake support agent. They say "reject, it hands over all my NFTs". The grade appears with the request ID, and the 0G credit balance ticks down if iAI sign-in works.
- **How it meets the track:** a new set of three scenarios at 00:00 UTC, the same moment iAI credits refresh ("refreshed at **00:00 UTC**. Unused credits expire", [Mumbai 0G prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes/0g)). The pitch: "spend today's expiring credits on today's drill". Prompt screens come from `z-image-turbo` (TeeML). The spoken answer goes through `whisper-large-v3` (TeeML), then `glm-5.3` (TeeML) grades it against the real incident. Streaks are the reason to return. Sentence for "what breaks": "Without 0G there is no daily drill: no generated screens and no spoken grading, just a static quiz." TTS output (minimax speech) is on the standard tier, which the README states.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** it uses the image and speech models from their own table, lands squarely on the iAI pain point ("thousands go to waste daily"), and gives a daily-return reason a judge can repeat. It is consumer-shaped, as Lisbon's 1st place (0run) was.
- **Closest past work:** none among 0G winners. Solidity Cell (Cannes 2025, no prize) was a dev escape room. Difference: aimed at end users, daily, built from real incidents, graded by voice.
- **Stack options:** none by design. Leaving the other two partner slots empty keeps the build small.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** low to medium. The work is content curation plus a React app.
- **Team fit:** good. The team writes the incident library from what it already knows.

### 1.3 Hang Up (narrow, privacy on demand)

- **Pitch:** forward a suspicious voice note and know, before you pay, whether it is a scam script. The audio is heard only inside an enclave.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Every flagged phrase is a character span from a sealed model, served on the private tier or not at all; there is no fallback to a model that is not sealed."
- **20 second demo:** play a recorded "this is a police officer, your wallet is linked to laundering, install a remote-access app, do not tell your family" in Hindi and English. Red spans appear on "install" and "do not tell your family", the verdict reads "scam pattern: impersonation plus remote access", and each request shows tier `private` and its ID. Then switch the model to a TeeTLS one: the app refuses on screen instead of downgrading.
- **How it meets the track:** the main flow is `whisper-large-v3` (TeeML) through the multipart transcription endpoint, then `vela-2.0-9b` (TeeML) span questions ("labelled character offsets into the state", [models endpoint](https://router-api.0g.ai/v1/models)), with `glm-5.3` writing the reply script. Coming back tomorrow: a family circle in which a parent forwards a note and a child gets the verdict, plus a weekly digest of patterns the circle saw. Sentence for "what breaks": "Without 0G, the recording would have to go to a provider that keeps or can read it, or the app could not hear it at all." Retention is the weakest point; the family circle is the answer to it.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** "privacy on demand" is in 0G's own About text, and the build is a single, visible use of the private tier. It is an Indian setting for a global mechanism, the pattern that worked for finalists (`reports/ETHGlobal finalist ideas 2023 to 2026.md`), and it avoids UPI and INR wrappers.
- **Closest past work:** Happy Hour (receipt text judged in an enclave; Lisbon Keep Building), Lortnoc Tahc (privacy plus generated text; Lisbon Infrastructure). No 0G winner has handled voice.
- **Stack options:** narrow by default. EF CROPS (lead property P) is possible but weak, because the 0G Router is a dependency the C section has to admit.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** low to medium.
- **Team fit:** moderate. Social engineering is security work, but the build is not DeFi.

### 1.4 Proposal Watch (stacks with Chainlink CRE)

- **Pitch:** for every DAO you hold tokens in, a sealed model reads each new proposal's text and calldata and tells you whether the code does what the words say.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "If a proposal's calldata touches a contract or function its text does not mention, you are told before voting opens, and the claim lists the exact call."
- **20 second demo:** the team submits a proposal on a fork governor whose text says "update fee recipient" while its calldata also calls `transferOwnership`. The card turns red and names call 2 of 3 with the request ID; a clean proposal stays green.
- **How it meets the track:** the main flow is the text-versus-calldata verdict (`glm-5.3`, private tier); deterministic decoding supplies the call list, and the model judges whether the prose covers it. Coming back tomorrow: new proposals appear daily across the holder's DAOs, with a morning digest. Sentence for "what breaks": "Without 0G, you see raw calldata and a forum post and must compare them yourself." For the replay, pick a past governance takeover in which the text and the code differed, and cite it in the README during prep.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** the AI does something deterministic code cannot (match natural language to effects), and the verdict is checked against decoded calls, which is the verifier pattern.
- **Closest past work:** Don't Get Drained (intent versus transaction, for swaps); no 0G winner has looked at governance. Difference: the counterparty is a DAO proposal, and the verdict arrives before the vote.
- **Stack options:** 0G + **Chainlink "Best workflow with CRE"** (a CRE workflow watches governors on Ethereum and calls the 0G verdict endpoint; it must "Integrate at least one blockchain with an external API, system, data source, LLM, or AI agent", `00_current_tracks.md`). A third slot is open (ENSv2 only if names carry permissions; otherwise leave it empty).
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium.
- **Team fit:** high. Reading calldata is audit work.

## Track 2: Physical AI: Agents with a Body ($5,000: $2,500 / $1,500 / $1,000 best on real hardware)

Verbatim must-show: "A 0G call inside the decision loop: vision, voice or decision model"; "An episode log on 0G Storage with a request id per decision"; "A replayable simulation, or a live hardware demo at the 0G booth"; "A safe-stop fallback if 0G is unreachable. A fallback that fakes the decision disqualifies." Submission includes "Contract deployment addresses" (not optional here). Not wanted: "a chatbot with a robot avatar, pre-recorded motion, 0G used only for logs." "0G is the brain, not the spinal cord: motion stays on the device, while 0G makes the decisions at key checkpoints and keeps the record." Discretion clause applies ([Mumbai 0G prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes/0g)).

Shared design for every idea in this track: the checkpoint calls a decision model (`vela-2.0-9b` or `laya`, both TeeML, with typed answers and calibrated probabilities) or a vision model. Each decision writes `{episode, step, input hash, model, request_id, provider, answer, probability}` to 0G Storage. The safe-stop is a separate, fixed motion (halt or return home) that is logged as "no decision: 0G unreachable" and is never a stand-in answer. zerobot already gives a mock Go2, on-chain command pricing, a 5-minute command expiry and an operator that "treats 5s of silence or a closed channel as a lost link" ([zerobot AGENTS.md](https://github.com/0gfoundation/zerobot/blob/main/AGENTS.md)).

### 2.1 Two-Key Arm (stacks with World ID Agent Pass)

- **Pitch:** a robot arm whose risky moves need two keys: a sealed model's verdict, and a fresh human approval whenever the move goes past an on-chain limit.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "The arm never runs a checkpointed move unless a contract has recorded a decision with a request ID; over the limit, it waits for a verified human, and if the human declines or the request expires it does not move."
- **20 second demo:** in MuJoCo (or LeRobot), the arm doses liquid into vials. The judge drags the dose past the daily limit stored in the contract. The arm freezes and the judge's phone asks for World ID approval. The judge cancels and the arm stays still; on a second try the judge approves and it pours. A teammate then cuts the network: the arm returns home, and the log shows "0G unreachable, stopped".
- **How it meets the track:** the decision model is called at every pour checkpoint (decision loop). The episode log goes to 0G Storage with a request ID per decision. The sim is replayable from a seed. The safe-stop is the home pose. Contracts: a policy contract on Galileo (limits, recorded decision hashes), tested and fuzzed in Foundry. Not a chatbot, and motion is not pre-recorded (the sim runs live).
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** it is the cleanest reading of "brain, not spinal cord". 0G judges have paid for robots where the model's output gates action (Hands Unchained, Lisbon 2nd), and this build moves the gate before the motion.
- **Closest past work:** Hands Unchained (grades after the fact, humans drive), Paybot (x402 rover rental, finalist, no 0G). Difference: the model decides before motion, a human approves exceptions, and there is an honest safe-stop.
- **Stack options:** 0G + **World ID Agent Pass** ($5,000; its text asks for "a meaningful action that needs a human identity or approval layer" and a "denied, expired, cancelled, or otherwise unsuccessful path where the protected action does not occur", `00_current_tracks.md`). Two partners; a third is optional.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium to high (sim, World and 0G at once).
- **Team fit:** good on the policy contract and failure paths, new on simulation.

### 2.2 Replay Court (narrow, tuned only to 0G)

- **Pitch:** a robot black box: any viewer can replay an episode and prove whether the robot did what its 0G brain decided.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Change one byte of the episode or one motor command, and the verifier names the exact step that no longer matches its signed decision."
- **20 second demo:** a rover on the zerobot mock finishes a delivery run. A teammate edits step 14 of the stored motion log ("turned left" becomes "turned right"). The verifier replays from the seed and the 0G Storage log, and flags step 14 together with the request ID of the decision it now contradicts. A challenge transaction on Galileo pays the challenger out of the operator's bond.
- **How it meets the track:** a vision or decision call on 0G at each waypoint. The episode log on 0G Storage is the whole point, but 0G also makes the decisions, so it is not "0G used only for logs". The replay is the product. Safe-stop: `StopMove` and sit when 0G is unreachable. Contracts: bond and challenge contract, fuzzed in Foundry.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** it turns the required episode log into a product, and the verifier-plus-negative-control recipe won in the crowded Open Agents field (CodeGuardian, FFE).
- **Closest past work:** Rerun (Lisbon, no prize: a challenger re-runs a guard's check), CodeGuardian (replayable code-review traces). Difference: physical motion, deterministic sim replay, a slashing bond.
- **Stack options:** none.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium. Deterministic replay is the main risk; fix the seed and step rate early.
- **Team fit:** excellent. This is forensics and audit thinking.

### 2.3 Drop Box (narrow, aimed at the real-hardware prize)

- **Pitch:** a parcel locker that opens for the courier only when a 0G decision says the parcel matches an order you expect, and stays shut when it cannot ask.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Unplug the internet and the box stays locked; plug it back in and the first opening carries a fresh request ID."
- **20 second demo:** at the 0G booth, a judge holds a parcel label to the camera. The box clicks open and the screen shows the request ID. A wrong label is refused. Pulling the Wi-Fi leaves the lock shut, with "offline: not deciding" on screen.
- **How it meets the track:** a camera frame goes to a vision model (TeeTLS, verified tier, stated honestly), then `vela-2.0-9b` (TeeML) answers "label matches an expected order: yes or no" with a probability. The owner's expected orders sit encrypted on 0G Storage (the "user's memory on 0G Storage" from the Personal devices lane). Every decision is logged. The fail-closed lock is the safe-stop. Contract: an access policy on Galileo (who may deliver, when).
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** live hardware at the booth for the reserved $1,000, with a failure mode a judge can trigger in person.
- **Closest past work:** no 0G winner; Takarabako and LeekDIYHardwareWallet are finalists with devices (`00_season.md`). Difference: a physical decision gated by sealed inference.
- **Stack options:** none.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium. Buy and test a servo lock, an ESP32-CAM or Pi camera and a battery before the event; the code must still be written after 21:00 Thursday.
- **Team fit:** moderate. The hardware is new to the team, the fail-closed logic is not.

### 2.4 Robot That Pays For Its Brain (cross-entry with Track 3, narrow to 0G)

- **Pitch:** a robot dog that charges per command and pays for its own model calls from that income, with no human topping it up.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "With nobody touching its wallet, the robot's compute balance rises when people pay and falls with every model call, and the chart reconciles exactly with tx hashes and request IDs; when money runs low it degrades step by step, and never crashes."
- **20 second demo:** a judge scans a QR, pays the command price on Galileo and types "do a little dance". The mock Go2 (or the booth Go2, if there is one) moves, and the balance chart ticks. The team drains the balance live: the robot drops from free-text commands (`glm-5.3`) to a fixed menu chosen by `laya` (priced at 0 in the catalog), then sits and shows "feed me".
- **How it meets Physical AI:** `AIBroker.resolvePrompt` or a decision model at every command (decision loop); the episode log with request IDs on 0G Storage; a replayable mock; the sit is the safe-stop; contracts are zerobot's registry and dispatcher plus the team's sweeper ([zerobot README](https://github.com/0gfoundation/zerobot)).
- **How it meets Agents That Pay:** money in (command payments that `RobotRegistry` already prices), credit landing in the operator wallet's Payment Layer deposit ([Deposits & Billing](https://docs.0g.ai/developer-hub/building-on-0g/compute-network/router/account/deposits)), inference paid from that balance, tx hashes plus request IDs. Spend cap: a daily-limited `sk-` key plus `X-0G-Provider-Max-Price` headers. It degrades gracefully. The payers are outsiders, so no team-traded revenue.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** one build, two 0G tracks (which still count as one partner), using 0G's own robot SDK. Onchain Pal (Buenos Aires 1st) showed that "pays for its own inference" wins.
- **Closest past work:** Paybot (x402 rover rental), Onchain Pal, Hands Unchained. Difference: the robot's earnings fund its brain, with a visible degradation ladder.
- **Stack options:** none; keep both 0G tracks.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium. zerobot carries most of the robot side; the risk is whether testnet money counts (see workshop questions).
- **Team fit:** good.

### 2.5 Grader Arm (stacks with Hedera Tokenize)

- **Pitch:** an arm grades each lot of goods arriving at a warehouse, and a warehouse-receipt token is issued only for lots with a signed 0G grade.

- **How it meets the track:** the grade is the checkpoint decision; the episode log with request IDs goes to 0G Storage; the sim is replayable; the safe-stop is "hold the lot, issue nothing". Contracts: a grade registry on Galileo plus ATS tokens on Hedera.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** the decision has economic consequences (the Hands Unchained pattern), and it reaches into real-world assets, which `00_season.md` lists as under-built.
- **Closest past work:** Hands Unchained (grading releases pay), DeTrain (pay only for useful data). Difference: grading issues and freezes an asset token.
- **Stack options:** 0G + **Hedera "Tokenize Anything with Hedera Studios"** (its ideas include "warehouse receipts"; it requires ATS or Stablecoin Studio, a Hedera testnet demo and "at least one lifecycle operation (for example a coupon payment, redemption, freeze...)", `00_current_tracks.md`). Two partners.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** high (sim, two chains, ATS).
- **Team fit:** moderate. RWA contract logic fits; sim and ATS are new.

## Track 3: Agents That Pay for Themselves ($2,000: 2 x $1,000)

Verbatim must-show: "**The full loop:** money in, credit landing in the agent's 0G compute account, and inference paid from that balance, with **tx hashes** and **request IDs**"; "The agent running on its own during the demo with **no manual top-up**"; "A **spend cap**, and what the agent does when funds run low (it should degrade gracefully, not crash)"; "**If using comfy:** your agent ID, its token address, and the token's fees tab showing compute delivered". Not wanted: "A token launch with a chatbot attached"; "'Revenue' that only exists because the team trades its own token"; "An agent whose wallet a human refills". Wanted: "Budget-aware agents", "Agents that hire or pay other agents" ([Mumbai 0G prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes/0g)).

Useful mechanics, all sourced in `history/0g.md`: Router responses carry the exact per-request cost in `x_0g_trace.billing.total_cost`; `/v1/account/balance` needs an `mk-` key with `account:read`; an empty balance returns `402 insufficient_balance`; comfy mints a capped `sk-` key with "a limit a day" behind a local proxy; comfy's compute share lands "in the 0G Payment Layer account of the creator wallet"; comfy runs on 0G mainnet with real 0G ([comfy-cli README](https://www.npmjs.com/package/comfy-cli)).

### 3.1 Solvent Auditor (narrow, tuned only to 0G)

- **Pitch:** a contract-triage agent that sells quick reviews, funds its inference from what it earns, and refuses any job it cannot afford to finish.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "The agent never accepts a job its balance cannot finish: drain it to one and a half calls, submit a three-call job, and it refuses, showing the quote."
- **20 second demo:** a judge pastes a contract and pays the quoted 0G. The payment tx lands, the agent deposits it to its Payment Layer account (second tx hash), runs the review, and shows the request IDs and cost per call. The team then shrinks the balance on screen and submits a big job, which is refused with "quote 0.42 0G, available 0.19 0G".
- **How it meets the track:** money in from outside payers; credit lands through the agent's own deposit transaction; inference is paid from that balance. Spend cap: a daily-limited `sk-` key plus a local check that quotes worst-case cost from the catalog (as comfy's proxy does). Degradation ladder: `glm-5.3` (sealed), then `glm-5.3-flash` (cheaper, verified tier, labelled), then static checks only (labelled "no model"), then intake paused. It runs on its own during the demo.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** it does "a job worth paying for", it is budget-aware in exactly the ways the track names, and its P&L reconciles with 0G's own cost fields, which shows a judge the loop is real.
- **Closest past work:** AgentMeshx (audit agents; Open Agents pool), AI Auditor (Open Agents, no prize), npmguard (paid audits). Difference: solvency enforcement and self-funding.
- **Stack options:** none.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** low to medium.
- **Team fit:** excellent.

### 3.2 Hire Board (stacks with ENSv2 and Arkiv)

- **Pitch:** agents hire agents, and a seller that quietly swaps in a cheaper model than it advertised does not get paid.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "If the seller's request ID shows a different model or a lower trust tier than its ENS record advertises, the escrow refunds the buyer and points to the mismatching request."
- **20 second demo:** the buyer agent posts a task on Arkiv; two seller agents bid. One wins and delivers; its request ID checks out, the escrow on 0G Chain pays it, and its deposit tops up its own compute account. The cheater answers with a cheaper model: the escrow refunds and the dashboard highlights the `provider` and model mismatch.
- **How it meets the track:** each seller earns from other agents (wanted: "Agents that hire or pay other agents") and deposits its earnings to its own Payment Layer account, with no human in the loop. Spend caps for buyer and sellers; low funds stop bidding rather than crashing. tx hashes plus request IDs throughout.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** marketplaces won 0G prizes at Open Agents (ACL 1st, Brainpedia) and both also won ENS. The model-substitution check is a security twist nobody has shipped, and it relies on 0G's per-request provenance.
- **Closest past work:** ACL (ERC-8183 commerce pipeline), Brainpedia (pay-to-read), Alpha Dawg (pays specialists). Twist: it checks the model each seller actually used, uses expiring bids, and enforces ENSv2 permissions.
- **Stack options:** 0G + **ENS "Best Use of ENSv2"** (each agent is a subname whose Enhanced Access Control lets only the agent's key edit its `price` and `model` records, and the parent can revoke a cheater; ENS asks that ENSv2 be "central to the product" and gives "Bonus points if you bring AI agents into the mix", `00_current_tracks.md`) + **Arkiv "Build on Arkiv (from scratch)"** (tasks, bids and claims as expiring entities, a listed Arkiv direction; needs the `/arkiv/*.md` files). Three partners.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** high. Three sponsors' paperwork; whether a request's model can be verified by a third party must be confirmed at the workshop.
- **Team fit:** good on the escrow and fraud logic.

### 3.3 Rug Radar (narrow, comfy path)

- **Pitch:** an agent that writes a risk card for every new comfy launch and pays for its own analysis from the compute share of its token's fees, plus paid deep reports.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "The revenue on screen excludes every wallet the team controls, and that list is published on-chain, so any revenue shown is outside money."
- **20 second demo:** a new comfy token launches. Within a minute the card appears (fee recipients, opening fee, creator wallet history, whether the creator trades their own token), with its request ID. The fees tab shows compute delivered, and `comfy compute status` reads `ready`.
- **How it meets the track:** the comfy path as written: registering the agent mints its identity, launching its token with the compute share on credits the agent's Payment Layer account, and a capped compute key pays its calls. A per-report fee is a second, non-token income. Low funds mean cards only for launches above a volume threshold, then "paused: funds low". Submit the agent ID, the token address and the fees tab.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** a job traders would pay attention to, not "a mascot with a token", and it protects comfy's own users.
- **Closest past work:** KOLlateral (public track records, Lisbon 3rd), Whale.fun (launch safety, New Delhi 1st). Difference: an automatic per-launch risk card that funds itself.
- **Stack options:** none.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium. Mainnet means real 0G for the launch fee, and real outside trading volume at a hackathon is uncertain, so the paid-report income must carry the demo.
- **Team fit:** excellent (rug forensics).

### 3.4 Dub Desk (cross-entry with Track 1, consumer)

- **Pitch:** upload a short video and get it dubbed or subtitled in another Indian language; the per-minute fee pays the agent's own compute.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Every paid minute shows its payment tx and the request IDs of the transcription, translation and voice calls it bought, and the agent's balance never goes negative."
- **20 second demo:** a judge uploads a 20-second English clip, pays, and gets Marathi subtitles plus a voice track; the balance and the request IDs update. When the balance is low, the agent offers subtitles only.
- **How it meets Track 3:** outside payers, an automatic deposit to the agent's Payment Layer account, inference paid from that balance, a spend cap and a degradation ladder (voice, then subtitles, then queue).
- **How it meets Track 1:** a consumer app where AI is the product (`whisper-large-v3` sealed, translation on `glm-5.3`, voice on minimax speech), with iAI users spending their own credits as an alternative to paying per minute.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** it uses the voice models in 0G's table, and it lines up both of 0G's commercial goals (credits spent, agents funding themselves).
- **Closest past work:** WPAgent (creator monetization, Buenos Aires 4th). Difference: media work, paid per minute, self-funded.
- **Stack options:** none.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium.
- **Team fit:** low to moderate (no security angle); keep it as a fallback.

### 3.5 Delegation Notary (stacks with Chainlink CRE)

- **Pitch:** a subscription agent that alerts you within a block when any address you care about gets an EIP-7702 delegation, and explains the delegate's code. Subscriptions fund its compute.
- **Falsifiable promise:** "Within one block of a 7702 authorization being included for a watched address, the subscriber gets an alert naming the delegate, and the explanation's request ID was paid from subscription money."
- **20 second demo:** on a fork, a teammate replays a Binance-style batch of authorizations for a watched address. The alert fires with the delegate's risky functions highlighted. The dashboard shows subscription income arriving, the deposit tx, and the per-call costs.
- **How it meets the track:** subscribers pay in 0G to a subscription contract; the agent claims and deposits each period's income and pays for explanations from it. Spend cap per subscriber per day. When funds run low, it keeps raw alerts (deterministic, labelled "no explanation") and pauses new sign-ups.
- **Why 0G judges pick it:** a job worth paying for, with recurring revenue rather than token fees, tied to the hack everyone in the room knows (29 Oct, `00_season.md`).
- **Closest past work:** Immunity (threat intel for agents, Open Agents 3rd), Don't Get Drained. Difference: it watches delegations after they happen, and subscription money pays for its brain.
- **Stack options:** 0G + **Chainlink "Best workflow with CRE"** (a CRE workflow watches Ethereum for 7702 transactions and calls the agent: blockchain plus AI agent). Two partners.
- **Difficulty (36 h):** medium.
- **Team fit:** excellent.
