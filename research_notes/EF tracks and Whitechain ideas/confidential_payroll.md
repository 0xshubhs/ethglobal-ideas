# Confidential On-Chain Payroll and B2B Payments on EVM Chains (state as of 2026-10-26)

Scope note: this brief covers (a) the privacy problem, (b) technical approaches and which run on a plain EVM chain, (c) existing products and what they lack, (d) compliance and selective disclosure, (e) prior ETHGlobal work, (f) EF / PSE / IPTF positions, (g) the most credible weekend architecture, and (h) concrete hackathon prototypes with difficulty estimates. Facts carry inline sources. Everything under "Inferences" is my reasoning, not a documented fact. Difficulty estimates are mine.

## 1. What exactly leaks when payroll and B2B payments run on a public EVM chain?

### Takeaway
Plain ERC-20 payroll exposes four things at once: per-employee salary amounts, the employer's treasury balance and runway, the link between an employee's identity and their wallet (and so their whole spending history), and the employer's counterparty graph (suppliers, contractors, pricing, timing). Most "private" products today hide only amounts and leave addresses and timing public.

### Cited Findings
- A Starknet RFP (published 2026-05-26) calls salary visibility "the single largest barrier to crypto-native companies hiring senior engineers and execs", and says that with public contributor payments "top earners get profiled, recruiters scrape leaderboards, phishers target high-value addresses" ([Starknet STRK20 RFP: Private payroll](https://strk20.starknet.io/rfp/private-payroll)).
- The same RFP lists what must be hidden: per-recipient amounts (from the public and from other recipients), vesting amounts and timing (so unlocks cannot be front-run), termination events, and recipient identities. Only aggregate treasury spend should stay public (its example: "$847K to 73 contributors in a quarter") ([Starknet RFP](https://strk20.starknet.io/rfp/private-payroll)).
- EthSystems (the spun-out EF Institutional Privacy Task Force) frames B2B: "Business-to-business payment patterns reveal supplier relationships, pricing, and strategic priorities." Must hide: amounts, counterparty identities, payment purpose/memo, timing patterns ([EthSystems: Private Payments use case](https://ethsystems.org/use-cases/private-payments/)).
- EthSystems' homepage premise: a public ledger exposes positions, counterparties and flows that institutions "are not allowed to reveal" ([EthSystems](https://ethsystems.org/)).
- An Aztec grant application for private payroll (2026-04-16) states the problem: salary structures, team sizes and compensation are visible to on-chain observers; mixers remove the audit trail needed for MiCA, FinCEN and FATF; and "no production-grade system lets a company prove correct payment to a regulator without revealing individual details" ([Aztec forum: zkCompliance grant](https://forum.aztec.network/t/grant-application-zkcompliance-protocol-private-payroll-compliance-on-aztec/8545)).
- Streaming payroll is public by design: Sablier's payroll page stresses that teams "can maintain complete transparency", so individual pay is visible on any block explorer ([Sablier payroll](https://sablier.com/payroll); [Sablier blog](https://blog.sablier.com/crypto-payroll-tools-compared-sablier-vs-traditional-solutions)).
- Even FHE payroll leaves the graph public: Zama's payroll page says "sender and recipient addresses stay public", and only amounts and balances are encrypted ([Zama: Confidential onchain payroll](https://www.zama.org/solutions/confidential-onchain-payroll)). Avalanche eERC likewise keeps "sender and receiver addresses, and timestamps" public ([Avalanche docs: Encrypted ERC](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/encrypted-erc)).
- Shielded pools leak at the edges: "Transfers hide amounts, but shielding and unshielding do not", and "A pool with ten participants offers weak unlinkability regardless of the cryptography" ([EthSystems: Shielded pools writeup, 2026-02-19](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)).
- Metadata leaks: EthSystems notes network timing correlation leaks in both L1 and L2 approaches, and third-party RPC note discovery leaks the spent-note index ([EthSystems: Private Payments approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)). L2BEAT lists Railgun privacy loss if a user sends from their own public address, or uses public RPCs that see public addresses and private transactions in one session ([L2BEAT: Railgun](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/railgun)).
- Stealth addresses have a gas-funding leak: funding the stealth address from a wallet linked to the recipient can break privacy ([ERC-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564)). An ETHGlobal project (Mercury) makes the same point: a per-payment stealth address needs a gas top-up that can deanonymize the recipient ([ETHGlobal: Mercury](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/mercury-rgq4m)).

### Inferences
- A useful threat taxonomy for a pitch: (1) amount privacy, (2) balance/treasury privacy, (3) counterparty/graph privacy, (4) identity-to-address linkage, (5) timing/metadata privacy (payroll runs on a fixed calendar, so timing is a strong fingerprint), (6) read privacy (who queries which notes/balances via RPC). Each approach in Section 2 covers a different subset.
- Treasury exposure is often overlooked: if the employer funds a pool or a confidential wrapper with one visible deposit per payroll cycle, the cycle total and cadence are public even if per-employee splits are hidden. Keeping the treasury inside the private system, and topping up with irregular amounts, mitigates this.
- Employee off-ramp is the weakest link: the first time an employee unshields or moves funds to an exchange deposit address, their salary stream becomes linkable unless they withdraw to a fresh address with sponsored gas.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study of how many crypto employees have been deanonymized or targeted because of public payroll; evidence is anecdotal or from RFPs and vendor pages.

## 2. Which technical approaches exist, what are their tradeoffs, and which run on a plain EVM chain with only standard precompiles?

### Takeaway
Only ZK-based designs (shielded UTXO pools, ZK+ElGamal confidential balances like eERC or Zeto) and stealth addresses run on an arbitrary EVM chain with standard precompiles (BN254 ecAdd/ecMul/ecPairing). FHE (Zama, Fhenix), TEE (Inco Lightning, Oasis Sapphire) and MPC (TACEO) all need an off-chain network or a special chain that the provider must support on that chain. Privacy L2s (Aztec) and Plasma (Intmax) are separate systems, not something you deploy on Whitechain.

### Cited Findings
**Shielded UTXO pools (Railgun, Privacy Pools, Hinkal, Unlink, EthSystems PoC, Paladin Zeto)**
- EthSystems L1 shielded-pool PoC design: notes = (token, amount, owner_pubkey, salt); commitment = poseidon(...); nullifier = poseidon(commitment, spending_key); two LeanIMT trees from PSE zk-kit (commitments, KYC attestations); three Noir circuits (deposit, 2-in-2-out transfer, withdraw), UltraHonk proofs; contracts ShieldedPool, AttestationRegistry, CompositeVerifier; note encryption ECDH + HKDF + ChaCha20-Poly1305 with secp256k1 viewing keys ([EthSystems writeup, 2026-02-19](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/); [spec](https://github.com/ethsystems/pocs/blob/master/pocs/private-payment/shielded-pool/SPEC.md)).
- Its stated limitations: fixed 2-in-2-out (no batching yet), no viewing-key revocation, no gas paymaster (user addresses leak), in-memory Merkle trees, not audited ([EthSystems writeup](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)).
- Benchmarks for the L1 shielded option: deposit ~155K gas + ~2.6M verification; transfer ~181K + ~2.6M; withdraw ~47K + ~2.6M; client proof generation 410-991ms; no trusted setup (UltraHonk, universal KZG SRS). CROPS: CR high, OS yes, Privacy partial, Security high. "Avoid for high-frequency, low-value transfers" ([EthSystems: approach-private-payments](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)).
- Groth16 verifier cost is roughly 207k + 7.16k x (number of public signals) gas, per a forum estimate ([Aztec forum: verification cost](https://forum.aztec.network/t/benchmark-for-verification-cost-across-noir-proof-backends/8061)). Railgun (Groth16) has a trusted setup with 55 phase-1 and 304 phase-2 contributions ([L2BEAT: Railgun](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/railgun)). Hinkal uses Groth16 ([EthSystems: Hinkal](https://ethsystems.org/vendors/hinkal/)). Unlink uses Groth16 proofs verified by an on-chain contract ([Avalanche docs: Unlink](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/unlink)).
- Paladin's Zeto is "a ZK UTXO token implementation" in circom (Groth16 by default), compatible with "any unmodified EVM chain"; it is an LF Decentralized Trust project ([LF Decentralized Trust: Paladin](https://www.lfdecentralizedtrust.org/blog/paladin-a-unified-programmable-privacy-framework-for-evm-becomes-newest-lf-decentralized-trust-project)). Zeto is Apache-2.0, has 2-in/2-out tokens plus `_batch` versions with 10 inputs/outputs, and claims "non-repudiation with respect to a trusted auditing authority, specified at contract-creation time" ([GitHub: LFDT-Paladin/zeto](https://github.com/LFDT-Paladin/zeto)).
- Privacy Pools (0xbow): deposit and withdraw with ZK proofs; an Association Set Provider (ASP) maintains approved deposit labels; non-approved depositors can "ragequit" publicly; circuits include Commitment, LeanIMT and Withdrawal; repo 0xbow-io/privacy-pools-core; the docs describe no private transfers inside the pool ([Privacy Pools docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/)). Launched on Ethereum mainnet March 2025; stablecoins added June 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348959/privacy-pools-vitalik-buterin); [The Block: USDS](https://www.theblock.co/post/363007/zero-knowledge-powered-privacy-pools-integrates-skys-usds-stablecoin-in-first-expansion-into-multi-asset-pools)).

**ZK + additively homomorphic encryption confidential balances (eERC, Solana CT as reference)**
- Avalanche eERC: balances and amounts encrypted with ElGamal over BabyJubJub, correctness via Groth16; "requires EVM chain with BN254 elliptic-curve precompiles" and block gas limit above ~1M; deployable on Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, Avalanche, or "any EVM chain" meeting those requirements; confidential transfer "~947,000 gas, roughly 15x a standard ERC-20 transfer"; single rotatable auditor key; Standalone and Converter (wrap ERC-20) modes; SDK `@avalabs/eerc-sdk`; repo ava-labs/EncryptedERC ([Avalanche docs: Encrypted ERC](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/encrypted-erc)). Avalanche Academy pages list only Avalanche C-Chain, Fuji and custom L1s, so "any EVM" is asserted on one page only ([Avalanche Academy: eERC](https://docs.avax.network/academy/blockchain/encrypted-erc/04-usability-eerc/01-deployments-mode)).
- Solana Confidential Transfers (reference design): each transfer carries three ElGamal public keys (sender, receiver, auditor); amounts are restricted to 48 bits and split into 16-bit low and high parts to keep decryption tractable ([Solana: ZK proofs](https://spl.solana.com/confidential-token/deep-dive/zkps)). The auditor secret key decrypts every transfer amount for the mint; historical transfers stay encrypted to whichever auditor key was active, so old keys must be retained ([Solana issuer guide](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/issuer-guide)). Scheme: twisted ElGamal, sigma protocols, Bulletproof range proofs ([third-party audit, 2025-10-29](https://app.notion.com/p/Solana-s-Confidential-Transfer-Audit-292296c11552801587f4f1a2f6f503ea)).

**Stealth addresses (ERC-5564 / ERC-6538, Umbra, Fluidkey)**
- ERC-5564 is Final; scheme 1 = secp256k1 with view tags (about 6x less parsing work; 1 byte of the shared secret reduces security from 128 to 124 bits); singleton ERC5564Announcer at `0x55649E01B5Df198D18D95b5cc5051630cfD45564` deployed via CREATE2 through the deterministic deployer `0x4e59b44847b379578588920ca78fbf26c0b4956c`; no precompiles named ([ERC-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564)).
- ERC-6538 is Final; registry at `0x6538E6bf4B0eBd30A8Ea093027Ac2422ce5d6538` via the same deterministic deployer; `registerKeysOnBehalf` takes EIP-712 or EIP-1271 signatures; the spec mentions un-registering but defines no un-register function ([ERC-6538](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-6538)).
- Stealth addresses hide the recipient, not the amount or the sender.

**FHE (Zama fhEVM / ERC-7984, Fhenix CoFHE, Inco FHE)**
- ERC-7984 needs an off-chain coprocessor network, a threshold KMS and a Gateway; Zama claims EVM chains can support it "without any protocol upgrades", and lists gas cost and off-chain decryption as limitations ([Zama: ERC-7984 explained, 2026-02-17](https://www.zama.org/post/erc-7984-the-confidential-token-standard-explained)).
- OpenZeppelin confidential contracts: "All contracts must set their respective co-processor configuration during construction", e.g. by inheriting `ZamaEthereumConfig`; "the code is not formally audited" ([OpenZeppelin confidential contracts](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/confidential-contracts)). OpenZeppelin lists ERC-7984 as a draft standard ([OpenZeppelin privacy](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/impact/privacy)).
- Zama Protocol mainnet launched on Ethereum 2025-12-30 ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/zama-protocol-launches-mainnet-on-ethereum-plans-token-listing-in-april-2026-50660)); Zama's chains page lists Ethereum (1), Gateway (261131), BSC (56), HyperEVM (999) and Solana ([Zama docs: chains](https://docs.zama.org/protocol/protocol-apps/chains)). Staking secured by 18 governance-elected operators ([Staking Rewards](https://www.stakingrewards.com/asset/zama)).
- Fhenix CoFHE reported live on Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base (secondary source) ([Hackernoon](https://hackernoon.com/canopy-and-fhenix-want-to-make-encrypted-data-the-default-state-of-onchain-apps); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/fhenix-goes-live-on-ethereum-and-arbitrum-letting-contracts-compute-on-hidden-balances/)). EthSystems still describes Fhenix as testnet in its approach page ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)): sources conflict.
- Circle Research + Inco published a "Confidential ERC-20 Framework" (2024-10-28): wraps ERC-20s with FHE, grants viewing rights to auditors/regulators, enforces AML rules on ciphertext; Circle says it is research, not a product ([Circle blog](https://circle.com/blog/confidential-erc-20-framework-for-compliant-on-chain-privacy); [Inco blog](https://inco.org/blog/circle-research-inco-confidential-erc20-report)).

**TEE (Inco Lightning, Oasis Sapphire)**
- Inco Lightning is "powered by verifiable compute within a Trusted Execution Environment"; live on Base mainnet; audited by Trail of Bits; cToken SDK `@inco/ctoken` wraps ERC-20s ([Inco Lightning](https://www.inco.org/lightning); [Inco: cToken on Base](https://www.inco.org/blog/confidential-token-live-on-base-mainnet)).
- Oasis Sapphire runs the EVM inside a TEE (Intel SGX per one source) with encrypted contract state; it is its own chain ([Oasis Sapphire](https://oasis.net/sapphire); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/caerlower/building-private-by-default-apps-with-tees-the-oasis-stack-51p1)).
- EthSystems CROPS for TEE payments: CR medium, OS no, Privacy full, Security low; "Avoid if the threat model includes nation-state-class side-channel adversaries" ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)).

**MPC, Plasma, privacy L2s**
- MPC (vendor TACEO Merces): committee computes on secret-shared balances, co-SNARKs verified on-chain; counterparties public unless configured; best for "bilateral or club settlement among known counterparties" ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)).
- Stateless Plasma (Intmax2): deposit ~137K gas, withdraw ~343K, batch ~255K (amortized); user proofs 5.9-9.8s; "user data loss becomes lost funds" ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)).
- Aztec: critical proving-system vulnerability found 2026-03-17 with fixes deferred to v5 ([Aztec blog](https://aztec.network/blog/aztec-tge-next-steps)); Alpha V5 since activated on mainnet, still alpha, with Nyx wallet for private transfers ([Aztec: introducing Alpha V5](https://aztec.network/blog/introducing-alpha-v5); [The Block](https://theblock.co/post/409065/aztec-upgrades-v5-alpha-adding-private-execution-environment-decentralized-ethereum-l2)).
- EY Nightfall_4: ZK rollup (April 2025) for private ERC-20/721/1155/3525 transfers; deployed as an L3 on Celo for B2B payments (2025-09-30); X.509-gated; EthSystems notes no built-in viewing keys ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250930088197/en/Celo-Becomes-First-Payments-Focused-Blockchain-to-Deploy-Nightfall-Privacy-Layer-for-Enterprise-Payments); [EthSystems: EY](https://ethsystems.org/vendors/ey/)).

**Target chain: Whitechain**
- Whitechain was built as a go-ethereum fork and claims compatibility "with all existing smart contracts on Ethereum and Ethereum tooling" ([GitHub: whitebit-exchange/wbt](https://github.com/whitebit-exchange/wbt)).
- On 2026-08-18 Whitechain announced a relaunch as an OP Stack Ethereum L2 (WBT as gas token), with a public testnet, a builder program up to $300,000 per project, and no mainnet date given ([The Defiant press release](https://thedefiant.io/news/press-releases/whitechain-relaunches-as-w-groups-distribution-first-ethereum-layer-2)). Mainnet is targeted for later in 2026 ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/markets/wbts-new-high-comes-as-whitechain-prepares-its-ethereum-layer-2-transition)). The release mentions no privacy features.

### Inferences
- "Plain EVM" compatibility matrix (my synthesis):
  - Runs on Whitechain or any geth fork / OP Stack chain today, permissionlessly: stealth addresses (pure Solidity, if the deterministic deployer exists), shielded UTXO pools with Groth16 or UltraHonk verifiers (need BN254 precompiles 0x06-0x08, present since Byzantium in every geth fork), eERC (BN254 + gas limit > 1M), Zeto, Privacy Pools core (self-deploy contracts plus your own ASP).
  - Needs the provider to support that chain: Zama ERC-7984 (coprocessors, KMS, Gateway), Fhenix CoFHE, Inco Lightning (TEE operators), TACEO MPC committee, Unlink (they deploy contracts; chain list not documented).
  - A separate system, not deployable on Whitechain: Aztec, Intmax, Oasis Sapphire, Secret, Aleo, Canton, Nightfall (a rollup you would run yourself).
- For a hackathon on a fresh chain, Groth16 (circom) is cheaper to verify (~250-300K gas) than UltraHonk (~2.6M); Noir is faster to write. On an OP Stack L2 the gas difference matters less.
- On a new chain the anonymity set starts near zero. A demo should say so plainly and push hiding amounts in-circuit (so a small set still hides amounts), plus batching many employees per payout so each run mixes recipients.
- The deterministic deployer at `0x4e59...4956c` is an OP Stack preinstall to my knowledge, so the canonical ERC-5564/6538 addresses can probably be reproduced on Whitechain's OP Stack testnet. Verify before relying on it.

### Gaps
- No official Whitechain developer docs were found: chain IDs, EVM fork level (Cancun/Prague), whether BLS12-381 (EIP-2537) or P256 (RIP-7212) precompiles exist, and whether the deterministic deployer is present. Verify with `cast` against the testnet RPC.
- No measured UltraHonk-vs-Groth16 Solidity verifier benchmark was found beyond the EthSystems ~2.6M figure and the Groth16 formula estimate.
- Privacy Pools' current chain list and the proof system named in its docs were not confirmed.

## 3. What existing products and protocols do private payroll or B2B payments, and what does each lack?

### Takeaway
Mainstream crypto payroll (Deel, Rise, Bitwage, Sablier, Superfluid, LlamaPay) offers no on-chain privacy. Private payroll has arrived in 2025-2026 mostly on non-EVM or special stacks (Aleo via Request Finance and Toku, Canton, Zama fhEVM on Ethereum) or as one vendor on Ethereum (Hinkal Prime). None of them gives an open, chain-agnostic, self-deployable stack with batch payouts, scoped auditor keys and employee proof-of-income on an arbitrary EVM chain.

### Cited Findings
- **Zama / Bron**: "first confidential payroll on Ethereum mainnet", 2026-01-16, CFO paid salaries in confidential USDT (ERC-7984); "Only the sender and each recipient know the exact figures"; no auditor mechanism described ([Zama blog](https://www.zama.org/post/the-first-confidential-payroll-on-ethereum-mainnet)). Addresses remain public ([Zama payroll page](https://www.zama.org/solutions/confidential-onchain-payroll)).
- **Zama ecosystem payroll apps** (unaudited community projects): DripPay (Sepolia, encrypted batch payouts), BlocPaie (encrypted payee address and salary in cheques), Blindroll (encrypted treasury-vs-salary comparison), cPay (ERC-7984 MVP that ingests ISO 20022 pain.001 files), Paychain (Zama Mainnet Season 1 winner) ([Zama community: DripPay](https://community.zama.org/t/drippay-confidential-onchain-payroll-with-fhe/4339); [BlocPaie](https://community.zama.org/t/blocpaie-gasless-confidential-on-chain-payroll-powered-by-zama-fhevm/4340); [Blindroll](https://community.zama.org/t/blindroll-encrypted-onchain-payroll-via-fhevm/4348); [Zama Developer Hub](https://zama.org/developer-hub)).
- **Hinkal Prime** (launched 2026-05-27): treasury dashboard for private payroll, batch payouts via CSV, approval workflows; Groth16 shielded pool; Chainalysis KYT on deposits; viewing keys for auditors; claims EVM chains, Solana and Tron ([OurCryptoTalk](https://ourcryptotalk.com/news/hinkal-prime-launches-institutional-privacy-treasury)). EthSystems lists Hinkal only on Ethereum and notes open-source status and audits are not stated ([EthSystems: Hinkal](https://ethsystems.org/vendors/hinkal/)). DeFiLlama TVL about $7.72M ([DeFiLlama](https://DeFillama.com/protocol/hinkal)).
- **Request Finance + Aleo** (2025-09-04): "first private crypto payroll" on Aleo ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250904207682/en/The-Aleo-Network-Foundation-and-Request-Finance-Partner-to-Launch-First-Private-Crypto-Payroll)); Aleo's page says Request Finance currently supports the ALEO native token, not stablecoins ([Aleo payroll](https://aleo.org/payroll)).
- **Toku + Aleo + Paxos Labs**: private stablecoin payroll in USAD, integrated with ADP, Workday, UKG and Gusto via Toku's API; launched January 2026 ([Aleo payroll](https://aleo.org/payroll); [Aleo solutions](https://aleo.org/solutions/)). Not EVM.
- **Canton Network**: a "major global enterprise" ran its first private stablecoin payroll on 2026-02-10; no technical detail ([Canton](https://www.canton.network/news/canton-network-executes-first-private-stablecoin-payroll-run)). Not EVM.
- **Deel**: added USDC/EURC stablecoin payouts in 2026 (sources conflict on February vs May 2026, MoonPay Iron vs Polygon); taxes computed in fiat first ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/deel-brings-stablecoin-payroll-mainstream-104806767.html); [Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-payroll-direct-deposit)). No privacy feature found.
- **Rise**: over $1.3B payroll, more than half in stablecoins; "Rise ID" on-chain professional identity ([Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/best-crypto-payroll-platform-rise/); [Rise vs Bitwage](https://www.riseworks.io/compare/rise-vs-bitwage)). **Bitwage**: founded 2014, reportedly acquired by Paystand late 2025 ([Spark comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/stablecoin-payroll-provider-comparison)). No on-chain privacy feature found for either.
- **Sablier**: fully public streams ([Sablier payroll](https://sablier.com/payroll)). RIAD Finance (hackathon, self-reported) layers Fhenix CoFHE on Sablier LockupLinear to hide stream amounts ([HackQuest: RIAD Finance](https://arbitrum-nyc.hackquest.io/projects/RIAD-Finance)).
- **Railgun**: Ethereum plus Arbitrum and Base deployments mentioned in governance; TVL ~$107M; shield/unshield fee 0.25% each; broadcasters over Waku ([L2BEAT: Railgun](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/railgun)). EthSystems says it has no built-in regulator viewing keys or scoped audit paths ([EthSystems: Railgun](https://ethsystems.org/vendors/railgun/)).
- **Privacy Pools**: deposit/withdraw only, no in-pool transfers in the docs ([Privacy Pools docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/)); $3.5M seed November 2025 ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/0xbow-raises-usd3-5-million-to-expand-privacy-pools)).
- **Fluidkey**: Safe-based stealth accounts on ERC-5564; a Dedaub audit notes the server uses the private viewing key to derive ephemeral keys, which differs from ERC-5564, so security depends on the Fluidkey server ([Dedaub audit](https://dedaub.com/audits/fluidkey/fluidkey-stealth-account-kit-may-24-2024/); [L2BEAT: Fluidkey](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/fluidkey)). Mainly on Base ([Bankless](https://www.bankless.com/fr/read/fluidkey-bank-base.md)). No payroll or accountant features were found.
- **Umbra**: over $500M across 270,000 stealth payments on Ethereum and L2s (self-reported) ([Gitcoin: Umbra](https://checker.gitcoin.co/public/project/show/umbra-1)).
- **Unlink**: on-chain contract plus TS SDK, Groth16, hides sender/recipient/amount for internal transfers; relayed gasless withdrawals; ERC-4337 `execute()` ([Avalanche docs: Unlink](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/unlink)). Chain list, compliance features and license are not documented there.
- **Paladin (Zeto, Noto, Pente)**: enterprise privacy on any unmodified EVM chain ([LFDT](https://www.lfdecentralizedtrust.org/blog/paladin-a-unified-programmable-privacy-framework-for-evm-becomes-newest-lf-decentralized-trust-project)); aimed at permissioned enterprise networks (Kaleido) ([Kaleido](https://www.kaleido.io/paladin)).
- **ERC-7984 adoption** remains concentrated in Zama's own ecosystem ([DefiLlama research](https://defillama.com/research/report/zama-a-look-into-confidential-defi)); ERC7984Rwa is still a draft without fully integrated identity and compliance ([Zama forum](https://community.zama.org/t/standard-for-rwas-using-zama/4305)).

### Inferences
- Gap matrix: FHE products hide amounts but not the graph, and need Zama-supported chains. Shielded pools hide graph and amounts but have weak, all-or-nothing viewing keys, no batching (EthSystems PoC) and tiny anonymity sets on new chains. Stealth-address products hide recipients only. Commercial private payroll (Toku, Request, Canton) sits on non-EVM chains. Hinkal is the closest EVM competitor but is a single vendor stack with unclear open-source status.
- None of the products I found ships employee-side proof-of-income or an employer-side "payroll book completeness" proof.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Superfluid's or LlamaPay's privacy roadmap, nor Deel/Rise custody details.
- No source confirmed whether Hinkal, Unlink or Railgun are deployed (or deployable by third parties) on Whitechain.

## 4. How do selective disclosure, viewing keys, auditor keys, proof of income and zkTLS work, and is there a standard?

### Takeaway
There is no cross-protocol standard. Each system has its own mechanism: all-history viewing keys (Railgun, Hinkal, EthSystems PoC), a single global auditor key (eERC, Solana CT), per-address observer ACLs (ERC-7984), ASP allowlists (Privacy Pools) and PPOI (Railgun). EthSystems lists "No common selective-disclosure format across MiCA, GENIUS, and national regimes" as an open problem. Scoped, time-bound, revocable disclosure and proof of income are largely unbuilt on EVM.

### Cited Findings
- Railgun: wallet derives spending and viewing keys from a recovery phrase; a read-only viewing key "discloses all of that user's private transactions"; PPOI shows a note comes only from shields a list provider accepted (default list uses Chainalysis sanctions API); Ethereum broadcasters require PPOI ([L2BEAT: Railgun](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/railgun)). EthSystems: "no scoped/threshold key system is natively supported" ([EthSystems: Railgun](https://ethsystems.org/vendors/railgun/)).
- EthSystems shielded pool PoC: viewing keys give read-only history access shareable with "auditors or regulators"; KYC-gated deposits by Merkle-membership proof of an attestation; revocation blocks new deposits but does not freeze funds; no viewing-key revocation ([EthSystems writeup](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)).
- EthSystems' Selective Disclosure pattern: mandate-based disclosure (a regulator's scoped request is checked by a policy engine, answered with a time-limited viewing key from threshold shares or a predicate ZK proof, every grant logged and anchored, e.g. via EAS) and "standing register" disclosure (each value-moving operation encrypts position data to a register key, and the circuit constrains the ciphertext). "A disclosed key cannot be withdrawn." Example: a 24-hour key covering one day's trades. Post-quantum risk rated high ("harvest-now, decrypt-later"); suggests ML-KEM (FIPS 203) ([EthSystems: pattern-regulatory-disclosure-keys-proofs](https://ethsystems.org/patterns/pattern-regulatory-disclosure-keys-proofs/)).
- EthSystems recommended disclosure stack for payments: "user-controlled viewing keys plus regulator viewing keys with time-bound, threshold-controlled scope"; open problem 4: "No common selective-disclosure format across MiCA, GENIUS, and national regimes"; open problem 1: freeze/denylist integration inside shielded pools ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)).
- ERC-7984: holders can designate observers ("auditors or regulators") who can decrypt balance and transfer amounts; optional KYC/AML, freezing and RWA modules ([Zama: ERC-7984 explained](https://www.zama.org/post/erc-7984-the-confidential-token-standard-explained)); the OpenZeppelin library includes extensions for observer access, freezing and transfer restrictions ([OpenZeppelin confidential docs 0.2](https://old-docs.openzeppelin.com/confidential-contracts/0.2/api/token)). Zama's payroll page describes ACL role-by-role access for auditors, tax authorities, fund administrators and AML providers ([Zama payroll](https://www.zama.org/solutions/confidential-onchain-payroll)).
- eERC: "a single, rotatable auditor key can decrypt all amounts and balances" ([Avalanche docs: eERC](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/encrypted-erc)). Solana CT: one auditor ElGamal key per mint; historical ciphertexts stay bound to the old key ([Solana issuer guide](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/issuer-guide)).
- Privacy Pools: ASP-approved deposit labels; public ragequit for unapproved deposits ([Privacy Pools docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/)); 0xbow offers ASP-as-a-Service to other protocols ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348959/privacy-pools-vitalik-buterin)).
- Starknet's payroll RFP sets the target behaviour: "Each recipient generates a viewing-key-derived income statement; the payer generates the full payroll book", with "Cryptographic proof of completeness; no fabrication, no omission"; auditors get aggregate-spend proofs, governance committees per-recipient splits, tax authority full disclosure "comparable to the W-2/P60/T4 model" ([Starknet RFP](https://strk20.starknet.io/rfp/private-payroll)).
- Aztec zkCompliance grant (2026-04-16): per-payment ComplianceNote encrypted to a jurisdiction oracle; planned ComplianceOracle with M-of-N approval, time-limited (e.g. 30-day) access windows and on-chain logging of decryption requests; public aggregate totals as "ZK Proof-of-Reserves"; status: local sandbox only, no token transfers yet ([Aztec forum](https://forum.aztec.network/t/grant-application-zkcompliance-protocol-private-payroll-compliance-on-aztec/8545)).
- zkTLS for income proofs: Reclaim uses proxy witnesses (weaker than full MPC); TLSNotary uses MPC-TLS with garbled circuits; zkPass a three-party handshake with MPC nodes; DECO targets oracles ([BlockEden, 2026-03-19](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/03/19/zktls-zero-knowledge-transport-layer-security-privacy-preserving-identity-web3/)). Reclaim's approach lets people share HTTPS facts "such as income" ([Stanford seminar](https://crypto.stanford.edu/seclab/sem-24-25/karamchati.html)). Cr3dentials does income verification with zkTLS and zkEmail ([HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@Metachaser/ry-UfIFu1g)). zkEmail proves selected fields of a DKIM-signed email ([Burnt docs](https://docs.burnt.com/xion/xions-core/concepts/verification-infrastructure/dkim-module-zkemail)).
- TLSNotary and zkEmail are PSE projects named in the EF privacy announcement ([EF blog, 2025-10-08](https://blog.ethereum.org/2025/10/08/privacy-commitment)).

### Inferences
- Four disclosure primitives a payroll system needs, from easiest to hardest: (1) full viewing key per entity (exists everywhere); (2) per-period derived viewing keys (e.g. HKDF(master_vk, "2026-Q3")) bound into each note's encryption, so an accountant gets one quarter only; (3) auditor ciphertext constrained in-circuit (the EthSystems "standing register" idea), so the employer cannot omit a payment from the auditor's view; (4) predicate proofs (aggregate payroll total, "every recipient is in the KYC registry", "employee income >= X over N months").
- Proof of income from inside the system is stronger than zkTLS: if salary notes are on-chain commitments carrying an employer-signed tag, the employee can prove "sum of notes from employer E in months M1..M6 >= X" directly against the Merkle root, with no TLS oracle. zkTLS/zkEmail remain useful for bridging off-chain payroll (Deel/Gusto payslips) into on-chain proofs.
- A minimal "viewing key standard" for EVM payroll (a key-derivation path plus an event schema for encrypted notes plus an EAS schema for disclosure grants) is an open, hackathon-sized standards gap.

### Gaps
- I found no ERC or EIP draft that standardizes viewing keys or disclosure grants across shielded pools.
- I could not find Railgun's own documentation on viewing-key scoping (the docs URL tried returned 404).
- No production zk proof-of-income product on EVM was found; examples are hackathon demos.

## 7. What is the most credible weekend architecture for private payroll on a generic EVM chain?

### Takeaway
On a chain like Whitechain, where no FHE or TEE provider runs, the credible weekend build is a self-deployed ZK shielded payroll pool (fork Privacy Pools core, Zeto, or the EthSystems Noir PoC) with a new 1-to-N batch payout circuit that publishes only the run total, notes encrypted to employees, an auditor ciphertext constrained in-circuit under a per-period key, ERC-5564 stealth addresses plus a paymaster for unlinkable employee withdrawals, and an EAS-style disclosure log. A cheaper fallback is eERC (amounts hidden, addresses public) combined with stealth addresses.

### Cited Findings
- Building blocks that exist and run on plain EVM: Noir shielded pool with KYC attestation tree and viewing keys ([EthSystems writeup](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)); Zeto circom UTXO tokens with 10-in/10-out batch variants, Apache-2.0 ([GitHub: zeto](https://github.com/LFDT-Paladin/zeto)); Privacy Pools core with ASP ([Privacy Pools docs](https://docs.privacypools.com/)); eERC with auditor key at ~947K gas per transfer ([Avalanche docs: eERC](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/encrypted-erc)); ERC-5564 announcer and ERC-6538 registry at deterministic CREATE2 addresses ([ERC-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564); [ERC-6538](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-6538)).
- Missing pieces EthSystems itself lists: batching, viewing-key revocation, gas paymaster, relayers "specified but not implemented" ([EthSystems writeup](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)).
- The standing-register disclosure variant: "The prover's circuit constrains the ciphertext to match the committed values" ([EthSystems pattern](https://ethsystems.org/patterns/pattern-regulatory-disclosure-keys-proofs/)).
- Starknet RFP's suggested shape: a Payroll helper managing (payer, recipient) channels, "batched disbursement in a single transaction (the example is 50 recipients)", session keys scoped to a cycle budget, paymaster-sponsored recipient discovery ([Starknet RFP](https://strk20.starknet.io/rfp/private-payroll)).
- Railgun via Kohaku already does 4337 relaying for private transactions ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/blockchains/ethereum-foundation-kohaku-sdk-privacy-wallet-integration-bb4t52)); Unlink relays withdrawals and sponsors `execute()` as ERC-4337 UserOps ([Avalanche docs: Unlink](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/unlink)).
- cPay showed ISO 20022 pain.001 ingestion for ERC-7984 payroll ([Zama Developer Hub](https://zama.org/developer-hub)); EthSystems recommends ISO 20022 interpreters ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)).

### Inferences
Recommended architecture (my design, for ~36h with 3-4 people):
1. **Contracts (Solidity, Foundry)**: `PayrollPool` (commitment tree via LeanIMT, nullifier set, ERC-20 custody of a mock USDC), `EmployeeRegistry` (Merkle root of employee receive pubkeys; employee shielded keys registered ERC-6538-style with `registerKeysOnBehalf`), verifiers generated from circuits, `DisclosureLog` (EAS schema or a simple event log of disclosure grants).
2. **Circuits (Noir or circom)**: `deposit` (employer shields treasury); `payrun` (1 employer note in, N employee notes plus 1 change note out, public output = run total and period id, each output's owner in the EmployeeRegistry root, each output also encrypted to `auditor_pk_period` with the ciphertext constrained in-circuit); `withdraw` (employee unshields to a fresh ERC-5564 stealth address); `income_proof` (employee proves sum of their notes tagged with employer E and periods P1..Pk >= X, public output = X, E, period range, nullifier-free since it only proves membership).
3. **Keys**: master viewing key per employer and per employee; per-period subkeys derived by HKDF, so the accountant receives only "2026-Q3"; auditor key per fiscal year, rotated like Solana CT.
4. **Gas privacy**: employee withdrawals relayed by a simple ERC-4337 paymaster or relayer that takes a fee out of the withdrawn amount, so the stealth address never needs funding.
5. **Private reads**: a self-hosted indexer (or a client that scans events locally from a full node) instead of a third-party API, since note discovery through a third-party RPC leaks the spent-note index.
6. **Dashboard**: employer CSV / pain.001 import producing a payrun proof; employee app to scan notes, export a viewing-key-derived income statement, and generate an income proof for a "landlord" verifier contract.
- Batch size: N fixed at 8 or 16 to keep proving within seconds in browser; larger runs split into multiple proofs.
- Fallback path if circuits stall: eERC Converter on Whitechain testnet (amounts hidden, auditor key), plus ERC-5564 stealth addresses for each payroll run (graph partly hidden), plus a Solidity batch-pay wrapper. Note: eERC requires recipients to register keys with its Registrar, which I expect conflicts with fresh one-time stealth addresses; verify before choosing it.
- Honest caveats to state in the demo: tiny anonymity set on a new chain; employer deposits and employee withdrawals reveal amounts unless amounts are rounded or split; payroll timing is a fingerprint; trusted setup required if Groth16 (use a published ptau plus a demo-only phase 2).

### Gaps
- I found no benchmark for a 1-to-N payout circuit in Noir or circom; proving times for N=8/16 are estimates, not measured.
- Whether Whitechain's testnet gas limit and precompiles accommodate ~2.6M-gas UltraHonk verifications is unverified (OP Stack chains normally allow this, but confirm).

## 8. Which concrete hackathon prototypes could a team build in ~36 hours, how hard are they, and which gaps do they fill?

### Takeaway
The strongest, least-crowded ideas are (1) a batch-payout shielded payroll pool with an in-circuit auditor ciphertext and per-period viewing keys, (2) employee proof-of-income from on-chain salary notes, and (3) a stealth-address payroll with paymaster-sponsored claims. Each fills a gap EthSystems or the Starknet RFP names explicitly and runs on any EVM chain, including Whitechain.

### Cited Findings
- Gaps documented by sources: no batching in the EthSystems pool; no viewing-key revocation; no paymaster ([EthSystems writeup](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)); no common selective-disclosure format; freeze/denylist inside shielded pools unsolved ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)); "no production-grade selective disclosure mechanism" for proving correct payment ([Aztec forum](https://forum.aztec.network/t/grant-application-zkcompliance-protocol-private-payroll-compliance-on-aztec/8545)); income statements, completeness proofs and private termination events are requested but unbuilt ([Starknet RFP](https://strk20.starknet.io/rfp/private-payroll)); stealth-address gas funding leaks privacy ([ERC-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564)); Railgun viewing keys disclose everything ([L2BEAT: Railgun](https://l2beat.com/privacy/projects/railgun)); FHE payroll leaves addresses public ([Zama payroll](https://www.zama.org/solutions/confidential-onchain-payroll)).
