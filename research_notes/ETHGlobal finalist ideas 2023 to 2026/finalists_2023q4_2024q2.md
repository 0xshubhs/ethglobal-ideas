# ETHGlobal finalists, October 2023 to June 2024 (11 events, 101 finalist projects)

Method note (applies to all sections). ETHGlobal's own list of past events (embedded in the showcase page) has 11 hackathons that start between 2023-10-01 and 2024-06-30. For each one I paged through the full showcase listing (`https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=<slug>&page=N`), collected every project card, kept the cards that carry the ETHGlobal organisation's prize logo, and then opened each of those project pages. On every one of the 101 pages the ETHGlobal prize reads "🏆 <Event> Finalist" (for example "🏆 ETHGlobal Istanbul 2023 Finalist", "🏆 LFGHO Finalist"), with sponsor "ETHGlobal". Partner prizes, taglines, descriptions and "How it's made" text below come from those project pages, read on 2026-10-27. Wherever an outside finalist list exists (TechFlow, ChainCatcher, Odaily), it matches the showcase list exactly. The category tags are my own classification, made from each project's description. They are not ETHGlobal labels.

## Which events in this window had ETHGlobal finalists, and who were they?

### Takeaway
All 11 ETHGlobal hackathons in the window had judge-picked finalists, 101 projects in total. In order: ETHOnline 2023 (11), ETHGlobal Istanbul (10), ETHIndia 2023 (13; Devfolio-run but listed on ETHGlobal with ETHGlobal finalist badges), LFGHO (10), Circuit Breaker (7), ETHGlobal London (10), Frameworks (10), Scaling Ethereum 2024 (8), ETHGlobal Sydney (8), HackFS 2024 (7) and StarkHack (7). The larger events picked 10 to 13 finalists; Sydney and the smaller async events picked 7 or 8.

### Inferences
- Finalist counts seem to scale with event size. Events with about 200 or more submissions picked 10 to 13 finalists; Circuit Breaker, HackFS 2024 and StarkHack (109 to 157 submissions) picked 7; Sydney (66) and Scaling Ethereum 2024 (198) picked 8.
- The showcase counts differ from the published figures by one or two projects (London 239 vs 241, Scaling 198 vs 199, Circuit Breaker 109 vs 110, Istanbul 428 vs 427). The likeliest cause is projects that were later hidden or deleted. The finalist sets are not affected, because they match the outside lists.
- Whether ETHIndia 2023 counts as an "ETHGlobal hackathon" is a judgment call. It is in ETHGlobal's event list, and ETHGlobal issued finalist badges for it, but Devfolio ran it. If the report needs only events ETHGlobal ran itself, drop those 13 rows and the total becomes 88.

### Gaps
- The submission and hacker counts mostly come from a third-party POAP mirror (feed.poap.demo.goldsky.com) reproducing ETHGlobal's POAP descriptions, as surfaced in search results. I did not find primary ETHGlobal recap posts with these numbers, except the London event page.
- The method relies on each finalist's showcase card showing the ETHGlobal prize logo. For Circuit Breaker, London, Scaling Ethereum 2024 and HackFS 2024 I found no independent finalist list to cross-check against, so a finalist whose card omitted the logo could in principle be missing. For the other events, the outside lists match exactly.
- I found no ETHGlobal blog post or YouTube "Finalist Demos" description for any of these events, so the order in which finalists presented, and any overall ranking, is unknown. (ETHGlobal finalists are not ranked on the showcase.)

## What do the finalist descriptions say they built, and what did the demo prove?

### Takeaway
Most finalists pair a consumer-legible flow with one hard primitive that can be checked. Examples: a ZK proof verified on-chain (zkVRF, Footy Stars, StarkSwirl, VeriBot, zkDL), a hardware tap that signs (TapTrust, NFC wallet, Buddy-Guard), a gasless or credit-backed transaction (DeCredit Wallet, AdFuel, PACKD), or a new EVM capability that actually runs (EVM+, Cryptopolis in a Cartesi machine, ChainSweep on Stylus). The descriptions state the build. Only a few state a measured demo result, so the "promise" column paraphrases the core claim the page makes.

### Inferences
- The demo moment that recurs most is "a proof (ZK circuit, zkVM or TLSNotary) that something done off-chain was done correctly". ZTF, zkVRF, Footy Stars, ZKvote.cc, zkDL, VeriBot, priv.cast, zkP2M, Zeke, Monadic DNA and StarkSwirl all use it, about 11 of 101. Noir is the most common proving stack among them (zkVRF, Sarma, ZKvote.cc, Graphite, zkDL, VeriBot, priv.cast).
- Physical "tap to sign or pay" with NFC is a repeat finalist pattern at in-person events: TapTrust and Buddy-Guard (Istanbul), NFC wallet (London), Relief Link and AlibudaHabitBuilder (Sydney).
- World ID as a one-human-one-vote or anti-bot gate shows up in at least five finalists: Clade Club, Yield.me, Cache Coherence, Relief Link and 0xPoll.
- EIP-4844 blob projects cluster in the two events right after Dencun. London has μBlob, panDA and BlobScriptions; Scaling Ethereum 2024 has BlobFusion, whose page says "One month ago, Dencun went live". So the judges rewarded projects that used a capability that had only just shipped.
- Winning a finalist slot did not require winning a sponsor track: about one in five finalists won no partner prize.

### Gaps
- Most pages describe what was built, not what the live demo showed. I did not watch the demo videos, so the "promise / demo" column reflects written claims, not observed demos.
- The "How it's made" text did not decode for two pages (Yield.me, AlibudaHabitBuilder), so their tech lists rely on the description and, for AlibudaHabitBuilder, on TechFlow.
- Partner prize names were shortened for readability. The exact prize strings, which include emoji, are on each showcase page.

## Which categories dominate each event?

### Takeaway
Across all 101 finalists, four groups lead: Payments and stablecoins (12), Privacy and ZK (12), Social and consumer (10), then a four-way tie at 9 each: Wallets and account abstraction, Gaming, Security and dev tooling, and Infrastructure and protocol. Each event's mix follows its theme: LFGHO was mostly payments (6 of 10), Frameworks mostly social and consumer (6 of 10), Circuit Breaker mostly privacy and ZK (3 of 7, and all 7 use or measure ZK), London mostly infrastructure because of blobs (3 of 10), StarkHack mostly gaming plus consumer (4 of 7), and HackFS 2024 the only event with AI-agent finalists (2 of 7). ### Inferences
- Counting secondary tags as well, ZK and privacy is the real dominant thread of the period: at least 30 of 101 finalists carry Privacy and ZK as a primary or secondary tag. Wallets and account abstraction is next, appearing as primary or secondary on 21 finalists, many built on ERC-4337 or ERC-6551.
- AI agents barely register as finalists before mid-2024. Only the two HackFS projects running on Galadriel are primarily AI agents; elsewhere AI is a supporting feature (Cosmic Cowboys' NPCs, Footy Stars' zkML, VeriBot, Athoos, FLockChain, Pixel Police). This contrasts with later 2024 and 2025 events, which are outside this window and not covered here.
- Not a single finalist is primarily RWA and tokenization. The nearest are AidDistribute (aid vouchers), WeatherProof (parametric insurance) and Restake//Regen (carbon credits).
- Many tags hinge on judgment between neighbouring categories: wallet or payments for the LFGHO wallets, security tooling or consumer for HoverChain, privacy or identity for Lens & Frens and Pixel Police. Per-event counts could move by one or two under a different reader.

### Gaps
- ETHGlobal does not publish categories for finalists, so there is no official baseline to check this categorisation against.
- I did not count categories across all submissions (for example all 515 ETHOnline projects), so I cannot say whether finalists over- or under-represent a category relative to the whole field.
