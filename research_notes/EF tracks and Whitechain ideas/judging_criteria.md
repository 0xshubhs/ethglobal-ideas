# How projects are judged at ETHGlobal Mumbai (5 to 7 Nov 2026): ETHGlobal finalist process, EF prizes, Whitechain prizes, other partner prizes, and the winning bar

Research date: 2026-10-26. All prize text was pulled from the live pages on that date (raw HTML and the embedded Next.js data, not a summary). Verbatim quotes are in double quotes. Wherever an original quote contained an em dash, en dash or the long separator used in prize titles, it has been replaced with a comma, colon or parentheses (marked "[dash replaced]") to follow the no-dash rule. Emojis in prize titles are dropped. Times are given in IST (UTC+5:30), converted from the UTC timestamps in the event data.

## Q1. ETHGlobal's own finalist judging: criteria, format, number of finalists, submission rules, deadlines, AI rules, Continuity Track, and what makes a finalist

### Takeaway
At Mumbai, every team that opts into "Finalist and Partner Prizes" gives a 7 minute live session in a judging room (4 minute demo, 3 minute Q&A) on Saturday 7 Nov, 09:30 to 12:30 IST, and is scored on five published categories: Technicality, Originality, Practicality, Usability (UI/UX/DX) and WOW Factor. Recent 2026 events then name a top 10 who demo at the closing ceremony. Partner prizes are judged only from the written submission, so the repo, README and video carry all the weight there. Submissions close at 09:00 IST on 7 Nov. AI use is allowed but has to be attributed, spec files must be included, and the video cannot use an AI voiceover.

### Inferences
- The judging-room session is the gate to the top 10. A 4 minute demo leaves no time for a long problem statement. Spend at most about 20 seconds on context (the same limit ETHGlobal sets for the video), then show the live flow and the moment the privacy or CROPS property matters, and use the 3 minute Q&A for architecture and trade-offs. The three listed prep questions (inspiration, tool choices, challenges) are a good structure for the Q&A.
- Partner prizes are judged entirely from submission materials, so the README and video have to stand alone for every partner chosen. Booth visits help only for feedback.
- Practicality ("Could it be used by its target audience today?") and Usability line up closely with EF's "could it ship after the hackathon" and "would someone use it without knowing what CROPS means". One working, polished flow scores on both the ETHGlobal and EF rubrics at once.
- Video length has to satisfy every chosen prize at once. ETHGlobal rejects uploads under 2:00 or over 4:00, EF CROPS allows "3 minutes max", 0G wants "under 3 mins", Whitechain wants 2 to 4 minutes and Hedera 5 minutes or less. A cut between 2:00 and 2:59 satisfies all of them.
- The Main Finalist Judging and Partner Prize Judging windows are the same 09:30 to 12:30 slot. A team that wants feedback at partner booths needs to split up or go before or after its judging-room slot.
- Continuity projects appear able to reach the finalist round. The Lisbon 2026 finalist "ethui agentic wallet" describes an existing open-source toolkit plus "At this hackathon we built MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers for both". ([ethui agentic wallet](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/ethui-agentic-wallet-6ohfx)) However, the Mumbai pages do not say whether Continuity entries are finalist-eligible, and the rules note says historically pre-existing work scores lower.
- Heavy AI generation is a risk, because the Involvement clause can remove finalist eligibility. Committing spec files and an "AI usage" section to the README is cheap insurance.

### Gaps
- No Mumbai finalist prize entry (count, cash, perks) is published yet. The "top 10" figure comes from the four 2026 events above.
- How many judging rooms or panels there are, how scores are aggregated, and whether categories are weighted: none of this is published.
- Whether finalists give a fresh stage demo at the Mumbai closing ceremony, and how long it is, is not stated on the Mumbai pages. The 4 minute limit documented on the page is for the judging-room session.
- No ETHGlobal blog post dedicated to "how to become a finalist" was found. The Medium and Paragraph recaps found only state that finalists were chosen by judges.
- Whether Continuity-track entries can be ETHGlobal finalists at Mumbai is not explicitly stated.

## Q2. Ethereum Foundation prizes at Mumbai: verbatim descriptions, ideas, judging criteria, qualification, and resources

### Takeaway
EF (the Access Layer team) puts up $15,000 across four prizes: Anon-RPC ($5,000), the Frame Transaction Alternative Mempool ($5,000), Best CROPS App ($3,500) and Best CROPS Upgrade to an Existing Project ($1,500, Continuity only). A project can enter all three Classic EF prizes, but EF places it in at most one. The CROPS prizes reward a property that is "structural (works without you)" over one that is "cosmetic (a setting)". They also require a CROPS README section and a demo video of 3 minutes or less that shows the moment the property matters, and they ban third-party trackers. Both CROPS prizes are still marked "(tbc: final review pending)".

### Cited Findings

**EF sponsor block** ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes))
- Total "$15,000". About, verbatim: "The Ethereum Foundation's Access Layer team builds the pieces that keep apps and wallets censorship resistant, open source, private and secure (CROPS) all the way to the user. At Mumbai we're bringing two of them: Anon-RPC, which anonymizes a wallet's or app's RPC reads, and the alternative mempool for EIP-8141 frame transactions, which opens up transaction types the public mempool won't accept. Build with either, or build any app where one CROPS property is the reason it exists."
- Sponsor-level resources: "What CROPS means (EF Mandate)" https://blog.ethereum.org/2026/03/13/ef-mandate; "Anon-RPC" https://ethereum.github.io/anon-rpc/; "Anon-RPC GitHub" https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc; "Documentation" https://docs.altmempool.wtf/; "Alt Mempool Devnet Explorer" https://explorer.altmempool.wtf/; "Alt Mempool Starter Page" https://www.altmempool.wtf/; "EIP-8141" https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141; "ERC-7562" https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7562.

- Description: "Use Anon-RPC as an app that consumes it, or produces anonymity via a new Anon-RPC worker, or both. You could also implement/fork the anon-rpc harness to bring anon-rpc to a new platform or improve support for an existing one."

- For the alt mempool prize, the bar includes an admitted transaction link or code that submits one, plus the ability to explain the trade-offs. That suits teams able to field a protocol-level Q&A.
- "(tbc: final review pending)" means the CROPS criteria could still change before 5 Nov. Re-check the page at kickoff.

### Inferences
- The EF bar is a novel cryptographic or AA mechanism, working end to end on real hardware or a real client, with an honest "mocked parts" section. Third-place EF winners (ZeroSurf, Proov) were still complete apps with a distribution hook (an NPM button, QR capsules).
- Private payroll is a crowded theme: Warden at Cannes 2026 and Manila at NYC 2026 both won with it. To stand out at Mumbai, a privacy payroll or payments project likely needs a distinctive structural CROPS property that those lacked. Examples: no team server at all, all reads via Anon-RPC, a recoverable wallet with nothing from the team, or a frontend served via ENS or IPFS. It also needs to avoid Whitechain's "Payroll ... on their own" exclusion.
- Manila shows the stacking pattern working in practice: four prizes from three partners (Unlink, Dynamic twice, Arc).
- About a third of the 30 recent IRL finalists (VEIL VPN, EVM PORST, maki, Carte Bleue, Lortnoc Tahc, UNSU, Proof of Scan, npmguard, ENShell, Lean Silicon) are security, privacy or self-custody projects. That suggests ETHGlobal's main judges reward CROPS-style projects when the claim is concrete and demonstrable. This count is the researcher's own classification.
- A recurring finalist pattern is a one-sentence verifiable promise plus a live proof of it in the demo ("Attestation proves it's running"; "The private key never leaves the card"). That maps onto both WOW Factor and EF's "structural beats cosmetic".
