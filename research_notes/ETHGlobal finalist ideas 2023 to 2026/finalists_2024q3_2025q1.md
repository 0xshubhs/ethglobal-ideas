# ETHGlobal Finalists, July 2024 to January 2025 (plus boundary events)

Method note (read first): Event dates and slugs come from the ETHGlobal past-events list ([ethglobal.com/events](https://ethglobal.com/events)). For each event I crawled every page of `https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=<slug>`. Showcase cards show a small logo for each prize organization, and ETHGlobal's own organization logo (`cdn.ethglobal.com/organizations/xdat5/...`, alt "ETHGlobal logo") appears only on cards that won an ETHGlobal prize. I then opened each of those project pages. Every one of them lists "ETHGlobal: <Event> Finalist" under "Winner of", so the finalist lists below come straight from ETHGlobal's own pages, not from secondary sources. 9 project pages were read from raw HTML; the other 64 were read with WebFetch, which returns a model-written summary, so prize place labels ("place not stated") and tech lists on those rows carry a small risk of summarisation error. Each project row's source is its showcase URL. Category tags are my own classification (see Inferences), not ETHGlobal's.

Correct showcase slugs (verified): `brussels`, `superhack2024`, `ethonline2024`, `singapore2024`, `sanfrancisco2024` (not `sf2024`), `bangkok`, `agents` (Agentic Ethereum), `starkhack`.

## Which events in this window had ETHGlobal finalists, and who were they?

### Takeaway
Six ETHGlobal hackathons fall fully inside July 2024 to January 2025: Brussels, Superhack 2024, ETHOnline 2024, Singapore 2024, San Francisco 2024 and Bangkok. A seventh, Agentic Ethereum, began on Jan 31, 2025 and ran into February. Together they had 66 ETHGlobal finalists: 10 per in-person event, 8 each at Superhack and ETHOnline, and 10 at Agentic Ethereum. StarkHack, which the brief expected, actually ran June 13-26, 2024, before the window opens; I list its 7 finalists separately as a boundary event.

### Inferences
- Pattern: the in-person events (Brussels, Singapore, SF, Bangkok) and Agentic Ethereum each picked 10 finalists, while Superhack and ETHOnline picked 8 each. On these two online events I have no ETHGlobal statement explaining the smaller number.
- My showcase crawl counts match the stated project counts almost exactly (for example 713 = 713, 417 = 417, 291 = 291). So the showcase listing seems to include nearly every submission, and the ETHGlobal-logo filter is unlikely to have missed a finalist.
- Agentic Ethereum starts on the last day of January 2025, so it counts as "held in" the window only if starting there is enough. The report writer may want to footnote it or move it to the next period's notes.

### Gaps
- Two finalist lists have no outside corroboration, only ETHGlobal's own badges: ETHOnline 2024 and Agentic Ethereum. I found no ETHGlobal blog, X post or press list for either.
- I could not open ETHGlobal's own X posts, YouTube "Finalist Demos" videos or paragraph.com recap posts, so I have no official hacker counts. Hacker and project counts come mostly from POAP descriptions shown in search results, not from pages I read in full.
- Singapore's count rests on two secondary sources ("over 300", 309); I found no official ETHGlobal figure.
- Fetching ETHGlobal project pages directly hit a Cloudflare challenge partway through, so I switched to WebFetch summaries. For those 64 rows, check exact prize place labels against the live pages before quoting them.

## What do the finalist descriptions say they built, and what did the demo prove?

### Takeaway
Most finalist pages describe a working flow but rarely say what the demo proved. The clearest checkable promises are cryptographic or on-chain:
- FHE matching over encrypted data (AdFHE, Eros, fheProxies)
- TLSNotary-verified task completion (Individuum)
- JWT signatures checked inside a contract (AuthWallet 2.5)
- attestation-gated re-encryption (SignCast)
- in-browser ZK proofs (Dark Factory)
- TEE plus ZK attestation (Zubernetes)
- STARK-proof mixer withdrawals (StarkSwirl)
- replaying real Uniswap v3 history into a v4 pool (UniV4Backtester)

Each project's showcase URL in the tables is its source. "Demo" quotes or paraphrases the page; "not stated" means the page gives no demo claim.

## Which categories dominate each event?

### Takeaway
Across the 66 in-window finalists (including Agentic Ethereum), AI agents lead (13), almost all of them from Agentic Ethereum. ### Inferences
- Event by event (these are my tags, so this is inference):
  - Bangkok leaned toward gaming and Telegram-native consumer apps.
  - San Francisco leaned toward IP and creator rights (three Story Protocol IP projects) and AI agents.
  - Singapore and Brussels leaned toward payments, privacy and consumer onboarding.
  - ETHOnline leaned toward privacy (FHE, re-encryption) and developer or security tools.
  - Superhack, built around the Superchain, leaned toward Base and Farcaster consumer apps and payments.
- AI rose steadily over the window: 1 AI-primary finalist at Brussels and 0 at Superhack, ETHOnline and Singapore, then 2 at SF, 1 at Bangkok (plus DAOGenie, an AI-executed DAO, as secondary), and 9 of 10 at Agentic Ethereum. Several non-AI finalists also use LLMs as a secondary feature (Cook some hooks, Piggy Wallet, Omma Cash, Memora, OmiSwap, Betsy, IP Infinity, DAOGenie).
- Sponsor tracks visibly shape the finalist mix:
  - Fhenix FHE finalists at Brussels, ETHOnline and Singapore.
  - Story IP finalists at SF.
  - Coinbase AgentKit finalists at Agentic Ethereum.
  - Sign Protocol attestation finalists at ETHOnline.
- Identity and reputation is rarely the primary pitch, but it is a common secondary component, mostly World ID gating, ENS or Sign Protocol attestations.
