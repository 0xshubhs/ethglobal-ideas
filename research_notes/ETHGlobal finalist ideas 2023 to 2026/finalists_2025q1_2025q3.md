# ETHGlobal finalists, February 2025 to August 2025 (Agentic Ethereum, Trifecta, Taipei, Prague, Cannes, Unite DeFi, New York 2025)

## Which events in this window had ETHGlobal finalists, and who were they?

### Takeaway
Seven ETHGlobal hackathons fall in the window, and all seven named ETHGlobal finalists: Agentic Ethereum (online), Trifecta (online, three tracks), Taipei, Prague, Cannes, Unite DeFi (online) and New York 2025. Taken from the finalist prize objects on ETHGlobal's own project pages, the list is: 10 each at Agentic Ethereum, Prague, Cannes and New York; 8 at Taipei; 7 at Unite DeFi; and at Trifecta, 15 track-ranked projects (9 "Track Winner" 1st to 3rd places plus 6 "Track Finalist" badges). That makes 70 projects, listed below.

### Cited Findings

**Method (how the list was built, so the report writer can judge completeness)**
- ETHGlobal's events list has exactly seven events of type "hackathon" whose dates fall in or overlap February to August 2025. They are agents (Agentic Ethereum, virtual, 2025-01-31 to 2025-02-14), trifecta (virtual, 2025-03-20 to 2025-03-26), taipei (physical, 2025-04-04 to 04-06), prague (physical, 2025-05-30 to 06-01), cannes (physical, 2025-07-04 to 07-06), unite (Unite Defi, virtual, 2025-07-25 to 2025-08-08) and newyork2025 (physical, 2025-08-15 to 08-17). Everything else in the window is a Pragma summit, happy hour, cowork or "10 Years of Ethereum" meetup, not a hackathon. The next hackathons are ETHGlobal New Delhi (2025-09-26) and ETHOnline 2025 (2025-10-10), both outside the window. [ETHGlobal events](https://ethglobal.com/events)
- Trifecta has three child events: trifecta-agents ("ETHGlobal Trifecta - Agents"), trifecta-tee ("Trusted Execution Environments") and trifecta-zk ("Zero Knowledge"), each dated 2025-03-20 to 2025-03-28. Its tagline is "ETHGlobal's First Competition". [ETHGlobal Trifecta](https://ethglobal.com/events/trifecta)
- I paged through every showcase listing page for each event slug (https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=SLUG&page=N). I flagged each project card that shows the ETHGlobal organization logo (organizations/xdat5) as a prize badge, then opened every flagged project page. The page JSON holds a prize object of type "finalist" (for example "ETHGlobal Cannes 2025 Finalist", sponsor ETHGlobal). For Agentic Ethereum, Prague, Cannes and New York, this gave exactly 10 finalists each, matching the "For the top 10 teams" line on each prize page. [Showcase example, Cannes](https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=cannes); [Hashlocked page with finalist badge](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/hashlocked-jwaq6)
- The Cannes list from this method matches the 10 finalists named in press coverage: BeamPay, OnlyDanks, PolyBet, livestakes, Pace Club, POAPrivacy, PrivyCycle, ChromaMind, PrivacyLinks and Hashlocked. [RootData](https://www.rootdata.com/news/309069); [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2190093)
- Finalist perks (Cannes prize page): each finalist team member gets 1000 USDC, a $500 flight reimbursement for hacking at another 2025 hackathon, $10,000 in AWS credits, a finalist hoodie and Pragma conference tickets. [ETHGlobal Cannes prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/cannes/prizes)

### Inferences
- Card-logo detection found exactly 10 finalists at all four events whose prize page says "top 10", and the Cannes result matches the press list. So the 8 for Taipei and 7 for Unite DeFi are most likely the real counts, not gaps in my method. ETHGlobal names up to 10 finalists; it does not always fill every slot.
- Trifecta did not run like a normal finalist round. ETHGlobal paid ranked prizes in each of three tracks (3 winners each) and gave "Track Finalist" badges to 2 more per track. If a report treats Trifecta finalists as one group, the 15 ranked projects are the fairest equivalent, and the 6 "Track Finalist" badges are the narrow reading.
- Partner-prize overlap was heavy. Most finalists also won at least one sponsor prize, and the sponsor that ran the event shows up in almost every row. ## What do the finalist descriptions say they built, and what did the demo prove?

### Takeaway
Most finalists pitch one clear end-to-end moment backed by a cryptographic or on-chain guarantee. Examples: a TEE attestation posted on-chain (Pass-Tee-Port, TeeTee), a ZK or email proof of an off-chain fact (Karma Proof, Wrld Map, 0xCollateral, zkDNSSEC, zkSampler), an atomic swap that can only fully settle or fully refund (Hashlocked, Enju), or a single transaction replacing many (BeamPay, Swap Pay, 1inchTeleport, DynaVest). These are the teams' own claims; ETHGlobal does not check them.

### Cited Findings
- **Proof of off-chain facts was the most common trust pattern.** vlayer email or web proofs turn emails and card pre-authorizations into on-chain facts at Prague: [Karma Proof](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/karma-proof-f1gdv) (donation emails), [Wrld Map](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/wrld-map-v64h2) (travel tickets), [0xCollateral](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/0xcollateral-wc4iq) (card pre-authorization as loan collateral) and [Decycle](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/decycle-ke8bj). [Pass-Tee-Port](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/pass-tee-port-2qoc1) does the same for passports using a TEE whose attestation quote is posted on-chain.
- **ZK proofs of computation.** [zkSampler](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zksampler-z5aeb) proves that audio transforms stayed within allowed edits using SP1. [zkDNSSEC](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zkdnssec-6sd9h) proves DNSSEC validity with Groth16 or PLONK proofs that EVM chains can verify. [zkFusion](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zkfusion-u2d0g) proves that auction winners were the best bids (Circom and Groth16). [ZKFOCIL](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zkfocil-private-ils-0oos6) benchmarks private inclusion lists with the Brevis Pico and SP1 zkVMs.
- **Atomic or all-or-nothing settlement.** [Hashlocked](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/hashlocked-jwaq6) pairs 1inch escrows with native Bitcoin HTLCs and claims "about $0.30 in gas fees instead of $300+". [Enju](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/enju-y7gdh) uses one SHA-256 hash lock across Ethereum, NEAR and TRON.
- **One transaction instead of many.** [BeamPay](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/beampay-of4bx) (EIP-7702 with payment metadata), [Swap Pay](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/swap-pay-4hpe9) (EIP-5792 checkout from mixed tokens), [1inchTeleport](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/1inchteleport-huiuk) (Aave to Compound migration, normally 5 transactions) and [DynaVest](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/dynavest-8noha) (Multicall3).
- **Live on-chain artifacts cited as proof.** [Yetris](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/yetris-czm88) gives the address of its Crown NFT contract on Polygon. [Streme.fun](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/streme-fun-4dppy) says its agent deploys tokens to Base mainnet. [Shaman](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/shaman-hyeav) says its contracts are deployed and "tested end-to-end" on Arbitrum testnet.
- **Physical-world demos.** [Decycle](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/decycle-ke8bj) is a Jetson Nano trash can that sorts waste. [ChromaMind](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/chromamind-4cx83) is an ESP32 device in an enclosure 3D-printed at the hackathon. [Rivals](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/rivals-cqy9r) is AR zombies pathing around real furniture. [Hardhat3-Ledger](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/hardhat3-ledger-198y7) shows signing on a Ledger Flex. [cellfi](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/cellfi-ctrmp) runs USDC over SMS through an Android gateway phone. [livestakes](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/livestakes-sn1wb) took pitch videos from hackers who visited its booth at Cannes.
- **Economic mechanism claims.** [Detox-Hook](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/detox-hook-mppeg) captures 70% of arbitrage profit and donates 80% of that to LPs. [Kyma Pay](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/kyma-pay-yn65a) charges 0.15% versus Stripe. [livestakes](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/livestakes-sn1wb) splits its pool 80/20 between bettors and hackers. [Streme.fun](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/streme-fun-4dppy) gives launchers 40% of trading fees.

### Inferences
- A finalist demo usually shows one verifiable moment: a proof checked, an attestation posted, a swap that cannot half-complete, or a one-click flow. Few finalists pitch broad platforms without such a moment. Exceptions include Synapze and conduct.chat, both agent orchestration platforms.
- About a dozen of the 70 rows are mainly consumer or "fun" apps with a light crypto core (Smol Universe, Pomodoki, Pace Club, livestakes, Rivals, DungeonHeim, Pumpkin Spice Latte, Primer, ChromaMind, among others). This suggests ETHGlobal judges reward a good live experience as well as technical depth.

### Gaps
- Showcase descriptions are self-reported. I did not watch demo videos, check deployed contracts or test any claim (for example Hashlocked's gas figure, YieldSeeker's backtests or Kyma Pay's fee comparison).

### Takeaway
AI agents dominate the online Q1 events built around them: all 10 Agentic Ethereum finalists are agent projects (counting tags in my classification), and agents make up the Trifecta Agents track. After that, agents mostly become a supporting feature. Finalist themes move to payments, DeFi and identity at Taipei and Prague, to privacy at Cannes, to 1inch-based DeFi at Unite, and to stablecoin payments at New York, where x402 agent payments become a theme.

### Inferences
- **How AI agents changed through 2025.** In February they were the product: agent hosting, solver agents, agent token launchers, agent games. In March they were judged next to TEE and ZK and started borrowing those trust tools (agents inside TEEs, privacy LLMs). From April to August only 1 to 3 finalists per in-person event were agent-first (Ask CaiShen, DynaVest, EthereumFighter at Taipei; conduct.chat at Prague; PolyBet partly at Cannes). Unite DeFi had none.
- **Agents shifted from actors to paying customers.** The new pattern by mid-2025 is payment rails built for agents: MCPay.fun (x402 plus MCP) at Prague, and x402-flash and Kyma Pay (x402) at New York. The rising x402 keyword count (0 to 2 to 8) supports this, though the sample is small.
- **Privacy rose at Cannes and in the online DeFi event.** Unite's finalists include a privacy layer, a dark pool and a sealed-bid ZK auction, all on 1inch.
- **Sponsor themes shape the finalist mix.** Agentic Ethereum (agent infrastructure sponsors) and Unite DeFi (1inch) show it most clearly, with every finalist in the sponsor's ecosystem. Prague (Blockscout and vlayer) and New York (Flow pool, PYUSD, CDP) show the same effect more weakly.

### Gaps
- The keyword share is a rough proxy. It reads only card taglines, not full descriptions, and can miss or over-count (for example "AI-powered" as a buzzword).
- The tags are one researcher's judgment, applied without a second reviewer.
- I did not compare these finalists with partner-prize winners who were not finalists, so I cannot say whether judges preferred agent projects more or less than sponsors did.
