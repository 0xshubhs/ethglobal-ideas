# Arkiv: sponsor history dossier for ETHGlobal Mumbai (5 to 7 Nov 2026)

Researched 2026-10-31. This dossier covers who Arkiv is, its hackathon record, what its winners did differently, how its briefs and rubrics changed, and what that means for the four Mumbai tracks open to a fresh build. Facts carry a URL. Lines starting **Judgement** are my own reading. Verbatim sponsor text is in double quotes; where a source contained an em dash it is shown as "[dash replaced]".

## How this was researched

- **ETHGlobal prize pages.** I fetched the raw prize page HTML (`https://ethglobal.com/events/<slug>/prizes`) for 39 ETHGlobal hackathons, one request at a time with a 3 second pause, and searched each for "arkiv", "golem" and Swarm. No page showed a bot check. Events checked: ETHOnline 2023, Istanbul, ETHIndia 2023, Circuit Breaker, London 2024, Frameworks, Scaling 2023 and 2024, Waterloo 2023, Paris 2023, New York 2023, HackFS 2023 and 2024, LFGrow, Trifecta, Sydney, Brussels, Superhack 2024, ETHOnline 2024, Singapore 2024, San Francisco 2024, Bangkok, Agentic Ethereum (`agents`), Taipei, Prague, Cannes 2025, New York 2025, New Delhi, ETHOnline 2025, Buenos Aires, HackMoney 2026, Cannes 2026, Open Agents, New York 2026, Lisbon 2026, ETHOnline 2026, Tokyo 2026, Unicode. Control: the Mumbai page returned 496 Arkiv matches; the other 38 returned 0 for both "arkiv" and "golem" ([events list](https://ethglobal.com/events); for example [Buenos Aires prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/buenosaires/prizes), [Tokyo 2026 prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/tokyo2026/prizes)).
- **Finalist catalogues.** `research_notes/ETHGlobal finalist ideas 2023 to 2026/` has no Arkiv, Golem Base or Golem DB entry in any of its five files, so no ETHGlobal finalist ever won an Arkiv prize.
- **Arkiv's own hackathons.** Arkiv publishes the rules, rubric, winners and every submission for its own challenges in public GitHub repos ([Arkiv-Network org](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network)). I read the README, rubric and rules of five of them, plus Arkiv's blog, Golem Network project pages, Taikai and Devfolio.
- **Winner analysis.** I fetched the READMEs of 11 submissions (6 winners, 5 non-winners) through the GitHub API and compared how they describe their Arkiv usage.
- **Blocked or missing.** Taikai's ETHWarsaw 2025 results page renders client side, and the fetch returned no winners. The Global Tour Stop winners (due 23 Oct 2026) are not in the rules repo, and its Devfolio project list showed "Projects (0)" when fetched. Both gaps are marked below.

## 0. Bottom line (Judgement)

1. **Mumbai is Arkiv's first ETHGlobal prize.** It has no ETHGlobal winners to copy. It does have about ten of its own challenges and side tracks since September 2025, with public rubrics and winners. The closest precedent for the Mumbai rubric is Arkiv's own "Global Tour Stop" (9 to 18 Oct 2026). Its four 25-point criteria are almost the same as Mumbai's, and the friction report weighs 25% in both.
2. **Arkiv judges score evidence, not polish.** Design and UX weighed 20% of the score in February to May 2026 and 0% from October. A working app is now a gate. The score goes to four written answers: friction, reach, why Arkiv, and how you use it (or data quality).
3. **Winners make one Arkiv primitive carry the product's main rule.** In Susurro, expiry is the access control. In Cortex, unused memories expire and used ones are extended. Agora sets a different lifetime for each of its seven entity types. In NotarIA, `changeOwnership` is the custody handover. The ideathon winners say it outright: "lifetime IS the certificate's legal validity". Entries that only store app data on Arkiv did not place.
4. **What Arkiv wants now is data that others depend on, and healthier nodes.** The Mirror track and the Node tooling track are new at Mumbai. Arkiv seeded the Mirror track with its own Sourcify mirror. Both tracks ask for reach and real use, not a demo app.
5. **For this team,** the best fits are a security dataset mirror (the Mirror track pays $3,000 for 1st) and a query-language fuzzer or slow-query finder (Node tooling, $2,000). Section 8 lists the traps: Sourcify's signature database, SDK 1.0.0 published on 30 Oct, testnet churn, schema-tag spoofing and the rate-limit quota.

## 1. Who Arkiv is (facts)

- **Lineage.** Arkiv is the renamed Golem DB, also called Golem Base, from the Golem Network team. Golem's ETHWarsaw 2025 page is titled "eth-warsaw-hackathon-golemdb-arkiv-track" and calls the event "GolemDB's first major public presence" ([Golem Network](https://www.golem.network/project/eth-warsaw-hackathon-golemdb-arkiv-track)). Gas is paid in GLM, Golem's token ([Rabdi docs](https://docs.arkiv.network/networks/rabdi/)).
- **Pitch on the Mumbai page.** "Arkiv is the Web3 database, an Ethereum-aligned DB-Chain. Your app's data lives in Arkiv entities: each one is owned by a wallet, can be queried with SQL-like filters, and expires when you choose." ([Arkiv Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes/arkiv))
- **Data model.** Each entity has `$key`, `$owner`, `$creator` (never changes), `$createdAt`, `$updatedAt`, `$expiresAt` and `$creationFlags`. An entity holds up to 32 typed attributes (`bool`, `i32`, `u64`, `u256`, `dec`, `str` up to 128 bytes, `addr`, `key`, and `bytes` up to 128 KiB). Only indexed attributes can be filtered. One page returns at most 100 rows. The creation flags are `readonly` and `permissionlessExtension`. The writes are `patchEntity`, `extendEntity`, `changeOwnership`, `deleteEntity` and atomic `executeBatch`. `watchEntityEvents` streams create, patch, extend, transfer and delete events, and "Arkiv emits no event when an entity expires." ([introduction](https://docs.arkiv.network/start-here/introduction/), [limits](https://docs.arkiv.network/reference/limits/), [column types](https://docs.arkiv.network/schemas/column-types/), [expiration](https://docs.arkiv.network/writing/expiration/), [watch events](https://docs.arkiv.network/querying/watch-events/); source in [arkiv-starlight-docs](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-starlight-docs))
- **Arkiv is public, and schema tags can be spoofed.** "Anyone can write an entity with your tag. When you need to trust what you read, also filter by owner or creator." ([define a schema](https://docs.arkiv.network/schemas/define/))
- **Query language.** A boolean expression over typed literals, for example `category = str('news') AND score > dec(4)`, with `BETWEEN`, `STARTSWITH`, `EXISTS`, `TYPEOF` and a one-attribute `ORDER BY` ([query language](https://docs.arkiv.network/reference/query-language/)).
- **Node.** The node client is Rust: `arkiv-reth`, built on reth, with crates for the query parser, a Merkle-Patricia committed store and read-only RPC. The README says "smart contracts not supported", and a devnet comes up through Kurtosis ([Arkiv-Network/arkiv](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv)). The node API offers `arkiv_query`, `arkiv_getEntityCount` and the standard `eth_*` methods, including `eth_getProof` "against the block `state_root`" ([api-spec.md](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv/blob/main/docs/api-spec.md)).
- **Testnet churn.** Kaolin was retired 15 May 2026, Braga on 12 Aug 2026, and Tiramisu (chain 7738577, used by the October Global Tour) was replaced by Rabdi (chain 7794095), the network Mumbai requires. Kaolin and Braga ran on Hoodi with a Standard Bridge ([older networks](https://docs.arkiv.network/networks/older-networks/)). The Sourcify demo still reads "Arkiv Sorbet testnet" ([sourcify.arkiv.network](https://sourcify.arkiv.network)).
- **The SDK just changed major version.** `@arkiv-network/sdk` is at 1.0.0, last modified 2026-10-30 (`npm view`, 31 Oct). The Global Tour pinned "0.8.x" ([Global Tour README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/global-tour-stop-pre-devcon)). The 1.0 docs use `defineSchema` and `select().from()` ([define a schema](https://docs.arkiv.network/schemas/define/)).
- **Rate limits.** "Arkiv's public RPC is heavily rate limited". The fix is a free access key with "a monthly quota, measured in cost units", one key per wallet per network ([access keys](https://docs.arkiv.network/start-here/access-keys/)).
- **AI tooling.** There is a Claude Code and Codex plugin (`claude plugin marketplace add Arkiv-Network/ai-plugin`), plus an MCP server and skills ([Arkiv Hub AI tools](https://hub.arkiv.network/ai-tools); [ai-plugin repo](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/ai-plugin)).
- **People.** The Mumbai workshop speaker is "Santiago Trujillo Zuluaga (DevRel, Arkiv)" (`00_current_tracks.md`, from the [event page](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)). Santiago hosted the ideathon's AI & DevTools lane and its open lane; Marcos Miranda hosted DeFi and Shantelle Awomoyi hosted Marketplaces ([ideathon README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ideathon)). The February challenge panel was "Marcos (Product @ Arkiv), Seweryn (Platform @ Arkiv), Veronica (1st prize winner @ Arkiv Track, Sub0 Hackathon BA 2025)" ([rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-web3-database-builders-challenge/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md)). **Judgement:** expect Santiago and Marcos among the Mumbai judges. Both have scored security and DeFi ideas before.
- **ETHGlobal presence before Mumbai.** One meetup: "ETHGlobal Happy Hour New Delhi with ENS, Arkiv, GrowthFi, 0xSpace" on 2026-10-22 ([events list](https://ethglobal.com/events); [event](https://ethglobal.com/events/ethglobal-happy-hour-new-delhi-with-growthfi-studio-zry0g)).

### 3.3 Web3 Database Builders Challenge, February to March 2026

- Brief: "build a Web3-native tool where the data is owned by users, not platforms". The three verticals are Job Board ("replacing LinkedIn, Indeed"), Event Platform ("Luma, Eventbrite") and Knowledge Base ("GitBook, Notion"). It offered "two full weeks" and said: "This wasn't a hackathon sprint where you glue an API together in 48 hours" ([README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-web3-database-builders-challenge)).
- Rubric: Arkiv Integration Depth 40% (entity schema design, query usage, ownership model, entity relationships, expiration dates, advanced features), Functionality 30%, Design & UX 20%, Code Quality & Documentation 10%. The tiebreaker is the Arkiv Integration score. A 5 on expiration means "Thoughtful, differentiated expiration [dash replaced] different entity types have different lifespans reflecting real-world product logic" ([scoring rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-web3-database-builders-challenge/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md)).
- **1st, Agora:** "A decentralized event platform for Web3 communities ... with on-chain RSVPs, waitlist auto-promotion, QR ticket verification, and wallet-native profiles." **2nd, Ocean:** "A decentralized knowledge base where your docs belong to you." There were 19 submissions in total, 10 of them event platforms ([README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-web3-database-builders-challenge)).

### 3.4 Arkiv x ETHNS Builder Challenge, May 2026

- Themes: "AI: An AI agent whose memory lives as Arkiv entities"; "Privacy: Confidential data patterns on a public, tamper-proof layer"; "DePIN: A queryable data layer for sensor / telemetry / device data". The deadline "was extended by 48 hours due to Braga testnet issues" ([README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ethns-builder-challenge)).
- The rubric kept the 40/30/20/10 weights but tied the anchors to the SDK. For example, a 5 on ownership means "End-user `$owner` for write/update/delete control, plus `$creator` used intentionally for tamper-proof attribution where it matters (e.g., DePIN readings filtered by `.createdBy(deviceWallet)`...)". A 5 on advanced features means "batch creates via `mutateEntities`, encrypted-payload patterns with envelope encryption + auto-revoking AccessGrants, ZK-proof attestations anchored on Arkiv" ([scoring rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ethns-builder-challenge/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md)).
- **Cortex:** "An AI that remembers and forgets the way people do [dash replaced] fresh thoughts stay short-lived and fade for free on Arkiv, while the memories you actually use in decisions graduate into lasting rules", with "an MCP server and Claude plugin bundled". **Ark Hive:** "Mint an ERC-8004 agent, then every session, message, scoped memory and hash-chained snapshot is written to Arkiv [dash replaced] signed by your own wallet", and it "Ships `arkiv-agent-sdk` on npm so any app can read another agent's memory" ([README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ethns-builder-challenge)).

### 3.6 Ideathon, August 2026 (ideas only)

- The rubric is out of 100: Track alignment 20, "Arkiv fit [dash replaced] the counterfactual" 20, Data & query design 20, Impact & usefulness 15, Clarity & feasibility 15, Uniqueness 10. A 5 on Arkiv fit means "The counterfactual is the product: the pitch collapses without queryable, time-scoped, tamper-proof entities [dash replaced] and says what deliberately stays off Arkiv". A "tooling lens" makes developer tools "first-class submissions" and scores them on "how much it moves Arkiv's developer experience and developer adoption" ([scoring rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ideathon/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md)).
- Placings ([README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ideathon)):
  - AI & DevTools: 1st "Tried That" ("A shared, self-expiring registry of what does NOT work ... confidence is simply how many reports are still alive"), 2nd "Capability Canary" ("every claim dies unless an independent prober re-proves it"), 3rd "MCP Attest" (expiring scanner attestations to an exact MCP release and tool-schema hash).
  - Other: 1st "Tideland" (a game where "whatever the players stop caring about literally sinks off the map, because the decay is not game code someone can patch, it is the database"), 2nd "Layak" (certificates "whose lifetime IS the certificate's legal validity"), 3rd "GelGit".
  - Marketplaces: 1st "Still Yours" ("A listing that stays alive only while the seller keeps proving they still have the item"), 2nd "Shared Compute Lease Board", 3rd "Strikethrough".
  - DeFi (host Marcos Miranda): 1st "Chorus" ("A witness log, not an oracle ... standing is only as good as their last corroborated report"), 2nd "PARALLAX" (blast radius of a protocol failure), 3rd "Wallet Guardian" (token approvals as shared security intelligence, with "an exploit-and-reputation graph that lives on Arkiv").
  - Unprized but listed: "On the Clock" (a 90-day disclosure embargo as an entity "whose lifetime IS the deadline"), "Half-Life" and "Warranti".
- Security-flavoured ideas that did not place include ThreatWindow, AuditClock, Socrates, Dependency Trust Windows, ArkivSentinel and Vulnproof ([SHOWCASE.md](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv-ideathon/blob/main/SHOWCASE.md)).

### 3.7 Global Tour Stop, October 2026: the template for Mumbai

- Theme: CROPS. Prizes: Best Indian Use Case "Devcon 8 ticket + $2,000 USDC" (needs "at least one member of Indian nationality"), and Censorship Resistance, Open Source, Privacy and Security at "$750 USDC" each. "One prize per team." ([README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/global-tour-stop-pre-devcon))
- Gates: a public deployment; reproducible steps with "the exact query"; MIT repo; `/arkiv/schema.md`; "On-chain evidence on Tiramisu: the wallet addresses that create the project's Arkiv entities"; `/arkiv/friction.md`; a video of 3 minutes or less; "Evidence that survives expiry". Not eligible: "the SDK installed but never queried · a Postgres app with an Arkiv logo · private keys, secrets, or real personal data stored in Arkiv in plaintext or hashed." ([scoring rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/global-tour-stop-pre-devcon/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md))
- Score: four criteria at 25 each, with a minimum of 50/100 to win: "Why this pillar?" (or "Why India?"), "Why Arkiv?", "How you use Arkiv" ("We judge how and why you use a feature, not how many you use. We check usage on chain and in the code; a feature claimed but not found counts against you."), and "Friction report". A 5 on friction means "Each issue has expected vs actual, versions and steps to reproduce ... A smooth integration documented with the same rigour ... scores the same. Invented complaints score 0". Ties go to "higher 'Why Arkiv?' ... then higher 'How you use Arkiv'" ([scoring rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/global-tour-stop-pre-devcon/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md)).
- Feature table from that rubric, the clearest list of what Arkiv's judges count:

| Feature | "Use that scores" (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| Attributes vs payload | "What you query lives in typed attributes (numbers where you need ranges); the rest in the payload" |
| Queries | "Compound predicates, ranges, `createdBy()` / `ownedBy()`, cursor pagination" |
| Entity Expiration | "Lifetimes that differ per entity type and follow product logic" |
| Lifetime Extension | "`extendEntity` as a lease renewed by activity" |
| Creation flags | "For example, permissionless extension so a community can keep data alive" |
| `$creator` / `$owner` | "Verifiable authorship surfaced in the UX; ownership transfer" |
| WebSocket entity events | "`watchEntityEvents` over a WebSocket transport (no `fromBlock`, which forces polling); the UI reacts without a refresh loop" |
| Batch writes | "Atomic batches when several writes must land together" |

- The Security pillar asks that "the app shows or filters Arkiv entities by their on-chain creator (`$creator`), not by a field anyone can write" ([scoring rubric](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/global-tour-stop-pre-devcon/blob/main/docs/scoring-rubric.md)).

### 4.2 What Arkiv's judges reward (Judgement, drawn from 4.1 and the rubrics)

1. **A primitive that carries the rule.** Expiry, extension, `$creator` or ownership transfer should be the reason the product works, not a field it happens to have. The ideathon's anchor says this directly: "The counterfactual is the product".
2. **Lifetimes that differ by entity type and follow product logic.** This is on every rubric from February to October.
3. **Trust by `$creator`, never by a writable field.** It became an explicit scoring line in May and October. It also matters for security: anyone can forge a schema tag.
4. **Evidence a judge can click.** Wallet addresses, a transaction, a runnable query. Since October this is a gate, and "a feature claimed but not found counts against you."
5. **Reusable output.** An SDK, MCP server or plugin (Cortex, Ark Hive, Licita Verify) maps directly to Mumbai's 25% Reach criterion.
6. **Rigorous friction notes.** This has been 25% since October. "Invented complaints score 0", and a smooth integration documented with the same rigour can still score 5.
7. **Not rewarded:** polish alone (Design & UX is now 0%), counting how many features are used, template verticals, and personal data on chain.

**What the change signals (Judgement).**
- **From apps to infrastructure.** In 2026 Arkiv paid for apps that showed the model. At Mumbai, two of its three fresh-build tracks pay for infrastructure: public datasets and operator tools. Arkiv built the reference mirror itself (Sourcify, 7.9M contracts) and excluded Sourcify from entries, which shows it wants a second and third dataset that people actually query.
- **From "does it work" to "will anyone use it".** Reach (25%) replaces Functionality. The Mirror track asks "How many people or projects already use or depend on this data?" It rewards picking a dataset with existing consumers and naming them.
- **From features to counterfactuals.** "Why Arkiv" has been a fixed quarter of the score since August. Mumbai sharpens the question for mirrors: "What can people do with your copy on Arkiv that they can't do with the original source today?"
- **Friction is a product input.** It is 25% in every Mumbai track, and Node tooling asks about "running or querying the node". With SDK 1.0.0 out on 30 Oct and Rabdi new, Arkiv wants issue reports it can reproduce.
- **Security has become respectable.** DeFi was an ideathon track whose 3rd place was a security graph, and "Security" was a CROPS pillar in October. Node tooling asks for a "slow query finder" and handles the findings like vulnerability disclosure: "never into your public repo ... or demo".

## 8. Facts that change idea design at Mumbai

- **A function-signature mirror can count as Sourcify data.** Sourcify runs the 4byte signature database: "Recently Sourcify took over openchain.xyz's 4byte signature APIs as well as the domain". Its dataset includes "Data from openchain's dataset; Data from 4byte.directory; Signatures from verified contracts in Sourcify" ([Sourcify signature database](https://docs.sourcify.dev/docs/repository/signature-database/); [4byte.sourcify.dev repo](https://github.com/sourcifyeth/4byte.sourcify.dev)). Arkiv's Sourcify records also carry "`signatures` from the signature tables" ([processor README](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/sourcify-arkiv-entity-processor)). Mumbai rules out "Sourcify data, from any chain." **Judgement:** "function signatures" is a listed idea, but any source that flows through Sourcify is risky. `ethereum-lists/4bytes` (MIT, last push 2024-12-21) is an independent source, but it is stale.
- **Licences of candidate datasets** (GitHub API, 31 Oct). ERC-7730 clear-signing registry, now at `ethereum/clear-signing-erc7730-registry`: CC0-1.0, 931 descriptor files (776 calldata, 133 EIP-712), 82 commits since 1 Oct ([repo](https://github.com/ethereum/clear-signing-erc7730-registry)). `ethereum-lists/chains`: MIT, 2,853 chain files ([repo](https://github.com/ethereum-lists/chains)). DeFiHackLabs: Apache-2.0, 898 `_exp.sol` PoCs ([repo](https://github.com/SunWeb3Sec/DeFiHackLabs)). Walletbeat: MIT ([repo](https://github.com/walletbeat/walletbeat)). `ethereum/ERCs`: CC0-1.0. `safe-global/safe-deployments`: MIT. MetaMask `eth-phishing-detect`: "DON'T BE A DICK PUBLIC LICENSE" ([LICENSE](https://github.com/MetaMask/eth-phishing-detect)). ScamSniffer `scam-database` and Uniswap `default-token-list`: GPL-3.0. `ethereum-lists/contracts`: no licence. `dawsbot/eth-labels`: MIT for the code, but **Judgement:** its labels are scraped from explorers, so the data licence is doubtful.
- **No contracts on Arkiv.** The node "smart contracts not supported" ([arkiv repo](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv)). The team's Solidity work has to live on Ethereum or a fork, with Arkiv as the data layer.
- **Testnet and SDK risk inside a 36-hour window.** Rabdi is new, SDK 1.0.0 is two days old, and the ETHNS deadline once slipped 48 hours because of testnet trouble (section 1, 3.4). **Judgement:** pin the SDK version on day one and log every problem straight into `friction.md`.
- **Quota.** Mirrors that write tens of thousands of entities hit the monthly access-key quota (section 1). Batch writes, index only the fields you filter on ("An index costs extra on every write"), and set shorter lifetimes ("A shorter lifetime costs less") ([define a schema](https://docs.arkiv.network/schemas/define/); [expiration](https://docs.arkiv.network/writing/expiration/)).
- **Proofs exist.** `eth_getProof` against `state_root` is in the node API ([api-spec.md](https://github.com/Arkiv-Network/arkiv/blob/main/docs/api-spec.md)). **Judgement:** a mirror or node tool that checks entity proofs answers "Why Arkiv" better than prose.
- **Swarm upload logistics.** Swarm needs a postage stamp batch, and the Mumbai rules ask for "The postage stamp batch ID you uploaded with and the wallet that bought it (or who gave you the batch)" ([Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes/arkiv)). The bee-js README shows gateway uploads with a placeholder stamp ([bee-js](https://github.com/ethersphere/bee-js/blob/master/README.md); [Swarm docs](https://docs.ethswarm.org/docs/develop/upload-and-download)). **Judgement:** ask at the booth whether batches are handed out.
