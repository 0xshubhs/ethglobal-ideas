# Build the zero options Ethereum still lacks

At ETHGlobal Mumbai (5 to 7 November 2026), the entries most likely to win take a gap the Ethereum Foundation has named itself and turn it into one structural claim that the demo proves live. Four builds fit that pattern. The first is an app whose reads are anonymized through Anon-RPC and also proof-checked by a light client; no such composition exists yet, and the prize lists it by name. The second is an alternative-mempool build that ships something nobody has released: a stateful ERC-20 paymaster that handles the documented edge cases, or the missing viem SDK and admission linter. The third is a CROPS tool that measures what a dapp leaks, or that proves a served frontend was rebuilt from its public repo. The fourth, for Whitechain, is private business payments: contractor invoicing with stealth receipts, scoped accountant keys and gasless settlement in the prize USDT. Plain private payroll is the wrong pitch there, because Whitechain's Operate track says payroll "on its own" will not place and private payroll already won partner prizes at Cannes and New York 2026. These picks line up with the EF's own ideology. The EF Mandate makes CROPS (censorship resistant, open source, private, secure) non-negotiable and commits the EF to a "zero option", meaning an intermediary-free path for every flow that today runs through an intermediary. All four EF prizes come from the Access Layer team, whose rubric rewards "structural (works without you)" over "cosmetic (a setting)". Many obvious ideas are already built or are thinner than they look: an opt-in Tor toggle, viem `fetchFn` wiring, an EIP-1193 wrapper and a React Native harness all exist. Judging has three layers: ETHGlobal's five unweighted categories in a 7-minute live session; EF's per-prize criteria, applied to submitted materials, with each project placed in at most one EF prize; and Whitechain's "What we want to see" and "Won't place" lists plus six hard qualification items. The hard constraints are 36 hours of hacking, submissions due 09:00 IST on Saturday 7 November, alt-mempool devnets whose parameters are known only on site, and a prize USDT with no documented way to obtain it.

## The Mandate turns CROPS into a non-negotiable build brief

The EF Board published the Mandate on 13 March 2026, called it "part constitution, part manifesto, and part guide", and inscribed it in an Ethereum transaction ([EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/03/13/ef-mandate); [Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x5dd574df963a1df1f064791e0f6ff41ec972cdbba12293b7e1ece582052ba855)). Its central sentence is that Ethereum "must, above all, remain censorship resistant, open source, private, and secure (CROPS)", that "self-sovereign use must be extraction-resistant and experience seamless", and that these properties "must never be traded away for convenience: without them we have nothing" ([EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/03/13/ef-mandate)). The full text calls CROPS "an indivisible whole" and "the sine qua non of all Ethereum's development priorities" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). It defines each letter tightly. **Censorship resistance**: "No actor can selectively exclude valid use or break functionality". **Open source and free**: "No privileged code or hidden specifications", with all work forkable. **Privacy**: "User data is not exposed beyond necessity or against their interests". **Security**: "Things must do what they claim to do, no more and no less" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). The purpose behind all four is self-sovereignty, defined as "a user has the final say over their identities, assets, actions, and agents."

The **walkaway test** is the stated "ultimate goal": the protocol and core application layers should keep working and evolving "even if the Foundation and today's core developers disappeared tomorrow." The Mandate applies the same test to users, who "should not be forced into frequent, complex migrations that create unintended risks" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). The open-source letter includes a licensing pledge: "Permissive licenses are accepted, viral copyleft licenses are appreciated, but merely source-available licenses are not tolerated." Privacy is meant to be the default, with users selectively negotiating disclosures "on top of a base of freely available, unconditional privacy" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). For a hackathon team, the practical effect is that a privacy app with an opt-in privacy mode, or a compliance layer that comes before privacy rather than on top of it, has already conceded the argument.

The Mandate is just as clear about what the EF will not do, and that defines where outside builders fit. The EF practices "subtraction" ("The more Ethereum succeeds, the tinier we become"). It is "not a development company. We do not build consumer apps. If it can be a sustainable business, it belongs in the community", and when it works downstream it focuses on "making CROPS-native affordances competitive and viable for adoption" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). It admits that "most use of Ethereum flows through partially centralized surfaces: wallets, RPC providers, relays to the MEV-industrial complex, app stores, exchanges". Its answer is the **zero option**: "for every affordance that has an intermediated path, any intermediary-free path that is possible must be built and must remain credible and accessible ... We do not skip this step," with block building, RPC servers and identity attesters as the named examples ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). A hackathon team building "the zero option for X" is doing exactly the community work the Mandate delegates.

Three of the Mandate's worked "quandaries" read like judging notes. The wallet-safety quandary condemns a default-on "safe mode" that silently blocks contracts, or an AI copilot built on "an uninspectable proprietary model" that "reports user actions back home silently". The alternative it endorses is "a choice of independent locally-verifiable filters with transparent rules" and "private-by-default tool use including any AI components" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). The identity quandary rejects treating a government, biometric or "corpoid" ID wrapped in a ZK proof as automatically CROPS. It suggests a ZK proof of ETH ownership or a ZK deposit when only sybil resistance is needed, and prefers multiple independent attestation sources. That matters in Mumbai, where an Anon Aadhaar guardian is tempting but should be one signal among several. The quandary on judging teams warns against designs that cover only "the 'happy case'", not the case where "third-party dependencies (whether APIs, content delivery networks, or otherwise) disappear or break, or worse, where the team itself disappears". Its red flags are "closed components, whitelists, soft-default routing, discretionary upgrade ability, or dependency-heavy integrations", and it reassures that "We do not require first versions to be complete; they only need to stay live" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)).

The organization has narrowed around this agenda. A June 2026 restructuring cut "54 fewer colleagues, roughly 20% of the EF" and created five clusters. The Access Layer cluster, which runs all four Mumbai EF prizes, defines its job as making "self-sovereignty available, legible and survivable across key actions: reading the chain, transacting, proving, delegating, and exiting". Its stated aim is that transaction outcomes are "either guaranteed or fail cost-free" ([EF structure](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/06/23/ef-structure); [ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). On the protocol side, the target is an L1 that is quantum-resistant across all three layers "by December 2029", with Hegota headlined by FOCIL and EIP-8141 frame transactions ([protocol priorities](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities)). Vitalik Buterin's framing is sharper still. That smart wallets and Railgun-style protocols "have to send transactions through intermediaries to get included onchain is honestly embarrassing". He also rejects the halfway answer of depending on intermediaries but making them easy to switch: "going 50% of the way would not make Ethereum Deeply Impressive in the CROPS way. So we push for 100%" ([Vitalik on X](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2058583593102844111)). His New Year post set the user-facing bar: apps where "you don't even notice if Cloudflare goes down" ([Vitalik on X](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2006737662942871574)).

## The EF's own gap list concentrates at the user edge

The EF has stopped publishing grant wishlists. As of 26 October, the Ecosystem Support Program lists "no active wishlists", "no active Request for Proposals" and "no active grant rounds" ([ESP wishlist](https://esp.ethereum.foundation/applicants/wishlist); [ESP RFP](https://esp.ethereum.foundation/applicants/rfp)). The most concrete "please build this" material now sits in Access Layer documents, and those documents start with reads. The Reads team says "on the order of a hundred operators serve Ethereum state ... Meanwhile, roughly ten thousand home nodes are running, but almost none serve state to anyone else". It calls "origin privacy and correctness proofs" "an open problem at RPC latency and cost" ([EF Reads](https://reads.ethereum.foundation/)). Its open-problems tracker lists, among others, "No protocol enumerates serving endpoints", browsers that cannot dial native transports, and home nodes behind NAT that cannot serve ([Reads open problems](https://reads.ethereum.foundation/open-problems)). The EF knowledge-base card for Anon-RPC states the limits directly. It "covers the IP side of W-1 only: the endpoint still reads the query content", "bootstrapping needs a pre-existing RPC connection", and it has "no audits and no production deployments" ([anon-rpc card](https://github.com/ethereum/access-layer-resources/blob/HEAD/tooling/anon-rpc.md)). On incentives, the team says operators "need a reason to serve requests" but that "We do not design the incentive mechanism" ([Reads about](https://reads.ethereum.foundation/about)). Meanwhile, the EF's zkAPI already lists "Blockchain RPC" as a use case for anonymous prepaid credits ([zkAPI](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/01/introducing-zkapi)), so the two artifacts could be combined.

On the sending side, the gap is the gas budget for validating transactions. EIP-8141's public mempool caps validation at **100,000 gas** ([EIP-8141](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141)), yet an ERC-8421 co-author showed that the cheapest fully optimized single-note privacy spend costs **211,828 gas** and concluded that "Nothing fits in 100,000" ([ethresear.ch](https://ethresear.ch/t/eip-8141-and-minimum-required-validation-budget-for-privacy-applications/25889)). The EF also states that "Ethereum is still missing some basic protocol support that privacy applications need" and names keyed nonces (EIP-8250) and recent roots (EIP-8272) ([protocol priorities](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities)). Its October post on transaction assertions (EIP-7906) leaves an explicit wallet-side job undone. "A compromised frontend can write an assertion that permits the attack," so a useful rule "must come from independently approved user intent, a standing account policy or protocol logic". Because the EIP does not force transactions to carry assertions, "the wallet must ensure that every frame transaction it builds includes the required assertion". The post closes with "we want to hear from the wallet and protocol teams who would use them" ([transaction assertions](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions)).

Frontends and signing are the third cluster. The EF's grant to WEBCAT puts the problem plainly: HTTPS "does not prove that the code the site serves matches what its developers published", and today "the current alpha Firefox extension prevents the page from loading" ([WEBCAT grant](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/08/05/1ts-grant)). The Trillion Dollar Security (1TS) dashboard still marks "Verifiable Frontend Hashes" and "Time-Limited Approvals" as Research, and "Hardware Wallet Privacy Support" as Planned. It also records that "Tor compatibility is broken" in private-wallet UX ([1TS dashboard](https://trilliondollarsecurity.org/category/user-experience)). Clear Signing launched in May 2026 but still needs descriptors, accuracy verification and expert attestations ([Clear Signing](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/05/12/clear-signing-announcement)).

The EF's guarantee catalog shows how far wallets and payments still are from CROPS. Address-to-address unlinkability (W-2) has "wallet-native adoption" that "remains minimal". Permissionless alternatives to bundlers and paymasters (W-4) "are still early". Locally verifiable filters (W-5) have "no card yet". Guardian-hiding recovery (W-6) is "early" ([wallet domain](https://github.com/ethereum/access-layer-resources/blob/HEAD/domains/wallet.md)). For payments, "Relayer- and paymaster-based flows need a user-broadcastable equivalent" (P-2). Approvals are "routinely unlimited: a blank check" (P-3). And "A merchant who learns 'you were paid' from a hosted API has re-created the acquiring bank" (P-4) ([payments domain](https://github.com/ethereum/access-layer-resources/blob/HEAD/domains/payments.md)). Outside data matches. Every one of the **24 software wallets** rated by Walletbeat lacks an L1 light client by default ([Walletbeat data](https://github.com/walletbeat/walletbeat/tree/beta/data/software-wallets)). A rescan of Umbra and ERC-5564 payments traced **38.92%** of withdrawn stealth addresses to their owners through withdrawal habits ([ephemeral.money](https://ephemeral.money/docs/research/method)). PSE's 38 team interviews put proving time, verification gas and missing wallet support at the top of builders' complaints, and found that relayer gas sponsorship is usually traceable ([PSE user research](https://github.com/privacy-scaling-explorations/pse.dev/blob/main/content/articles/private-transfers-engineering-user-research.md)). Its user survey found that 86% of users had abandoned a privacy flow at least once, and that passive, default-on tools retain users better than manual ones ([PSE survey](https://github.com/privacy-scaling-explorations/pse.dev/blob/main/content/articles/px-user-survey-2025.md)).

## Judges reward one working, structural claim proven live

### ETHGlobal's finalist gate is a 7-minute live session

Mumbai is a 36-hour, strictly in-person event with teams of up to five. Hacking starts Thursday 5 November at 21:00 IST and submissions close Saturday 7 November at 09:00 IST; "Late submissions won't be accepted" ([ETHGlobal Mumbai details](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details); [ETHGlobal Mumbai](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)). Teams that opt into "Finalist and Partner Prizes" get **7 minutes: 4 minutes for the demo and 3 for Q&A**, between 09:30 and 12:30 IST. They are scored on five unweighted categories, quoted here: "Technicality", "Originality", "Practicality: How complete and functional is your project? **Partner prizes are judged only from submitted materials.** Booth visits are optional and "not required for judging" ([ETHGlobal Mumbai details](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)), so the README and video must make the whole case to EF and Whitechain without the team in the room. A project may select at most three partner prizes, and a partner with several tracks counts once ([ETHGlobal Mumbai](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)). Videos under 2 minutes or over 4 minutes are "automatically rejected", AI voiceovers are banned, and introductions should not run past 20 seconds. Repos with "large single commits or missing histories may be disqualified" ([ETHGlobal Mumbai details](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)). A team on the Continuity track may build on existing code if it documents what came before. However, the Mumbai FAQ still says pre-existing projects are not allowed, which contradicts the details page, and ETHGlobal's rules note that mostly pre-existing work historically scores lower ([ETHGlobal rules](https://ethglobal.com/rules)). Applications close 30 October at 23:59 IST ([ETHGlobal Mumbai](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai)).

## Track by track, much of the obvious work is already done

### Anon-RPC: verification and new platforms are open; basic wiring is done

Anon-RPC is a standard plus a sandbox, not a network. A wallet passes a specifier contract address to the harness, which reads the keccak256 of a worker bundle from that contract, fetches the bundle from any resolver, and runs it only if the hash matches. The worker runs in a Web Worker inside a null-origin iframe ([anon-rpc](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc)). One common reading is wrong: **workers get no `fetch` capability**. The standard gives them only `signalReady`, `signalFailed`, `acceptCall`, `config`, `kps`, `storage` and `log`. Workers may use ambient browser APIs (the passthrough worker uses `fetch`, Nym uses WebSocket), while tor-js and Nox use only KPS ([SPEC](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/blob/main/SPEC.md)). Versions are mid-transition. Spec 0.4.0 (23 October) added the per-call `anonOptions.isolationKey`, but npm still serves harness 0.3.3 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@anon-rpc/browser-harness)). No merged worker honours the key yet; the tor-js implementation is an open PR ([tor-js PR #20](https://github.com/ethereum/tor-js/pull/20)). A demo should not depend on per-account circuits.

Latency is now measured, and every design has to plan around it. The official weekly bench on 26 October recorded these numbers ([bench run](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/actions/runs/40303047893)):

| Arm | Boot | p50 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | none | 22 ms | 131 ms |
| tor-js | 8.1 s | 810 ms | 1.8 s |
| Nym | 10.7 s | 764 ms | 6.1 s |
| Nox | did not boot (specifier on Sepolia) | | |

The Anon wallet team saw "~1.5 s either way, against ~0.1 s direct" for wallet-shaped calls ([issue #10](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/issues/10)). Bundles are large: 6.8 MB for tor-js and 18.3 MB for Nym. All live specifiers are owned by single EOAs, and none has been renounced. The demo tor-js gateway "may disappear at any time" ([adopters.json5](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/blob/main/adopters.json5)). The harness also has no boot timeout, which is why adopters add 90 to 120 seconds of their own ([Kiwi News transport](https://github.com/attestate/kiwistand/blob/HEAD/src/web/src/anon-transport.mjs)). Any app that makes many calls therefore needs multicall batching, caching and a gateway the team runs itself, with Nym as a no-config fallback.

The plain integration work is taken. Privacy Pools already wraps viem's `http({ fetchFn })` behind an egress allowlist that fails closed and pins the expected worker hash. Its code also records an incident: "108 per-nullifier `eth_call`s issued by SDK internals during a single send" leaked outside the anonymizer ([Privacy Pools egress transport](https://github.com/0xbow-io/privacy-pools-website-v2/blob/HEAD/services/egress-transport.ts)). Kiwi News has a failover requester. Kohaku ships an EIP-1193 provider, `@kohaku-eth/provider/anon` ([Kohaku anon provider](https://github.com/ethereum/kohaku/blob/HEAD/packages/provider/src/anon/index.ts)). 0xbow vendors a React Native package whose source is private and whose native sandbox "has no `WebAssembly`, so the tor-js worker does not run here" ([0xbow vendor dir](https://github.com/0xbow-io/privacy-pools-website-v2/tree/HEAD/vendor)). A standalone viem transport package or an opt-in Tor switch would add little.

Three openings remain clearly unclaimed. **Verified anonymous reads** is first: the prize names "combine anon-rpc with a light client or proof verification", and no composition was found. Kohaku ships its anon, Helios and Colibri providers as mutually exclusive options. Colibri's config takes a custom `fetch` documented "to route traffic through Tor" ([Colibri on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@corpus-core/colibri-stateless)), and Lodestar's prover wraps any EIP-1193 provider ([Lodestar prover](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@lodestar/prover)). Helios, by contrast, has no fetch hook ([Helios on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@a16z/helios)). The same build can close the one trust gap the wallet guide admits, where the bootstrap provider "is trusted for one read" ([wallet guide](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/blob/main/docs/integrate-wallet.md)): verify the specifier's `workerHash` slot with `eth_getProof` against a light-client header. **A non-browser harness** is second. The Node prototype is "Not yet a conforming harness" and its sandbox lacks WebAssembly ([node-harness README](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/blob/main/impl/node-harness/README.md)), so every real anonymizing worker, all of which are WASM, is browser-only. A Deno or Electron harness that exposes a local anonymizing JSON-RPC endpoint would, for the first time, let MetaMask custom networks, Foundry and cast use Anon-RPC. That removes the familiar objection that it is "a library, not an endpoint". **A new worker** is third. OHTTP looks feasible in a weekend with `ohttp-ts` ([ohttp-ts](https://www.npmjs.com/package/ohttp-ts)), provided the team discloses that running both relay and gateway itself removes the non-collusion property. The EF-adjacent threat map's caveat applies to every network, and is a reason to state limits honestly: all of them hide the IP, "and none hides what the wallet asks (that needs PIR)" ([threat map](https://github.com/andyguzmaneth/anon-rpc-threat-map)).

### Alternative mempool: tooling and stateful paymasters are open; target the devnet

EIP-8141 is a Draft but is Scheduled for Inclusion in Hegota ([EIP-8081](https://github.com/ethereum/EIPs/blob/master/EIPS/eip-8081.md)). Its current text defines a type 0x06 transaction with up to 64 frames, three modes (DEFAULT, VERIFY, SENDER), an APPROVE opcode and five introspection opcodes. The public mempool accepts only four prefix shapes, at most 100,000 gas of validation, SLOAD only on the sender's storage, and one pending transaction per sender ([EIP-8141](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141)). ERC-8421, still an unmerged draft, adds what the public pool refuses. It sets per-entity budgets of 100k unstaked and 1M staked, adds a state-writing `pre_verify` frame, allows reads of storage "associated" with the sender, and applies ERC-7562-style reputation, but stake is never slashed ([ERC-8421 draft](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/493ba8ac3be97dc5cf6335eb97d2e8e9f95983c5/ERCS/erc-8421.md)). Every category in the prize text sits in that difference: PQ signatures and ZK proofs that exceed 100k, and ERC-20 or quota paymasters that must read and write state.

The practical trap is version drift. The always-on devnet (chain 1337) pins EIP-8141 at a 30 July commit, and the sidecar enforces an earlier rule set that is "not equivalent" to ERC-8421 ([workshop README](https://github.com/forshtat/mempool-workshop)). The organizers' landing page quotes 250k and 2.5M budgets where the draft says 100k and 1M ([docs: differences](https://github.com/forshtat/mempool-workshop-documentation/blob/HEAD/src/content/docs/project/differences.md)). No released JS SDK supports frame transactions: viem's stack was closed unmerged ([viem PR #5118](https://github.com/wevm/viem/pull/5118)). The working toolkit is `@forshtat/hegota-support` 0.0.1, published 25 October. It records that only APPROVE and SIGPARAM were verified on chain 1337, and that Forge and Hardhat cannot execute the opcodes ([hegota-support](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@forshtat/hegota-support)). On 26 October the hackathon devnets (devnet1 to devnet3) were not yet answering. Teams should therefore ask the alt-mempool team on site which codec profile those devnets run before writing any encoder.

The less-done builds are tooling and stateful paymasters. The EIP itself admits that its ERC-20 paymaster example accepts front-running in the public pool ([EIP-8141](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141)). Magicians reviewers found that if the fee transfer fails, the user's call still runs for free, and that tokens returning false need handling ([Magicians #178](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/frame-transaction/27617/178)). ERC-8421 warns that an approving frame that ignores a failed `pre_verify` leaves the payer with the loss ([ERC-8421 draft](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/493ba8ac3be97dc5cf6335eb97d2e8e9f95983c5/ERCS/erc-8421.md)). A paymaster that pulls USDT in `pre_verify`, checks that frame's status before APPROVE, and refunds in `post_op`, shown rejected by the canonical pool and admitted by the alternative one, sits squarely in the prize text and has no published implementation. Tooling gaps are just as concrete: a viem action layer over the devnet codec, an admission linter that maps the node's ERC-7562 trace to rule IDs, and a prober that measures each devnet's real budgets. "Breaking the rules" also has a quantified opening. The draft concedes that a staked entity can cause "`BAN_SLACK * MIN_INCLUSION_RATE_DENOMINATOR / 24` invalid transactions per hour", which is about 21 per hour and "a rate limit, not a guarantee" ([ERC-8421 draft](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/493ba8ac3be97dc5cf6335eb97d2e8e9f95983c5/ERCS/erc-8421.md)). A competing design, MATCHA, prices the same risk in gas used rather than transactions seen ([MATCHA](https://ethresear.ch/t/mempool-account-transaction-capacity-from-historical-activity-matcha/25949)).

The headline "only possible here" ideas have more prior art than they appear to. The devnet hosts the real Tornado Cash 0.1 ETH pool and Railgun ([sidecar deployments](https://github.com/forshtat/mempool-workshop-sidecar/blob/HEAD/deployments/devnet.md)). An ERC-8421 co-author has published a minimal shielded pool on frames ([minimal-shielded-pool](https://github.com/Robert-MacWha/minimal-shielded-pool)). Himitsu, at ETHGlobal Tokyo 2026, already did privacy-pool spends as frame transactions with no app relayer ([Himitsu](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/himitsu-y62cx)). For PQ wallets, the cheapest path is already wired. The lfglabs SPHINCS- variants verify in 94k to 127k gas, and a full frame transaction costs about 188k on ethrex ([SPHINCS-](https://github.com/lfglabs-dev/SPHINCS-)). ZKNox's NIST Falcon-512 costs 447,047 gas and ML-DSA-44 about 1.2M, which does not fit even the staked 1M budget ([ETHFALCON](https://github.com/ZKNoxHQ/ETHFALCON); [ETHDILITHIUM](https://github.com/ZKNoxHQ/ETHDILITHIUM)). A hash-based PQ scheme on the EVM was already a Cannes 2026 finalist ([EVM PORST](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/evm-porst-kkcfo)). These remain good WOW-factor demos. To win, though, they need a twist nobody has shown, such as relayerless withdrawal from the unmodified Tornado pool through a staked paymaster, or a hybrid ECDSA plus SPHINCS- account with PQ key rotation.

### CROPS App and Upgrade: measurement and verification beat another wallet feature

The CROPS prize lists five ideas: recovery with nothing from the builder, a frontend that survives losing its domain or RPC, a server that learns nothing about who talks to whom, a leak-revealing tool, and a frontend-matches-repo verifier ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). They are not equally open. Recovery is the most crowded: WalletRecover, Shadow Guardians, Keyless Recovery and Carte Bleue all reached ETHGlobal showcases ([Shadow Guardians](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/shadow-guardians-8obhq); [Keyless Recovery](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/keyless-recovery-sb6rg)). PSE and Kohaku have social-recovery SDKs in flight, with open problems in guardian salt handoff and DKIM key rotation ([PSE social recovery](https://github.com/privacy-scaling-explorations/pse.dev/blob/main/content/articles/social-recovery-sdk.md); [Kohaku PR #338](https://github.com/ethereum/kohaku/pull/338)). Leak measurement exists for wallets, through Walletbeat's mitmproxy captures ([Walletbeat tools](https://github.com/walletbeat/walletbeat/tree/beta/src/tools/wallet-data-collection)), and for protocols, through L2BEAT's new privacy section ([L2BEAT privacy](https://l2beat.com/privacy)). It does not exist for dapps, even though one study counted over 2,000 leaks across 211 dapps ([arXiv 2306.08170](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.08170v1)). Frontend verification has only thin prior art. TEE Shield stored a developer-asserted hash on chain ([TEE Shield](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/tee-shield-6fx8p)), and dapp3.eth verifies ENS lookups with Helios ([dapp3.eth](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/dapp3eth/gagojfdpbmahciaapdcejmmaeehgpmib)). Nobody ties a served bundle back to a reproducible rebuild of a tagged commit.

Several smaller openings come straight from EF text. Kohaku's receive-side withdrawal rules (H1 to H5, with 18 conformance vectors) are written but not implemented in any wallet ([Kohaku issue #324](https://github.com/ethereum/kohaku/issues/324)). That makes a "stealth spend guard" a direct answer to the 38.92% linkage figure. Vitalik wrote that someone "should" build "an internet search skill that wraps around Tor or other internet anonymization" for local AI agents ([secure LLMs](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2026/04/02/secure_llms.html)). The Mandate's wallet quandary and the W-5 guarantee ask for local, inspectable risk filters with override paths, and neither has a reference implementation. The CROPS Upgrade prize, open only to Continuity entrants, has a ready-made target. Building the Kohaku reference extension requires Alchemy, Envio HyperSync and Pimlico keys ([kohaku-extension](https://github.com/ethereum/kohaku-extension)), and Kohaku has an open issue to "remove integrations into permissioned or centralized providers" ([Kohaku issues](https://github.com/ethereum/kohaku/issues)). Swapping those dependencies for Anon-RPC plus Colibri would be a textbook structural upgrade with a clean before and after.

## Whitechain wants private business payments, not payroll alone

Whitechain is a confirmed Core sponsor with $15,000 across four tracks ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). Its framing is entirely about merchants: "Give a business everything it needs to get paid in USDT on Whitechain, from the first checkout to what happens after it." Operate explicitly excludes "Payroll, escrow and revenue splits on their own" while inviting "Payment links and invoices for remote studios, contractors and cross-border teams" and "an export the accountant can use". Scale wants payers, human or AI agent, who never hold WBT for gas or see a seed phrase. Tokenize wants "compliance logic onchain" for invoices, bonds and trade finance ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). Privacy appears nowhere in Whitechain's prize text or developer docs ([Whitechain llms-full.txt](https://docs.whitechain.io/llms-full.txt)). A privacy project must therefore argue that confidentiality is what makes on-chain business payments viable: salaries, supplier lists, pricing and runway that a public ledger would otherwise expose. EthSystems, the spun-out EF institutional privacy team, makes the same case: "Business-to-business payment patterns reveal supplier relationships, pricing, and strategic priorities" ([EthSystems](https://ethsystems.org/use-cases/private-payments/)).

Technically, Whitechain is now a stock OP Stack L2 running op-reth, with WBT as the gas token and 1-second blocks. The hackathon testnet is Whitechain Sepolia (chain ID 1874). Mainnet (1875) went live on 26 October, despite the sponsor blurb's "mainnet arriving in November" ([Whitechain mainnet](https://docs.whitechain.io/learn/network/mainnet); [Whitechain testnet](https://docs.whitechain.io/learn/network/testnet)). A direct probe of the testnet RPC on 26 October found working **BN254 ecAdd, ecMul and ecPairing** precompiles, a working P256VERIFY precompile, and EIP-7702 delegation. It also found that the Osaka CLZ opcode is "NotActivated" on Sepolia, so contracts should be compiled for Prague ([Whitechain testnet RPC](https://rpc.testnet.whitechain.io); [testnet rollup.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/whitechain-labs/whitechain-bootstrap/main/testnet/rollup.json)). Groth16 and Noir verifiers should therefore deploy unchanged, subject to the 24,576-byte contract size limit. Nothing privacy-related is pre-deployed: the canonical ERC-5564 Announcer, ERC-6538 Registry, Semaphore and PoseidonT3 addresses are empty, though the CREATE2 deployers, Permit2, Safe and ERC-4337 EntryPoints v0.6 to v0.9 are present. No hosted bundler serves chain 1874 (Pimlico rejects it), and Reown embedded wallets warn "Login completes, transactions may fail" ([Pimlico endpoint](https://public.pimlico.io/v2/1874/rpc); [Whitechain Reown guide](https://docs.whitechain.io/build/dapps/connect-wallet-with-reown)). The prize USDT at `0xcedfB561...55a7` is verified as TetherTokenV2 with both `permit` and EIP-3009 `transferWithAuthorization`. It is a different contract from the bridged USDT in Whitechain's token list, which lacks EIP-3009 ([Blockscout implementation](https://explorer.testnet.whitechain.io/api/v2/smart-contracts/0x26425662d126412128967ecf0a02ec48573026f7); [token list](https://docs.whitechain.io/build/block-explorer/token-list)). The faucet dispenses only 0.5 WBT per day ([faucet docs](https://docs.whitechain.io/learn/get-started/get-testnet-wbt)), so teams must ask Whitechain how to get the qualifying USDT. The public RPC allows 50 requests per second per IP ([network reference](https://docs.whitechain.io/learn/network/reference)).

That chain profile decides which privacy technology is usable. FHE confidential tokens (ERC-7984) need Zama's coprocessors, threshold KMS and gateway ([Zama ERC-7984](https://www.zama.org/post/erc-7984-the-confidential-token-standard-explained)), and Zama's chain list does not include Whitechain ([Zama chains](https://docs.zama.org/protocol/protocol-apps/chains)). TEE options such as Inco Lightning likewise need operators on that chain. What runs without anyone's permission is ZK and stealth addresses. ERC-5564 stealth addresses hide recipients and their linkage across payments, but not amounts ([ERC-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564)). Shielded UTXO pools such as Zeto, which runs on "any unmodified EVM chain", hide amounts and the payment graph ([Zeto](https://github.com/LFDT-Paladin/zeto)). Avalanche's eERC hides amounts at about 947,000 gas per transfer but leaves addresses public ([Avalanche eERC](https://docs.avax.network/integrations/encrypted-erc)). Proof systems differ in verification cost. Groth16 verification costs roughly 207k plus 7.16k gas per public signal ([Aztec forum](https://forum.aztec.network/t/benchmark-for-verification-cost-across-noir-proof-backends/8061)), while the EthSystems Noir pool pays about 2.6M per UltraHonk verification ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)). Either fits Whitechain's 40M block gas limit.

The theme is crowded, so differentiation has to be structural. Warden won Unlink's prize at Cannes 2026 with shielded-pool payroll and email onboarding ([Warden](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/warden-puu33)). Manila won four prizes from three partners at New York 2026 with confidential USDC payroll run by an AI agent under a policy engine ([Manila](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/manila-fv16y)). PAYLOAD built Railgun payroll whose backend its own team called "not secure" ([PAYLOAD](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/payload-brtgd)). Cloak and CloakPay covered private freelancer invoicing and payer-hidden checkout at Cannes 2026 ([Cloak](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/cloak-7zm7s); [CloakPay](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/cloakpay-x7w9x)). FHE payroll apps fill Zama's community hub, and even Zama's own payroll page concedes that "sender and recipient addresses stay public" ([Zama payroll](https://www.zama.org/solutions/confidential-onchain-payroll)). What remains unbuilt is disclosure and hygiene, not hiding. EthSystems lists the missing pieces in its own pool: no batching, no viewing-key revocation, no gas paymaster ([EthSystems writeup](https://ethsystems.org/writeups/building-private-transfers-on-ethereum-with-shielded-pools/)). It also notes that there is "No common selective-disclosure format across MiCA, GENIUS, and national regimes" ([EthSystems approach](https://ethsystems.org/approaches/approach-private-payments/)). Starknet's payroll RFP asks for recipient income statements and a payroll book with "Cryptographic proof of completeness; no fabrication, no omission" ([Starknet RFP](https://strk20.starknet.io/rfp/private-payroll)), and no EVM product delivers either.

The credible Whitechain build follows from those facts. It is a drop-in invoicing and payment-link widget, the "about 20 lines" Operate wants. Each invoice resolves to a fresh stealth address derived from the business's registered meta-address, using a self-deployed Announcer and Registry. The client pays prize USDT by signing an EIP-3009 authorization that the merchant's own relayer submits, which gives Scale its zero visible crypto steps. Invoice metadata is encrypted on the client to the business's viewing key, and the accountant receives a per-period derived key (for example, one quarter) plus an exportable ledger. Refunds go out in one click, and contractor payouts from received revenue become the "what happens after it" step, which keeps the product out of the payroll-alone exclusion. The same token feature fixes the best-documented stealth-address leak. ERC-5564 warns that funding a stealth address's gas from a linked wallet breaks privacy ([ERC-5564](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5564)), but a stealth address that holds EIP-3009 USDT can spend by signature through a shared relayer and never needs WBT at all. The design also answers the EF's P-2 and P-4 guarantees. Because an EIP-3009 authorization can be submitted by any account, the payer or payee can broadcast it directly if the relayer refuses. And merchants can confirm settlement by scanning events locally through Anon-RPC, rather than trusting a hosted webhook that "re-create[s] the acquiring bank". Whitechain's CORS policy and viem's `fetchFn` make Anon-RPC reads work against its RPC unchanged. The risk is the 50 requests-per-second limit per exit IP, and the team did not test whether Cloudflare challenges Tor exits. A stretch tier adds a small USDT note pool with a batch payout circuit and an employee proof of income ("earned at least X over six months") computed against on-chain notes. That is harder, but it is the piece no prior project has shipped on EVM.

The README has to be honest about three weaknesses. A new chain starts with an anonymity set near zero. Payment timing is a fingerprint. And Whitechain itself has a single sequencer, permissioned fault proofs, a Guardian that can pause withdrawals, and forced inclusion only within a 12-hour window ([Whitechain finality](https://docs.whitechain.io/learn/network/transaction-finality)). Whether EF CROPS judges penalize that settlement layer is unknown; stating it as a trade-off is the documented mitigation. For the third partner slot, Uniswap needs only open source, a FEEDBACK.md and a form, and World IDKit's text names "a privacy-preserving eligibility check". Arc requires Arc Studio and settlement on Arc, which clashes with both Whitechain and CROPS "O" ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).

## Seven bets clear the novelty bar; the obvious ones do not

The ranking below weighs four things: whether the EF or the sponsor named the gap, whether prior art was found, whether a 2 to 4 person team can finish it in 36 hours, and how well it fits each rubric's "works end to end" and "could ship" tests. The ranks and difficulty ratings are this report's judgment. Prior art was searched mostly by project name, so "none found" means none surfaced, not none exists.

The table sorts into three portfolios, because one EF prize per project and three partner slots force a choice. A team optimizing for EF depth should take rank 1 or 2 and leave Whitechain out. A protocol-minded team should take rank 3 with rank 4 as its tooling spine; the alt-mempool prize then stands alone, because its devnets cannot host a Whitechain product. A team optimizing for prize count should take rank 7, enter Whitechain plus EF plus Uniswap or World, and route reads through Anon-RPC by default so the EF can place it under either CROPS or Anon-RPC. Three wildcards could also win if a team has the right expertise: a wallet policy engine that attaches EIP-7906 assertions to every frame transaction, which the EF explicitly requested; an Anon-RPC endpoint paid with zkAPI credits, which addresses the "reason to serve" incentive the Reads team declines to design; and Vitalik's Tor-wrapped search and read skill for local agents. These should be demoted: an opt-in Tor toggle, a bare viem transport, an EIP-1193 wrapper, a health dashboard on its own (a dev-only bench already runs weekly), a React Native harness, FHE payroll (crowded, and unavailable on Whitechain), and any "CSV upload plus vendor SDK" payroll dashboard.

## Conclusion

The EF's prizes are less a call for privacy apps than a call for the Mandate's "zero option" with a kill-switch demo attached. The richest unclaimed space is not any single primitive but compositions of EF artifacts that their teams currently ship separately: Anon-RPC with a light client, Anon-RPC with zkAPI payment, and frame transactions with assertions or stateful paymasters. The EF's own tooling is weeks old (spec 0.4.0 on 23 October, the devnet codec on 25 October), and the Mandate says it appreciates teams that "publish early, build openly, and invite critique". A team that hits a real bug in that tooling and documents it in the submission is doing work the authors want, which also gives the 3-minute Q&A something concrete to discuss.

For payments, the frontier has moved. Hiding amounts is done and crowded. What remains open is scoped, revocable disclosure, withdrawal hygiene, and removing the relayer as a chokepoint. That shift also resolves the apparent conflict between the two sponsors. Whitechain asks teams to run their own relayer, while the EF counts every intermediary against a project. A design in which the relayer is a convenience anyone can bypass, because the signed authorization can be self-broadcast and settlement can be checked locally, scores on both rubrics with one architecture and one README section.
