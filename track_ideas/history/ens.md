# ENS at ETHGlobal: track history, winners and what the judges reward (Sep 2023 to Oct 2026)

Written 2026-10-31 for ETHGlobal Mumbai (5 to 7 Nov 2026). Team: 3 people, DeFi and smart contract security, Classic track, fresh build only. Only one ENS track is open to us at Mumbai: **Best Use of ENSv2** ($7,000). The Continuity twin ("Best Integration of ENSv2 into an Existing Project", $3,000) is ignored except as context.

## How this was made

- **Prize text.** Fetched `https://ethglobal.com/events/<slug>/prizes/ens` for every ETHGlobal hackathon slug from New York 2023 to Tokyo 2026 (slugs from the event data on [ethglobal.com/events](https://ethglobal.com/events)), one request every 2.5 s. 20 events returned an ENS page. For the 15 that did not, the main prizes page was fetched and checked: none lists an ENS sponsor page (ETHOnline 2023, ETHIndia 2023, LFGHO, Circuit Breaker, Frameworks, Scaling Ethereum 2024, Sydney, StarkHack, Superhack 2024, ETHOnline 2024, Agentic Ethereum, Trifecta, Prague, Unite DeFi, ETHOnline 2025).
- **Winners.** Every showcase listing page for the 20 ENS events was scraped (`https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=<slug>&page=N`, 2 s apart); each project's prize organizations include ENS's organization id. Then 111 project pages were opened one at a time, 8 s apart, to read the "Winner of" badge (track and place), description and "How it's made". 
- **Bot check.** One early burst, run in parallel with the listing scrape, returned Cloudflare "Just a moment..." pages for four project URLs. I stopped, did not try to get around it, waited, and refetched serially at 8 s spacing; every later request succeeded. Nothing in this file rests on a blocked page.
- **Existing notes reused:** finalist catalogues in `research_notes/ETHGlobal finalist ideas 2023 to 2026/` (partner prizes per finalist), `track_ideas/00_current_tracks.md` (verbatim Mumbai text), `track_ideas/00_season.md`.
- **ENSv2 protocol facts** come from docs.ens.domains pages fetched today and the ENS blog post of 27 Oct 2026.
- Facts carry a URL. Anything marked **Judgement** is my inference. Quotes are verbatim; where a source quote contained a long dash I cut or paraphrased it.

**Judgement on the signal.**
- ENS has narrowed from "use ENS" (pools paying $140 to 43 teams) to one ENSv2 prize whose description is a feature checklist: wildcard resolution, subname registries, EAC, Permissioned Resolver, record aliasing, namespace aliasing, expiring, revocable, non-transferable and "forever names with no parent control". It wants builds that exercise the new contracts, ideally ones that teach ENS something about them.
- The agent tracks folded into one sentence ("Bonus points if you bring AI agents into the mix. Think agents as namespaces, each with their own identity and permissions."). Agents still help, but the judging centre is permissions and namespace design, not agent discovery.
- Mumbai is the first event after mainnet launch. The 27 Oct blog says the new Explorer lets people "inspect a name's history, see who controls it, understand how it resolves and examine its configuration" ([ENS blog](https://ens.domains/blog/post/ensv2-live-on-mainnet)), and names "Tokenized assets" as the example of "where this could lead". Safety of real names, and issuer namespaces, are therefore on ENS's mind this week.
- The docs page for app developers still says "ENSv2 is currently deployed on Sepolia for testing" and the deployments page says mainnet "resolution and registrations ... continue through the ENSv1 contracts" until launch completes ([app developers](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/tutorial-app-developers/); [deployments](https://docs.ens.domains/learn/deployments/)). The docs lag the blog; which mainnet paths are live this week is a workshop question.

**What this says about ENS's ENSv2 judges (Judgement).**
1. **A clear, legible delegation story wins, even ahead of depth.** Relay won 1st with a simulated model, over ENS Drive's verified registry subclass. Judges seem to reward the "agents as namespaces, each with their own identity and permissions" picture made concrete (derek.eng.sodalabs.eth), with a strong UI. Do not rely on this: Mumbai's rule "must be built on ENSv2" may be enforced harder now that mainnet is live, and a security team should win on both counts, not on a gap.
2. **The permission must sit on the path that matters.** Every placed agent entry enforced its limit somewhere an attacker cannot skip (Batas in the opcode, Capsule in the resolver, Herit in EAC bits). The many "read a policy from ENS, then decide in our backend" entries lost.
3. **Using an ENSv2 primitive for its exact reason** beats using many: Kura uses non-transferable and revocable names, Tide record-scoped roles, Verdict separation of setter roles.
4. **Writing like an auditor helps.** Herit ("We had to find three ENSv2 behaviors the hard way"), ENS Drive (fail-closed STATICCALL), Capsule ("That ordering is the security model") and Batas all document the protocol's sharp edges. ENS is still hardening ENSv2 ("Since Beta, the focus has been on refinement, security fixes", [ENS blog](https://ens.domains/blog/post/ensv2-live-on-mainnet)), so findings about its edges are useful to them.
5. **Stacking is normal.** Kura (Uniswap), Batas (Aqua mechanism), Herit (World ID), Tide (Aqua, v4, World), Verdict (agents). Of the 8 placed fresh ENSv2 entries, at least 5 used a second sponsor's stack at its core.

### 3.3 ENSv2 technical facts a winning entry must respect (sourced)

- "every account gets its own Permissioned Resolver proxy with fine-grained per-record permissions and record linking" ([overview](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/overview/)).
- Resolver roles are not per name: "There is no per-name scoping: a role holder can write the covered records on every name served by the resolver instance" ([Permissioned Resolver](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/permissioned-resolver/)).
- "Name ownership alone does not grant those roles" (resolver write roles) ([app developers](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/tutorial-app-developers/)).
- "A subname without its own resolver is served by the closest ancestor resolver" (same page); unlinked names fall back to "the default record, which is the record linked to the root name 0x00" ([Permissioned Resolver](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/permissioned-resolver/)).
- Operator approval "is an all-or-nothing delegation ... There is no way to scope operator approval to specific names or roles" ([Permissioned Registry](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/permissioned-registry)).
- `safeTransferFrom` reverts with `TransferUnsafeUntilRegistryIsEmancipated` or `TransferUnsafeWithMultipleAssignees`; `unsafeTransfer` skips both (same page).
- "rely on isEmancipated() only after verifying which implementation the registry runs: an arbitrary contract can simply always return true", and "True emancipation requires the entire chain from root down to be secured" (same page).
- EAC: "up to 15 holders of a role within the context of a single resource"; roles on `ROOT_RESOURCE` act as a master key; role changes regenerate the token ID to kill stale approvals ([EAC](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/enhanced-access-control/); [Permissioned Registry](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/permissioned-registry)).
- Contracts install with `forge install ensdomains/contracts-v2`; UserRegistry and resolver proxies come from the Verifiable Factory ([contract developers](https://docs.ens.domains/ensv2/tutorial-contract-developers/)).
- Agent records: ENSIP-25 `agent-registration[<registry>][<agentId>]`, whose security note says "If an ENS name is transferred to a new owner, any existing verification text records may become stale" ([ENSIP-25](https://docs.ens.domains/ensip/25/)); ENSIP-26 `agent-context` and `agent-endpoint[<protocol>]` ([ENSIP-26](https://docs.ens.domains/ensip/26/)).

**Judgement.** Read together, these facts leave an open question no past winner addressed: after a name or subname changes hands, who can still change where it resolves or what sits under it? The candidates are the old per-account resolver's role holders, the old owner's root roles on the name's subregistry (including `ROLE_UPGRADE` on a UUPS UserRegistry), operators, and ancestors that are not emancipated. `safeTransferFrom` checks only the parent registry's emancipation and other holders on the token itself. This needs confirming with ENS at the workshop, but if it holds, it is a security-team-shaped ENSv2 entry that nobody has built.

## 4. What this means for Mumbai (Judgement)

- **Expect a larger, more agent-heavy field than Tokyo** (44 ENSv2 entries at Tokyo, 49 at ETHOnline) and many reused "agent subname with scoped records" builds. Winning needs either a new enforcement point or an ENSv2 property used for a precise reason.
- **Mainnet is allowed and new.** A live demo on mainnet, even with one cheap name, is a differentiator nobody had before 27 Oct. Sepolia remains acceptable.
- **The live demo link is mandatory** (Tokyo and Mumbai text). A video alone does not qualify.
- **Highest-yield shapes for this team**: (a) a contract that extends or wraps ENSv2 (the ENS Drive route) with fuzz and formal checks; (b) a refusal on stage that rests on an ENSv2 rule (Batas and Herit style); (c) a DeFi or RWA product where one ENSv2 primitive carries the trust rule (Kura, Tide, Verdict), stacked with a second sponsor.
- **Avoid**: agent registries, agent discovery or "policy text record read by our backend" without onchain enforcement; ENS as a display name; name marketplaces without a new rule (rent.eth and SubnodeSymphony did this in 2023 and 2024).
