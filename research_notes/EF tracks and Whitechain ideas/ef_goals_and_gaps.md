# Ethereum Foundation goals, ideology (Mandate, CROPS) and stated gaps, mapped to ETHGlobal Mumbai (5 to 7 Nov 2026) prototypes

Research date: 2026-10-26. Sources are primary unless marked. Quotes are verbatim except that any dash character inside a source quote has been rendered as a comma or colon (the notes contain no em or en dashes by rule). Text outside quotation marks is my paraphrase; anything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning, not EF wording.

Note on method: blog.ethereum.org posts, the Mandate PDF, trilliondollarsecurity.org (including its JS data bundle), reads.ethereum.foundation, the ethereum GitHub org and Vitalik's X posts (via the fxtwitter API) were downloaded raw and read in full or keyword-scanned. The ESP wishlist and RFP item pages could not be read (see Q3 gaps).

## Q1. What does the EF Mandate say (CROPS definitions, walkaway test, commitments, will and will not do)?

### Takeaway
The Mandate (published 13 March 2026, also inscribed onchain) makes CROPS (Censorship resistance, Open source and free, Privacy, Security) an "indivisible whole" that is the non-negotiable filter for all EF work, sets the walkaway test as the "ultimate goal", and commits the EF to "subtraction": doing only what no one else will, refusing to build consumer apps, and insisting on a "zero option" (an intermediary-free path) for every intermediated affordance. That last commitment, plus its worked examples (wallet "safe modes", identity, relayer networks, dependencies that disappear), reads almost like a CROPS hackathon brief.

### Cited Findings
- Publication: blog post "The Promise of Ethereum: Introducing the EF Mandate", 13 Mar 2026, signed "The Ethereum Foundation Board"; describes the Mandate as "part constitution, part manifesto, and part guide" and says it "now lives on the World Computer" in an Ethereum transaction ([EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/03/13/ef-mandate); [Etherscan tx](https://etherscan.io/tx/0x5dd574df963a1df1f064791e0f6ff41ec972cdbba12293b7e1ece582052ba855); [PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)).
- Core framing from the blog post: "Ethereum must, above all, remain censorship resistant, open source, private, and secure (CROPS). Its self-sovereign use must be extraction-resistant and experience seamless. These are the conditions that make Ethereum worth using, and therefore worth building, and worth defending. They must never be traded away for convenience: without them we have nothing." ([EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/03/13/ef-mandate))
- Steward framing: "We were Ethereum's first steward. Now we are one of many." and "The EF is not Ethereum's parent, ruler, or final authority. Our role is stewardship." Infinite Garden: "a growing ecosystem of people, projects, communities, and institutions working to keep systems open, private, resilient, humane, and free." ([EF blog](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/03/13/ef-mandate))
- EF X thread wording: "EF will, above all else, remain focused on an Ethereum that is censorship resistant, open source, private, and secure (CROPS), in the service of user self-sovereignty, resistant to extraction and with seamless UX." (search snippet of [EF on X](https://x.com/ethereumfndn/status/2032460728855048667); not fetched directly)
- Mandate structure: I Ethereum, II Our Role, III Our Mandate, IV Principles for Action, V Carrying Out the Work, VI Resolving Quandaries, VII The Future, VIII Closing ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)).
- Self-sovereignty definition: "a user has the final say over their identities, assets, actions, and agents." Earlier: users need final say over "their data, their assets, their instructions, their identities, their agents, their essential digital gestalt, and the right to exit from any system that proves unfavorable" ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)).
- CROPS as non-negotiable: "We hold that these properties, CROPS, must remain, as an indivisible whole, the sine qua non of all Ethereum's development priorities, which cannot be displaced." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Walkaway test (Ch. II): "Our ultimate goal is for Ethereum to pass the walkaway test: its protocol and core application layers become robust and trustless enough that they would continue to reliably function and evolve even if the Foundation and today's core developers disappeared tomorrow." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Walkaway test for users (Security principle): "Security additionally means passing the walkaway test, not just for the protocol, but also for users: self-sovereignty means a user should not be forced into frequent, complex migrations that create unintended risks." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- CROPS definitions (Technical Pillar), verbatim headers: "Censorship Resistance: No actor can selectively exclude valid use or break functionality, including by gaining durable, non-competitive control of any critical mechanisms." "All work must be architected to be maximally unstoppable and to function without incorporating centralized intermediaries or kill switches." / "Open Source and Free, as in Freedom: No privileged code or hidden specifications." "All work must be public and auditable: no proprietary 'black boxes.' All work must also be forkable" / "Privacy: User data is not exposed beyond necessity or against their interests." / "Security: Things must do what they claim to do, no more and no less." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Licensing rule: "Supported projects must pledge that they will not change their open source or copyleft license in the future. Permissive licenses are accepted, viral copyleft licenses are appreciated, but merely source-available licenses are not tolerated." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Privacy as default: "We strongly advocate for maximal privacy to become the default for user data to the greatest extent possible: first in any tools that sit above the protocol that the Ethereum Foundation builds, and then ultimately in the protocol itself"; "end users should always selectively negotiate their disclosures, and that this should only be supported on top of a base of freely available, unconditional privacy." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Security and simplicity: "a protocol is not 'trustless' if only a small number of people can understand how it works and why it is secure. Work must be verifiable to many." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Social Pillar: "A billion users in a centralized silo is not a success"; Right Association: "we prefer to work more closely with those who also actively work to achieve independence from us." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Only-EF Rule: "We focus on critical tasks that have no other natural home and that no other ecosystem actor can or will reliably undertake," listing "core protocol upgrades and long-horizon research, neutral multi-client specs and tests, public-good security work, crisis coordination, preventing chokepoints, and core dev tooling and documentation where no sustainable owner exists." Downstream work: "When we work downstream, it is on making CROPS-native affordances competitive and viable for adoption." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Subtraction: "Our goal is to reduce the Foundation's relative influence over time." "The more Ethereum succeeds, the tinier we become." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Will NOT do (the "We are NOT" list): "a Corporate: We are not a development company. We do not build consumer apps. If it can be a sustainable business, it belongs in the community, and use of the protocol must not depend on it."; "a Kingmaker"; "an Accreditation Body: We do not certify or endorse projects, teams, or audits. However, we do support the development of mechanisms in line with our principles to help users evaluate security and legitimacy without relying on us"; "a Product Studio"; "a Marketing Agency"; "the Boss: We cannot force hard forks"; "a Government or Regulatory Body"; "a Casino"; "Opportunists: We do not actively assist in adoption of Ethereum in ways that compromise trustlessness." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- The admitted gap: "The world Ethereum must function in is not yet CROPS-native. Today, most use of Ethereum flows through partially centralized surfaces: wallets, RPC providers, relays to the MEV-industrial complex, app stores, exchanges, institutions, and the social defaults that surround them." Default path is "the CROPS-native approach"; the incrementalist path only "when it durably reduces central control." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Guiding question: "does this make Ethereum and its users less susceptible to capture over time, or does it normalize capture in exchange for reach?" ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Quandary 1 (relays): prefers a design where "broadcast is auditable and does not depend on a small set of intermediaries; private propagation is opt-in and escapable; and where users can route around censorship or extraction permissionlessly." "it is not sufficient that a solution simply works today; it also needs to not become a chokepoint tomorrow." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Quandary 2 (cross-layer): names five places where missing protocol features push users to intermediaries: scale, account types ("lacks a general-purpose account model capable of supporting smart accounts"), native privacy ("No construction layered on top could match the anonymity set the protocol itself could provide"), transaction protections ("protection against adverse execution outcomes"), and aggregation of ZK proofs (a likely "monopolistic" chokepoint) ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)).
- Quandary 3 (wallet safety), the anti-pattern: "a wallet ships a 'safe mode' enabled by default that incorporates dark design patterns such as silently blocking certain contracts, steering users toward preferred venues ... or that ships an AI copilot that flags 'risky' actions using an uninspectable proprietary model and reports user actions back home silently." The CROPS alternative: "a choice of independent locally-verifiable filters with transparent rules, multiple independently-built community-created and propagated whitelists and blacklists with clear override paths, and private-by-default tool use including any AI components." "The goal is not to sanitize the environment; it is to keep users sovereign inside it." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Quandary 4, the "zero option": "for every affordance that has an intermediated path, any intermediary-free path that is possible must be built and must remain credible and accessible ... We do not skip this step." Intermediary examples named: "block building, RPC servers, entities attesting to aspects of digital identity." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Identity example: rejects wrapping "government, biometric or corpoid" ID in a ZK proof as automatically CROPS; if only sybil resistance is needed, "Users who hold some quantity of ETH, for example, could provide a zero-knowledge proof of ownership of it, or post a zero-knowledge security deposit"; credentials should be "as local, verifiable, and non-custodial as possible"; stacks should "integrate multiple independent attestation sources" and support "multiple weaker signals, such as social-graph attestations." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Quandary 5 (judging teams), directly relevant to judges: failure mode of "thinking only about the 'happy case' ... but not about the 'unhappy case' such as where third-party dependencies (whether APIs, content delivery networks, or otherwise) disappear or break, or worse, where the team itself disappears or is hacked or an insider turns hostile." Red flags: "closed components, whitelists, soft-default routing, discretionary upgrade ability, or dependency-heavy integrations." Encouragement: "We do not require first versions to be complete; they only need to stay live." "We appreciate teams that publish early, build openly, and invite critique." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- d/acc-style framing: cites the Mohists' "Differential and open source promotion of 'defense'"; "Our approach is closer to writing the manuals and making them available, and not intervening in individual conflicts"; "de-totalization ... is the most reliably good aim." Closest collaborators: "those working directly on privacy, verifiability, and programmable cryptography." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- Closing: "Ethereum is so other people can't rug you; ... AI can't rug you; ... and so you don't accidentally rug yourself either." ([PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf))
- ethereum.org's EF page: the Mandate "enshrines the EF's dedication to censorship resistance, open source, privacy, and security (CROPS) as non-negotiable priorities." ([ethereum.org](https://ethereum.org/foundation/))

### Inferences
- The Mandate explicitly leaves consumer apps to the community and only works "downstream" to make CROPS-native affordances viable. That is the exact space a hackathon team occupies, so a project framed as "the zero option for X" (an intermediary-free fallback path for some common intermediated flow) aligns with the document's own vocabulary.
- Quandary 5's "unhappy case" language is effectively the CROPS prize demo requirement ("switch off your backend, swap the RPC provider"). Quoting it in the README CROPS section is likely to resonate with EF judges.
- The O letter in CROPS includes a license pledge; a README that states the license and a no-relicense pledge goes beyond the prize's minimum.

### Gaps
- The Mandate does not define a formal "walkaway test" procedure or checklist; the prize criteria (structural beats cosmetic) are the closest operationalization found.
- I did not find an EF document listing measurable CROPS metrics for apps; the closest are the access-layer-resources guarantees (Q3) and Walletbeat.

## Q2. What do 2025 to 2026 EF blog posts say about goals and open problems (protocol, lean, 1TS, privacy, institutions, interop, apps/DeFi, treasury, restructuring)?

### Takeaway
The EF has narrowed: a June 2026 reorganization cut about 20% of staff into five "layer" clusters (protocol, access, user, community, institutional); Protocol's north star is a quantum-resistant L1 by December 2029, with Hegota built around FOCIL plus EIP-8141 frame transactions (and privacy EIPs 8250/8272, plus EIP-7906 transaction assertions as a candidate); 1TS has moved from mapping to concrete work on clear signing, transaction assertions and verifiable frontends (WEBCAT); the privacy cluster ships private reads (anon-RPC, PIR) and zkAPI. The Access Layer cluster's mission statement is the clearest list of what the EF thinks is missing at the user edge.

### Cited Findings
Organization, leadership, treasury
- New structure (23 Jun 2026): "the EF now has five clusters ... protocol layer, access layer, user layer, community layer, and institutional layer," plus operations and management; "54 fewer colleagues, roughly 20% of the EF" ([EF structure](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/06/23/ef-structure)).
- Access Layer cluster mission (the key gap statement): "The access layer is where Ethereum either serves or fails individuals who need CROPS properties in practice. This cluster's job is to make self-sovereignty available, legible and survivable across key actions: reading the chain, transacting, proving, delegating, and exiting." Users and "the agents acting on their behalf ... must be able to read current state, history, and related data without depending on intermediaries they can't verify. They should be able to transact privately and without risk of censorship, with transaction outcomes that are either guaranteed or fail cost-free if conditions are not met." "users have to stay in control, granting bounded authority and revoking it at will, and keeping custody of their own intents rather than exposing them to intermediaries. The interfaces, from silicon to frontend, must be verifiable, understandable and recoverable" ([EF structure](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/06/23/ef-structure)).
- Protocol cluster: "defending the transaction pipeline against toxic MEV and privileged orderflow"; "It does not exist to make Ethereum more marketable" ([EF structure](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/06/23/ef-structure)).
- User layer: "The goal is not for the EF to become a product studio"; Community layer seeks allies in "free and open source, secure and local-first software and hardware"; Institutional layer goal: "showcases of effective integration ... that maximizes CROPS properties," e.g. "ensuring data portability and more generally practical ability to exit, protecting all actors' privacy, proving authenticity of data" ([EF structure](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/06/23/ef-structure)).
- Leadership: Tomasz Stanczak stepped down as Co-Executive Director; Bastian Aue appointed interim Co-ED (13 Feb 2026) ([leadership update](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/13/leadership-update)). Protocol coordinators Barnabe Monnot and Tim Beiko leaving, Alex Stokes on sabbatical; new coordinators Will Corcoran, Kev Wedderburn, Fredrik (May 2026) ([May 2026 update](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/05/11/protocol-update-may-26)). pcaversaccio joined the Board (29 Jul 2026); board now "Aya Miyaguchi (President), Vitalik Buterin (Founder), Patrick Storchenegger (Swiss counsel) and pcaversaccio" ([board update](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/07/29/ef-board-update)).
- Vitalik's personal note (24 May 2026): "EF is not a 'center of Ethereum', rather EF is 'one node, with a defined purpose, alongside other nodes'"; "the EF is choosing to use its remaining resources to pursue longevity over breadth (yes, this means we sell less ETH)"; EF focuses "specifically on those activities critical to the success of ethereum as a censorship/capture-resistant, open, private and secure system, that would not happen otherwise." ([Vitalik on X](https://x.com/VitalikButerin/status/2058583593102844111); coverage: [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/402435/vitalik-buterin-says-ethereum-foundation-will-be-a-smaller-ship-sell-less-eth-amid-researcher-exodus))
- Treasury policy (4 Jun 2025): opex target "A = 15% of treasury" and "B = 2.5 years" buffer, reducing opex "roughly linearly over the next five years, ending at a long-term 5% baseline"; "Defipunk" criteria include permissionless access, FLOSS, privacy, immutability, oracle minimization, and "Is the primary UI open source and hosted in a decentralized manner?"; names the problem that DeFi relies on "centralized and surveilled UIs, and a general absence of onchain privacy"; commits to "Promoting research and development of decentralized UIs" ([treasury policy](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/06/04/ef-treasury-policy)).
- Staking: about 70,000 ETH staked using Dirk and Vouch, minority clients, building blocks locally (24 Feb 2026) ([staking](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/24/staking)).

Protocol
- 2026 tracks (18 Feb 2026): Scale, Improve UX, Harden the L1. Improve UX: "the end state is smart contract wallets as the default without bundlers, relayers, or extra gas overhead"; Harden the L1 covers "post-execution transaction assertions and trustless RPCs" and "the development of measurable censorship resistance metrics across the ecosystem" ([priorities 2026](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/18/protocol-priorities-update-2026)).
- Current priorities (7 Sep 2026): "Ethereum L1 to be quantum-resistant across all three layers (execution, consensus, and data) by December 2029"; five research arcs: fast finality, post-quantum, privacy, state, zkEVM; imperative: "Concentrate Protocol on what only Protocol can do and will do, and complete the Strawmap items that allow Ethereum to uphold the Mandate." ([protocol priorities](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities))
- Hegota headliners: "EIP-7805 Fork-choice enforced Inclusion Lists (FOCIL) ... and EIP-8141 Frame Transaction." Privacy gap stated: "Ethereum is still missing some basic protocol support that privacy applications need. EIP-8250 Keyed Nonces lets many users share the same sender for better anonymity ... EIP-8272 Recent Roots lets private transactions use recent onchain state in a form FOCIL can check." Privacy arc goal: "native, trustless, censorship resistant private transactions on Ethereum L1," later "encrypted mempools" ([protocol priorities](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities)).
- Security package: "EIP-7906 Transaction Assertions via State Diff Opcode lets transactions verify specified effects before committing, protecting against attacks such as drainers"; EIP-8298 and EIP-8151 "form the account path for retiring secp256k1 as a master key" ([protocol priorities](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-priorities)). Tier list: 8141 tier S; 8250, 8272, 7906 tier A; 7906 "currently running with Frames on a public devnet"; EIP-8182 (enshrined private transfers) tier D because "the Frames-based path delivers the same goal with less protocol surface"; EIP-8077 mempool announcements tier B: "Many want richer announcements for the Frames-era mempool" ([Hegota tier list](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/07/protocol-hegota-eips)).
- Schedule status: Glamsterdam activated on Sepolia 6 Oct 2026; Hoodi activation (26 Oct) "has been postponed. A new activation date has not been set." ([Hoodi delay](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/22/glamsterdam-hoodi-delay))
- Frame tx mempool design note: public mempool rules (validation-only simulation, one pending tx per sender, storage reads limited to sender); "The hard problem is paymasters"; Strategy 3 (full ERC-7562 staking and reputation) is "likely a later-stage policy" ([frame mempool note](https://hackmd.io/@matt/frame-mempool)).
- lean Ethereum (31 Jul 2025): "Ethereum must survive anything: nation states, quantum computers"; lean consensus "formerly branded as 'beam chain'"; "full chain verification across every browser, wallet, phone" ([lean Ethereum](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/07/31/lean-ethereum)).

Trillion Dollar Security (1TS)
- Launch (14 May 2025): mapping domains including "UX (blind signing, frontend security), wallet security (firmware issues, supply chain attacks) ... internet infra (DNS level censorship)" ([1TS launch](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/05/14/trillion-dollar-security)).
- Phase 2 (20 Aug 2025): first wave "mostly focus on UX issues"; wallet "Minimum Security Standard" requiring "Transparent transactions; Compromise-resistant interfaces; Privacy-supporting architecture; Standards for wallet behaviour, e.g. approval management, key handling, frontend verification" with Walletbeat; decoding problem: function-signature datasets are often "closed source and require expensive licenses," so promote VERA (8M+ contracts). Explicit asks: "Ultra simple non-tech wallet ... easy transactions to friends and businesses (not having to type a public key), easy payments for goods and services, built-in basic swapping, and the ability to restore your wallet. If you have ideas on how to address these issues then please reach out." and "Enterprise focused wallets: Enterprises have mentioned the importance of privacy, censorship resistance (including external services being used by the wallet to interact with the network)" ([1TS phase 2](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/08/20/trillion-dollar-sec-2)).
- Clear Signing (12 May 2026): working group launched an open standard to end blind signing; "'What You See Is What You Sign' (WYSIWYS) must be our goal, and Clear Signing must be the default"; needs "a shared format for these descriptions (ERC-7730), a registry to store and distribute them, a way to verify that they are accurate, and tools that make it easy for wallets and developers to adopt"; "Anyone can contribute descriptors"; "security experts are encouraged to review and attest to their correctness"; EF 1TS hosts the registry; Rust and TypeScript libraries on clearsigning.org ([Clear Signing](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/05/12/clear-signing-announcement)).
- WEBCAT grant (5 Aug 2026): "HTTPS authenticates the site you connect to ... but it does not prove that the code the site serves matches what its developers published." "Trillion Dollar Security has identified front-end hacks as an infrastructure risk and verifiable front ends as a next step." Grant funds "a WEBCAT verification library that wallets can integrate," Chromium research, an audit, and "an Ethereum Request for Comments (ERC) standard." Current state: "the current alpha Firefox extension prevents the page from loading." Adoption requirement: "Wallet extensions must integrate the library, and app teams must enroll their domains and serve a signed manifest with each release." ([WEBCAT grant](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/08/05/1ts-grant))
- Transaction assertions (5 Oct 2026): "Ethereum has no general mechanism for onchain code to inspect the full set of net state changes and events produced by a transaction." Simulation limits: Radiant case where "both the wallet interface and the simulation showed the intended transaction, while malware ... substituted a malicious payload." Key design point: "A compromised frontend can write an assertion that permits the attack. A useful rule must come from independently approved user intent, a standing account policy or protocol logic." "EIP-7906 does not require transactions to include an assertion ... the wallet must ensure that every frame transaction it builds includes the required assertion." Ask: "we want to hear from the wallet and protocol teams who would use them." Also suggests agent delegation use: "constrain both what the agent may do and what its actions produce." ([tx assertions](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions))

Privacy
- Commitment (8 Oct 2025): Privacy cluster of 47 people coordinated by Igor Barinov; initiatives: Private Reads and Writes, Private Proving, Private Identities, Privacy Experience, Institutional Privacy Task Force (IPTF), and Kohaku ("a new reference implementation of a privacy-preserving wallet and an open-source wallet SDK") ([privacy commitment](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/10/08/privacy-commitment); [cluster leads](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/10/01/privacy-cluster-leads)).
- zkAPI (1 Oct 2026): "Every AI API call today carries an identity"; vault plus Groth16 proofs mint short-lived capped API keys; live on mainnet; use cases explicitly include "Blockchain RPC" (hides "which funding source paid for the queries") and VPN bandwidth; stated limitations: "the lack of network anonymity, and privacy leakage via prompt contents" ([zkAPI](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/01/introducing-zkapi); [repo](https://github.com/ethereum/zkapi)).
- Funding-mechanism experiment: Keyring zkVerified vault fees routed to Tornado Cash developers' legal defense funds ([funding](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/10/09/new-funding-mechanisms)).

Apps, DeFi, institutions, interop, L2s
- DeFi (23 Feb 2026): wants DeFi "permissionless, censorship-resistant, privacy-first, self-custodial, and open source"; security focus on "interfaces, oracles, upgrade mechanisms, admin keys, and the discretionary multisigs"; privacy "first for payments of all tokens, then for more complex use cases like trading and lending"; open idea: "combine DeFi with our work on ZK reputation, and make the world's first implementation of privacy-preserving undercollateralized lending" ([DeFi](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/23/commitment-to-defi)).
- Institutions (1 Jul 2026): report "Ethereum for Governments and Institutions" argues for "credibly neutral infrastructure where the protocol itself enforces the rules" ([institutions](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/07/01/ethereum-for-institutions)).
- Interop (29 Aug 2025): "interoperability ... as the highest leverage opportunity within the broader UX domain"; warns "intent protocols can often introduce trust assumptions on solver intermediaries, which can create censorship vectors and privacy issues"; projects: Open Intents Framework, EIL, interop standards ERC-7828/7930, ERC-7811, ERC-5792, ERC-7683, ERC-7786 ([Protocol Update 003](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/08/29/protocol-update-003)). EIL: "Cross-L2 interop still resembles a CEX model, with bridge operators, relayers, solvers, and opaque off-chain infrastructure" ([EIL](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2025/11/18/eil)).
- L1 and L2 (23 Mar 2026): L2s should "at least reach Stage 1 and pass the 'walkaway' test, meaning users can safely exit to L1 even in the presence of malicious operators or security council failure" ([L1 and L2](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/03/23/l1-l2-ethereum)).
- Public goods sustainability (Project Odin, 27 Feb 2026): "everyone depends on shared infrastructure, but no one wants to risk ending up at a competitive disadvantage by being the one to fund it" ([Odin](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/02/27/project-odin)).

### Inferences
- The Access Layer verbs (read, transact, prove, delegate, exit) are a ready-made taxonomy for pitching a CROPS project; the EF prizes at Mumbai are explicitly from the Access Layer team (Q7).
- EIP-7906's own post names an unbuilt wallet-side piece: something that derives assertions from independent user intent or standing policy and refuses to build frame transactions without them. That is buildable on the frame devnets during the hackathon and touches both the alt-mempool and CROPS tracks.
- zkAPI lists "Blockchain RPC" as a use case, and the Reads team says operators "need a reason to serve requests" but the EF will "not design the incentive mechanism" (Q3). A zkAPI-paid anon-RPC endpoint would combine two EF artifacts in a way neither team has shipped.

### Gaps
- I did not read the Strawmap or individual Hegota EIPs beyond the EF posts.
- No 2025 to 2026 EF blog post specifically about "d/acc" was found; the Mandate's de-totalization passage is the closest EF statement. Vitalik's "d/acc: one year later" (Jan 2025) was not read.

## Q3. What do EF sub-sites list as needed work (ESP, PSE/privacy, Reads/Access Layer, Kohaku, 1TS dashboard)?

### Takeaway
ESP currently has no open wishlist items, RFPs or rounds, so the most concrete "please build this" lists now live in the Access Layer team's materials: the Reads roadmap and open-problems tracker, the anon-RPC repo, the access-layer-resources guarantee catalog (W-1..W-6, P-1..P-4, X-1..X-4), the Kohaku roadmap, and the 1TS dashboard's controls marked "Research" or "Planned".

### Takeaway
Decentralized hosting (IPFS or Arweave under ENS) is rated "Live" by 1TS, but verification that the code a user runs matches the published build is still research (1TS "Verifiable Frontend Hashes") or alpha (WEBCAT Firefox extension, wallet library and ERC being funded). ## Q6. Which security gaps are named (blind signing, simulation, ERC-7730, drainers, key recovery, social recovery, incident response)?

### Takeaway
The EF's own diagnosis is that "today user experience is the weakest link." Clear signing (ERC-7730 registry) is live but needs descriptors, attestations and wallet adoption; simulation is spoofable; transaction assertions (EIP-7906) are the protocol answer but require wallets to enforce independently sourced rules; approvals are still unlimited by default; recovery without custodians and without linkage is early.

### Cited Findings
- "UX security and safety was the top issue identified ... today user experience is the weakest link." Key management: seed phrases lead to "insecure workarounds"; hardware wallets often "not open source and may have opaque supply chains"; enterprises need key replacement on personnel change ([1TS report PDF](https://ethereum.org/reports/trillion-dollar-security.pdf)).
- Blind signing: "Users routinely approve transactions 'blindly'"; approvals: "many wallets default to granting unlimited approvals with no expiry date. There is no way for users to manage or review their outstanding approvals from within most wallets." ([1TS report PDF](https://ethereum.org/reports/trillion-dollar-security.pdf))
- Fragmentation: "users cannot rely on consistent UX cues to protect themselves from phishing and spoofing as they differ across wallets" ([1TS report PDF](https://ethereum.org/reports/trillion-dollar-security.pdf)); 1TS risk: "few wallets actively engage in standards work, and coordination is hosted by a single vendor rather than neutral ground" ([1TS dashboard](https://trilliondollarsecurity.org/category/user-experience)).
- Clear Signing gaps: registry, accuracy verification, tooling, descriptor contributions and expert attestations all needed ([Clear Signing](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/05/12/clear-signing-announcement)); EF-hosted registry repo exists ([clear-signing-erc7730-registry](https://github.com/ethereum/clear-signing-erc7730-registry)).
- Simulation and assertions: simulation fails when "the transaction signed may differ from the one simulated"; assertions must come from "independently approved user intent, a standing account policy or protocol logic" ([tx assertions](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions)).
- Smart contract gaps: "Lack of secure defaults in popular frameworks ... unlimited token approvals"; "Users and developers cannot easily assess the trustworthiness of deployed contracts"; "some of the existing services are themselves centralized dependencies" ([1TS report PDF](https://ethereum.org/reports/trillion-dollar-security.pdf)).
- Incident response gaps: "It can be difficult to get in contact with the team whose application has been compromised"; "It is often unclear how many incident response teams are assisting"; "Lack of monitoring capabilities" ([1TS report PDF](https://ethereum.org/reports/trillion-dollar-security.pdf)).
- Recovery: W-6 says "guardian-hiding variants ... are early" ([wallet domain](https://github.com/ethereum/access-layer-resources/blob/HEAD/domains/wallet.md)); Kohaku wants "standardized maximally-intermediary-free" social recovery via ZKemail, ZKpassport, Anon Aadhaar ([Kohaku roadmap](https://notes.ethereum.org/@niard/KohakuRoadmap)); PSE: "zk account recovery combination framework of N of M methods" ([PSE roadmap](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/pse-roadmap-2025-and-beyond/25423)).
- AI-assisted security: EF found "the triage is the hard part" when running AI agents against protocol code ([triage post](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/07/09/triage-is-the-product)).

### Inferences
- The CROPS prize's first example ("a wallet a user can fully recover with nothing from you: no server, API or relayer") sits exactly on W-6 plus W-4: recovery that needs a bundler or relayer fails the zero-option test, which frame transactions (self-relay in the public mempool) could remove.

### Gaps
- I did not check current Walletbeat scoring criteria in detail.
