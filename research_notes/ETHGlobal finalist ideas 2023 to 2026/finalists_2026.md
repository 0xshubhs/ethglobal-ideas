# ETHGlobal finalists at ETHGlobal hackathons, March to October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-27. Method: the ETHGlobal events list was parsed from the raw Next.js data on https://ethglobal.com/events to find every event of type "hackathon" in the window. Every showcase page for each event (https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=<slug>&page=N) was then scraped until the pages ran out. Every project carrying a prize from the organization "ETHGlobal" (org uuid xdat5, prize type "finalist") was opened on its own showcase page, which gave the badge name, partner prizes, description and "How it's made" text. Tokyo 2026 finalists carry no badge in the showcase data, so they were identified from ETHGlobal's own YouTube closing-ceremony description and matched to showcase pages by name. Quotes are verbatim unless marked. Where a quote contained an em dash or en dash it was cut or paraphrased. Category tags are the researcher's own classification (inference), using the fixed tag list from the brief.

## Q1. Which ETHGlobal hackathons in March to October 2026 had ETHGlobal finalists, and who were they?

### Takeaway
Six ETHGlobal hackathons fall in the window: Cannes 2026, Open Agents (online), New York 2026, Lisbon 2026, ETHOnline 2026 (online) and Tokyo 2026. Together they named 53 ETHGlobal finalists: 10 at Cannes, 7 at Open Agents, 10 at New York, 10 at Lisbon, 8 at ETHOnline and 8 at Tokyo. HackMoney 2026 ran 30 Jan to 11 Feb 2026, so it falls outside the window, and Mumbai (5 to 7 Nov) has not happened yet. The Continuity Track first ran at New York in June 2026, where 3 of the 10 finalists were Continuity entries. Lisbon, ETHOnline and Tokyo each had at least one Continuity finalist.

### Inferences
- ETHGlobal names "up to 10" finalists rather than exactly 10. Online events named fewer (7 and 8), and so did Tokyo (8), even where the prize copy says "top 10".
- The Continuity share among finalists is similar to its share of submissions. At NYC it was 30% of finalists against 26% of submissions. So pre-existing work is not a bar to the finale when the new hackathon work is clearly separated. Void Tactics, for example, has a heading "What Existed Before the Hackathon".
- Several builders made the finale more than once in 2026. These links are inferred from matching GitHub owners or the teams' own statements:
  - ENShell (Cannes) → Immunity (NYC). Immunity's page says "It also merges concepts from my previous Cannes hackathon project ENShell" and "I started this project in Open Agent hackathon".
  - GitHub user forever8896: Slopstock (Open Agents) and Lortnoc Tahc (Lisbon).
  - GitHub user JeanBaptisteDurand: LPlens (Open Agents) and TARE (ETHOnline).
  - GitHub user Avoisavo: credited on DIVE (Cannes, per crypto.news) and owns the Petri repo (ETHOnline).

### Gaps
- Tokyo 2026 finalists have no "Finalist" badge on any showcase page. The 8 names come only from ETHGlobal's YouTube description. Two Tokyo projects share the name: [Omamori (omamori-snyca)](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/omamori-snyca) and [Omamoris (omamoris-rn3nc)](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/omamoris-rn3nc), whose page also calls the product "Omamori". Which one was the finalist is not confirmed. Inference: omamoris-rn3nc is more likely, because its text says "A bun + TypeScript monorepo with a firewall" and the chapter title is "Agent Payment Firewall". However, omamori-snyca is also an agent-payment safeguard. Tokyo partner prize data also looks incomplete, with only 25 of 254 projects showing any prize.
- No official submission count was found for Open Agents or ETHOnline 2026. The showcase counts (468 and 812) may differ from submissions if some projects are hidden.
- The YouTube transcripts could not be fetched (caption endpoints returned empty), so details spoken on stage are not captured. That includes the Tokyo Continuity finalists and ETHOnline stats.
- Continuity status is confirmed only where a page or ETHGlobal says so. Other finalists at Lisbon, ETHOnline and Tokyo may also have been Continuity entries.
- Why ETHOnline named 8 finalists when the prize copy says "top 10" is not stated anywhere found.

## Q2. What did each finalist build, and what did the demo prove? (full catalogue)

### Takeaway
Most of the 53 finalists rest on one concrete, checkable claim shown live. Examples: an attestation that breaks if one line of code changes, a key that never leaves a card or chip, a refund the contract executes when data is stale, a verifier rule that a model cannot widen, or real money on mainnet. About half of all finalists won no partner prize at all. That points to ETHGlobal's judges choosing on the project itself, not on sponsor integrations.

## Q3. Which categories dominate each event, and how do 2026 finalists differ from earlier years?

### Takeaway
AI agents dominate 2026 finalists, appearing as the primary tag on 13 of 53 and as a primary or secondary theme on over half. The agent work has shifted from "agents that do things" to agent safety, budgets, verification and markets. Security and dev tooling (8) and DeFi (8) come next. Hardware appears at a notable rate, with 9 of 53 relying on a physical device. Privacy is usually built in as a property (TEE attestation, keys that never leave a device) rather than being the product itself. Compared with Bangkok 2024, where 4 of 10 finalists were games, 2026 finalists are much less about gaming and much more about agent infrastructure, x402 payments and verifiability.

### Inferences
- **AI agents:** the leading theme, but the finalists are mostly about constraining or verifying agents rather than raw autonomy. Examples are firewalls and guards (ENShell, Immunity, Aegis402, Omamori), budgets (Cordon), refunds (OpenBook), verified evolution (Petri), legal wrappers (Novi Corpus) and human-in-the-loop routing (yohaku). This differs from Bangkok 2024, where agent finalists (DAOGenie, Industry.ai) were about agents taking actions.
- **Hardware:** stronger than the 2024 baseline. Nine of 53 finalists rely on a physical device, and Lisbon put two hardware projects among its ten (robot arm, FPGA chip). Bangkok 2024's ten finalists, as described by The Block, include no hardware project.
- **Privacy:** usually present as a property built into the design (TEEs, keys that never leave a device, client-side-only wallets), and rarely as the product itself (VEIL VPN, Lortnoc Tahc, Enconomy). Classic ZK circuits are rare among finalists.
- **Security and dev tooling:** a strong second cluster (8 primary), often aimed at the agent and supply-chain era: npm malware, prompt injection, phishing cloaking, hook fee transparency, agent-drivable dev wallets.
- **Gaming:** fell from 4 of 10 at Bangkok 2024 to 3 of 53 in 2026, and the 2026 games each add a twist: agent players (Clan World), anti-sybil tournaments (Void Tactics), physical hardware (ETH Arcade).
- **Event differences:** NYC leaned to prediction-market DeFi and consumer earning. Lisbon was the most varied, with hardware and real-world assets. The online events were dominated by agent infrastructure (Open Agents was agent-themed by design). Tokyo leaned to real-world and Japan-local uses (cash kiosk, trading cards, elderly payments, proximity proof) and to sponsor-specific primitives (1inch Aqua took three finalist prizes: OddsFlow, watermark, Takarabako).
- A practical read for Mumbai: a finalist-grade 2026 project pairs one sponsor-agnostic, falsifiable claim with a live demo. When it touches agents, it adds a refusal or verification layer rather than more autonomy.

### Gaps
