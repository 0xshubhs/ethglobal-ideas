# Finalists win by proving one claim live

From ETHOnline 2023 to ETHGlobal Tokyo 2026, ETHGlobal's judges named **320 finalists across 34 hackathons**. That is between about 1 and 6 percent of submissions at most events (Sydney 2024's 8 of 66 is the outlier), and 1.6 percent at New Delhi's 616. The projects that recur share one habit. They put **one falsifiable claim on stage and prove it in the room**, and they usually add a stated limit, real money or a physical device, and a standard that shipped only weeks earlier. Themes turned over quickly. ZK proofs of off-chain facts and EIP-4844 blobs marked late 2023 and 2024. AI agents filled all ten slots at Agentic Ethereum in February 2025, fell back to about one finalist in nine for the rest of 2025, and rose to a quarter of finalists in 2026. x402 agent payments went from a single Prague finalist in mid 2025 to the core of 9 of 40 finalists between New Delhi 2025 and HackMoney 2026. In 2026, finalists turned to constraining agents and to purpose-built hardware. Agent payment rails, agent firewalls, prediction markets, dark pools, private payroll and ZK fiat ramps are now crowded. Real-world-asset finance, DeFi-security measurement, verified private reads, signing and frontend integrity, and exit paths that survive a team's disappearance are nearly empty. India adds a warning. None of New Delhi 2025's ten finalists mentioned India, although at least 18 submissions were built around UPI, Aadhaar or INR. ETHIndia 2023's zkP2M did reach the finalist round, because it proved its Razorpay leg with TLSNotary at a time when nobody else had. For a three-person DeFi and smart contract security team at Mumbai, the strongest new bets are **Walkaway**, which exits real DeFi positions with the protocol's frontend, API and RPC companies switched off, and **Truthsign**, which proves that a clear-signing screen matches what the transaction does; **Lastgas** or **Tripwire** suit the alt mempool prize, and **Hundi** suits Whitechain Tokenize. Of the team's five existing plans, only SameBuild (E) and the Dapp Leak Report (B) look like finalists, and both need their demos rebuilt around a single beat.

## 320 finalists from 34 hackathons, counted from ETHGlobal's own badges

The catalogue comes from five research passes over ETHGlobal's showcase. Each pass paged every event's project listing and kept the projects carrying ETHGlobal's own finalist prize ("<Event> Finalist", sponsor ETHGlobal). Every one of those pages was then opened. Where press lists exist, they match name for name, for example The Block for Bangkok and RootData for Cannes 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/326975/ethglobal-hackathons-10-finalists-showcase-ai-agents-crypto-games-dao-tools-and-more); [RootData](https://www.rootdata.com/news/309069)). Four counting choices shape the total. **ETHIndia 2023 is included**: Devfolio ran it, but ETHGlobal listed it and issued finalist badges ([zkP2M](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zkp2m-igbxk)). **Trifecta counts as 15**, because it ranked three winners and two "Track Finalists" in each of three tracks ([Trifecta prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/trifecta/prizes)). **Hubble Trading Arena is included**: it is in ETHGlobal's announced Buenos Aires top 10, but its page shows no badge ([Hubble](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/hubble-trading-arena-oddx1)). And **Tokyo 2026 is reconstructed**: its eight finalists carry no badges, so the names come from ETHGlobal's closing-ceremony description ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XLFyrOE19N8)). By calendar year, the total splits into 34 finalists from 3 events in late 2023, 123 from 14 events in 2024, 100 from 10 events in 2025 and 63 from 7 events in 2026.

Two caveats apply throughout. First, every "promise" here is the team's own showcase text. Nobody watched the demo videos, so the analysis reads what finalists claimed, not what judges saw. Second, the category tags are the researchers' own, with one primary tag per project. ETHGlobal publishes none, so any per-event count could move by one or two. The judging itself is stable and public: a 7-minute session (4 minutes of demo, 3 of Q&A) scored on Technicality, Originality, Practicality, Usability and WOW Factor, with no published weights. The wording is identical at Mumbai, New York 2026 and Agentic Ethereum ([Mumbai details](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details); [NYC 2026 details](https://ethglobal.com/events/newyork2026/info/details)).

**ZK and blobs (late 2023 to mid 2024).** In this period the recurring demo was "a proof that something done off-chain was done right". About 11 of 101 finalists used it, with Noir the most common stack. Privacy or ZK appeared as a primary or secondary tag on at least 30 of 101. Blobs show how quickly judges reward a capability the protocol has just shipped. London 2024 put three blob projects among its ten finalists ([μBlob](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/mblob-u9tkv); [panDA](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/panda-jk4mc); [BlobScriptions](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/blobscriptions-k42gp)). Scaling Ethereum added BlobFusion, whose page opens "One month ago, Dencun went live" ([BlobFusion](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/blobfusion-fjcjd)). Sponsor-themed online events skewed the mix completely: all ten LFGHO finalists used GHO, and all ten Frameworks finalists were Farcaster Frames ([LFGHO showcase](https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=lfgho); [Frameworks showcase](https://ethglobal.com/showcase?events=frameworks)). Late 2024 shifted toward FHE at Fhenix-sponsored events, IP licensing at San Francisco (three Story projects) and Telegram consumer games at Bangkok, where four of ten finalists were games ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/326975/ethglobal-hackathons-10-finalists-showcase-ai-agents-crypto-games-dao-tools-and-more)).

**AI agents (early 2025).** Agents were rare before the dedicated event: 2 of 101 finalists through June 2024, and 4 of 56 from July to November 2024. At Agentic Ethereum, agent keywords appeared in 432 of 517 submission taglines and every finalist was an agent project, with six of ten winning Coinbase's AgentKit pool ([Agentic Ethereum prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/agents/prizes); [Nimble](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/nimble-d5y6f)). Trifecta then judged agents next to TEE and ZK tracks, and agents started borrowing those trust tools: Glider and AgenticME ran browser agents inside TEEs ([Glider](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/glider-zr9bd); [AgenticME](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/agenticme-jspd4)). From April to August 2025, events had zero to three agent-first finalists each, and agent keywords fell to 10 to 27 percent of taglines. Across 2025 after Agentic Ethereum, agents were the primary tag of 10 of 90 finalists.

**x402 and agent safety (late 2025 and 2026).** Agents changed from actors into paying customers. HTTP 402 first reached a finalist with MCPay.fun at Prague 2025 ([MCPay.fun](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/mcpay-fun-y16d3)). x402-flash and Kyma Pay followed at New York 2025 ([x402-flash](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/x402-flash-yj9wd)). Between New Delhi 2025 and HackMoney 2026, x402 was central to 9 of 40 finalists, including Autonome, Paybot and router402 ([Autonome](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/autonome-d8cxe); [Paybot](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/paybot-q7grd); [router402](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/router402-b717q)). In 2026, about 16 of 53 finalists use x402, but the agent projects that win now limit autonomy rather than add it: firewalls and guards (ENShell, Immunity, Aegis402, Omamori), budgets and refunds (Cordon's hierarchical budgets, OpenBook's freshness refunds) and verified self-improvement (Petri) ([ENShell](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/enshell-6t95y); [Cordon](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/cordon-vw3kh); [OpenBook](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/openbook-8ngw6)). Slopstock's page states the shift directly: "The 2024 narrative was 'agents that do things.' The 2026 narrative is 'agents as productive property'" ([Slopstock](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/slopstock-7zgd6)).

**Hardware (2026).** The primary-tag share of hardware barely moves (3 to 5 percent in every year). What changed is the kind of device. In 2023 and 2024, hardware finalists mostly tapped NFC chips, often the event's own wristbands. In 2026, **9 of 53 finalists depend on a device the team built or wired**, among them a robot arm that remote workers drive (Hands Unchained), an FPGA prototype of a proving coprocessor (Lean Silicon), a cash kiosk with a bill acceptor (Takarabako), an ESP32 hardware wallet (LeekDIYHardwareWallet) and a Raspberry Pi arcade handheld (ETH Arcade) ([Hands Unchained](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/hands-unchained-6kdf3); [Lean Silicon](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/lean-silicon-f134z); [Takarabako](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/takarabako-qmgg5)). Privacy moved the other way. As a primary tag it fell from 18 percent to 5 percent, and only about 3 of 53 finalists in 2026 build in a ZK proof system. It survives as a built-in property instead: TEE attestation, keys that never leave a device, client-side-only wallets.

One more trend matters for strategy: **the finalist room has come loose from sponsor prizes.** Through mid 2025, between one finalist in eight and one in five won no partner prize (21 of 101 from October 2023 to June 2024; 8 of 66 from July 2024 to February 2025; 10 of 45 from April to August 2025). From New Delhi on, the share is about half: 20 of 40, and 26 of 53 in 2026, although data for Open Agents and Tokyo may be incomplete. ETHGlobal's FAQ makes the separation formal: "The ETHGlobal Finalist Track is separate and does not count as a Partner Prize" ([ETHGlobal New Delhi](https://ethglobal.com/events/newdelhi)). Continuity entries also reached the finale once that track opened: "Continuity represented 26% of submissions and 3 of the 10 projects selected for the finale" at New York 2026 ([ETHGlobal blog](https://paragraph.com/@blog.ethglobal/inside-the-continuity-track-debut)).

## Finalists put one falsifiable claim on stage and prove it

Across all five research windows, the modal finalist pairs a legible flow with one hard, checkable primitive. A real judge's checklist, from a Citrea DevRel who judged Taipei 2025, explains why. He asks "What is this product?", "Is this concept new?" and "Is this just a copy of other products?", and he notes that judges "can't delve into every line of code" ([PANews](https://panews.io/articles/wojdx61h)). In seven minutes, a claim the room can repeat and watch being proven beats a richer one it cannot follow.

| Pattern element | What it looks like on stage | Finalist examples |
|---|---|---|

Attestation proves it's running." and "One line changes, the attestation breaks." The pattern also explains repeat finalists. The same builders reached the finale twice in 2026 by reusing one skill, falsifiable measurement in a niche they know. LPlens at Open Agents and TARE at ETHOnline 2026 share a GitHub owner, and both replay real Uniswap activity with labelled numbers. ENShell (Cannes) grew into Immunity (New York), whose page says it "merges concepts from my previous Cannes hackathon project ENShell" ([Immunity](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/immunity-eg56a)). For a security team, TARE is the closest model: a fork, a counterfactual, and a gap reported to the wei.

The pattern is the common route to a finalist slot, not the only one. A cluster of finalists won with a good live experience and a light crypto core: Smol Universe, Pomodoki, Pace Club, livestakes, PaintGlobal, Happy Hour and Accrue ([Pomodoki](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/pomodoki-oomhr); [Accrue](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/accrue-racfy)). Sponsor-themed online events (LFGHO, Frameworks, Agentic Ethereum, Unite DeFi, Open Agents) picked finalists entirely inside their theme. Broad platforms occasionally got through without a single moment, as Synapze and conduct.chat did ([conduct.chat](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/conduct-chat-9kzz0)). And ETHGlobal's rules add a structural penalty that cuts against Continuity-heavy entries: "Historically, projects that use a majority of pre-existing work do not score as high" ([ETHGlobal rules](https://ethglobal.com/rules)).

## India rewards a verifiable mechanism, not a local label

New Delhi 2025 is the closest precedent for Mumbai: ETHGlobal-run, Indian venue, 616 projects, 10 finalists ([ETHGlobal on X](https://x.com/ETHGlobal/status/1972276802270925116)). **No finalist mentions UPI, Aadhaar, INR or India anywhere in its showcase text.** At least 18 submissions used those terms in their names or taglines, and none placed. They included seven UPI or INR rails (CryptoUPI, PyPI, Unipay.exchange, ShieldRamp, Piggy Bit, uPAL, Nexus Pay), two UPI-style wallets (Pokket, Namaste Wallet), Aadhaar and DigiLocker identity tools (SewaChain, ZeroSurf, ZK-Digilocker, Wisk) and an on-chain chit fund, cheat.fund ([ShieldRamp](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/shieldramp-okrzw); [cheat.fund](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/cheat-fund-aqpyi)). The finalists that did place were technical builds aimed at global problems: ZK private perps (Nox), perps inside v4 hooks (UniPerp), EIP-7702 delegation (Igris AI), x402 payments (Autonome, ad-402), and one emerging-market angle, NoNet, which relays signed transactions over a Bluetooth mesh for areas with poor connectivity ([NoNet](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/nonet-xyebu)). ETHGlobal's own recap grouped the winners as "Privacy-first DeFi", "Offline and inclusive infrastructure" and "AI x crypto". It cited India's "900 million internet users" and does not mention UPI ([ETHGlobal recap](https://paragraph.com/@blog.ethglobal/hzEx3iDcZl6HwCxJvKZR)). Local identity ideas could still win partner money. ZeroSurf, a mobile browser with Anon-Aadhaar age proofs, took third in the EF's general privacy prize ([ZeroSurf](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zerosurf-9988k)), but the finalist room passed it over.

ETHIndia 2023 went the other way. At least two of its 13 finalists were framed for India. One was Bet On Balls, a cricket betting dapp ([Bet On Balls](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/bet-on-balls-2zxxr)). The other was **zkP2M**, a KYC-free INR on-ramp in which "Razorpay payment data is the source of truth, proven with TLSNotary", so the contract releases funds without trusting the counterparty ([zkP2M](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zkp2m-igbxk)). Two months later, priv.cast reached Circuit Breaker's finalists by using Anon Aadhaar as one component of private Farcaster polls; it was not the pitch ([priv.cast](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/priv-cast-hzitq)). Read together, the cases give a consistent lesson in three parts. First, **a local leg wins only once it is verifiable**: zkP2M placed because its off-chain Razorpay payment became something the contract could check, while ramp submissions whose fiat leg stays off-chain have nothing to prove on stage. Second, **the novelty decays fast**. Zeke made the 2024 Sydney finalists with the same idea applied to PayPal and Venmo ([Zeke](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/zeke-xarwm)), so by New Delhi, ShieldRamp's TEE web proofs were the third version of a known trick, competing with six other ramps at the same event. Third, **local problems work best as the setting for a global primitive**. NoNet (connectivity), Sippy (phone-number payments pitched at Latin America; [Sippy](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/sippy-2smms)) and Takarabako (leftover travel banknotes at a Tokyo kiosk) won with local stories carried by mechanisms that would work anywhere. Two further cautions apply. ETHIndia was run by Devfolio, so it is a weaker precedent for an ETHGlobal judging room ([Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/blog/inside-ethindia-2023-building-the-impossible-with-ethereum/)). And the EF Mandate warns that wrapping a government ID in a ZK proof does not make it CROPS by default, so an Aadhaar gate should be one signal among several ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf)). Neither the EF nor the Whitechain prize text mentions India ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)).

## Eleven Mumbai builds that fit the pattern and the prizes

The constraints decide what is buildable. Hacking runs from Thursday 5 November at 21:00 IST to submission at 09:00 IST on Saturday, and "Late submissions won't be accepted" ([Mumbai details](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/info/details)). The EF places each project in at most one of its prizes, and EF judges decide which. A project can pick three partner prizes, and a partner with several tracks counts once. The alt mempool prize runs on EF devnets, so it cannot share a product with Whitechain ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). And a video cut between 2:00 and 2:59 satisfies ETHGlobal's 2 to 4 minutes, the CROPS 3-minute cap and Whitechain's 2 to 4 minutes at once. The proposals below are original against the 320-finalist catalogue and the team's five plans. Each is aimed at the team's strengths (DeFi mechanics, adversarial testing, fork-based evidence) and at one open lane above. Difficulty and finalist odds are this report's judgement.

| 6 | **Tollgate**: a relayer judges cannot drain | Best CROPS App | Whitechain Scale and Operate | Medium | Low to moderate |
| 7 | **Bolo**: a counter speaker that only announces settled USDT | None | Whitechain Operate and Scale | Medium | Moderate |
| 8 | **Quiet Port**: Anon-RPC as a localhost endpoint for wallets, Foundry and agents | Anon-RPC | None | Medium | Low to moderate |
| 9 | **Spendguard**: a stealth wallet that refuses linking spends | Best CROPS App | None | Low to medium | Low to moderate |
| 10 | **RuleDiff**: differential fuzzer for alt mempool admission rules | Alt mempool | None | Medium | Low |
| 11 | **Blind Meter**: home nodes paid per read without learning who asked | Anon-RPC | None | High | Moderate (high risk) |

### Walkaway: leave a DeFi protocol without its team

**Promise.** With the protocol's website, its API and every RPC company blocked, you can still withdraw from Aave, a Morpho vault or a Uniswap position using one local file; where you cannot, Walkaway names the key or keeper that can stop you.

**20-second moment.** The team blocks app.aave.com and the major RPC hosts in the laptop's hosts file and opens walkaway.html from a USB stick. A real $5 mainnet position then leaves Aave, with reads arriving through Anon-RPC and checked by a light client, and the explorer confirms it. A second card turns red: on a fork with the vault's keeper switched off, the withdrawal queue never pays.

**Why it is not done.** The EF Mandate's walkaway test asks core layers to keep working "even if the Foundation and today's core developers disappeared tomorrow". The CROPS prize lists "a frontend that keeps working when its domain, backend or RPC provider disappears" ([Mandate PDF](https://ethereum.foundation/ef-mandate.pdf); [ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). No finalist exits third-party positions without their frontends. The nearest are UNSU, a wallet with no app or account ([UNSU](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/unsu-o1e71)), and Carte Bleue, recovery that needs nothing from a vendor. Plan A's Lightfoot shares the read stack but has no write path and no user.

**Prizes.** EF Best CROPS App, leading on C (censorship resistance). Because reads go through Anon-RPC by default, the EF may place it there instead. Uniswap Foundation's "Build with Uniswap" fits the v4 position exit, which needs a FEEDBACK.md and the feedback form. Whitechain does not fit.

**Difficulty: Medium.** Exits are raw calldata a DeFi team writes quickly. The risk is Colibri running through the Anon-RPC worker. Colibri documents a custom `fetch` "to route traffic through Tor" ([Colibri on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@corpus-core/colibri-stateless)), but nobody has shipped that composition. A two-endpoint cross-check is the fallback. Tor's 810 ms median latency on the official bench means the demo must be pre-warmed ([bench run](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/actions/runs/40303047893)).

### Truthsign: the signing screen must match the effect

**Promise.** Before a wallet clear-signs, Truthsign runs the transaction on a fork and proves that every field on the screen matches what the transaction actually does. If one field lies, the wallet refuses to clear-sign and shows a raw warning instead.

**20-second moment.** A descriptor maps "recipient" to the wrong argument. The left pane shows the signing screen: "Send 100 USDC to alice.eth". The right pane shows the fork result: 100 USDC lands at an attacker address. Truthsign blocks. The closing slide gives the number measured during the hackathon: how many public ERC-7730 descriptors disagree with their contracts under field-by-field fuzzing. Report zero if zero.

**Why it is not done.** Clear Signing still needs "accuracy verification and expert attestations" ([Clear Signing](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/05/12/clear-signing-announcement)). Every signing-safety finalist checks something else. Octopus AI pitched an AI co-signer against "Bybit-style blind-signing hacks" ([Octopus AI](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/octopus-ai-2xcne)), ENShell used ERC-7730 clear signing for its own verdicts, LeekDIYHardwareWallet refuses what it cannot display ([LeekDIYHardwareWallet](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/leekdiyhardwarewallet-5qssg)), Oh Snap! shows community notes before signing ([Oh Snap!](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/oh-snap-4u2kv)), and maki simulates and policy-checks each write ([maki](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/maki-564eg)). None checks the descriptor itself.

**Prizes.** EF Best CROPS App, leading on S, the Mandate's "Things must do what they claim to do, no more and no less". Shipping the check as a library that a wallet calls before rendering makes the property structural rather than a report. Chainlink CRE is optional, to run the registry-wide audit as a scheduled workflow; a simulation qualifies. Whitechain does not fit.

**Difficulty: Medium.** The method is differential fuzzing: mutate each displayed parameter and check that the rendered value and the effect on the fork move together. That is native work for a security team. The learning curve is the descriptor format.

### Lastgas: a loan that pays for its own rescue

**Promise.** A wallet holding zero ETH repays its own loan before liquidation. A paymaster approves only transactions that raise the position's health factor, and the collateral repays the paymaster in the same transaction.

**20-second moment.** On the devnet a position sits at health factor 1.03, and the team pushes the oracle price down. The canonical mempool rejects the rescue, because validation reads the lending pool's storage. The alternative mempool admits it. The explorer shows the health factor up, the account's ETH still at zero, and the paymaster repaid.

**Why it is not done.** The prize names "more flexible state access in sender or paymaster (e.g. ERC20 paymasters, daily quota paymasters)", and EIP-8141's public pool allows SLOAD only on the sender's storage ([EIP-8141](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8141)). The nearest prior work does something else. DeCredit Wallet (LFGHO 2024) flash-minted GHO to pay for purchases ([DeCredit Wallet](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/decredit-wallet-0su87)), BorrowBot and ALMA manage positions but need gas ([BorrowBot](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/borrowbot-o41nr)), and Plan D applies a self-repaying paymaster to Tornado withdrawals. No finalist makes collateral pay for its own rescue under a health-factor rule.

**Prizes.** EF alt mempool only.

**Difficulty: Medium-high.** The team deploys a minimal lending pool and oracle on the devnet. Validation has to stay under 100,000 gas: the team's own review of the sidecar source found that chain 1337 applies that cap before it even looks at stake ([sidecar repo](https://github.com/forshtat/mempool-workshop-sidecar)). Before writing code, confirm at the EF workshop which associated-storage reads the event devnets allow. The sentence "the wallet saved itself" is legible, but practicality on a devnet alone is the same weakness that cost Plan D.

### Tripwire: what you approved is checked at inclusion, not at signing

**Promise.** What you approved becomes a rule inside the transaction ("I lose at most 100 USDC and grant no approvals"). If the contract changes between your click and inclusion, the chain reverts.

**20-second moment.** The wallet simulates a swap and finds it safe. One block later, the attacker upgrades the router's proxy. In an ordinary wallet, the same signed call drains the account. Under Tripwire it reverts on-chain with "assertion failed: USDC change -10,000, allowed -100".

**Why it is not done.** The EF's October post says useful rules "must come from independently approved user intent, a standing account policy or protocol logic", and that "the wallet must ensure that every frame transaction it builds includes the required assertion". It closes with "we want to hear from the wallet and protocol teams who would use them" ([transaction assertions](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/05/transaction-assertions)). The guards among the finalists either check before signing (maki, ENShell, Aegis402, Octopus AI) or cap what an agent may spend in a contract (Omamori, ALMA's selector whitelist). None turns the effect the user approved into a post-condition checked at inclusion, which is exactly where time-of-check-to-time-of-use attacks happen ([Aegis402](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/aegis402-o8x6z); [Omamori](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/omamoris-rn3nc)).

**Prizes.** EF alt mempool if it is built as frame transactions on the devnets; if it ends up as an EIP-7702 delegate on Sepolia, it fits Best CROPS App instead. No partner fits naturally.

**Difficulty: High.** The gating fact is whether the devnet build supports EIP-7906, or at least makes a failing later frame revert the earlier ones. Ask at the EF workshop before writing code; if neither holds, build the 7702 variant.

### Hundi: an invoice that can be financed exactly once

**Promise.** An invoice mints only with the buyer's signed acknowledgement, and a second financing attempt reverts. On the due date the buyer's USDT goes to whoever holds the invoice, not to the seller.

**20-second moment.** The seller tries to sell the same invoice to a second financier, and the mint reverts with "already financed". On the due date, the buyer pays by passkey through EIP-3009, and the explorer shows the prize USDT going to the investor.

**Why it is not done.** RWA is the emptiest finalist category, the primary tag of only 4 of 320. explorador.pt and Kura are its only 2026 entries ([explorador.pt](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/explorador-pt-35okq); [Kura](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/kura-so3wx)), and invoice3.xyz (LFGHO 2024) used borrowed GHO to pay invoices but financed none ([invoice3.xyz](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/invoice3-xyz-3fb87)). Whitechain Tokenize names invoice financing and wants "Real transfer rules enforced in the contract" ([ETHGlobal Mumbai prizes](https://ethglobal.com/events/mumbai/prizes)). The name comes from the traditional Indian bill of exchange. This is the proposal where an Indian frame is carried by a mechanism, which is the New Delhi lesson applied.

**Prizes.** Whitechain Tokenize. Whitechain's own rule sends financing there: "Financing or selling an invoice belongs in Tokenize". For the second partner, either World IDKit for the investor allowlist (it rewards "minimum sufficient assurance") or Chainlink CRE for the due-date job (simulation suffices). No EF prize.

**Difficulty: Medium.** The binary risk is the prize USDT, which has no documented faucet; Plan C faces the same risk. The token supports EIP-3009, which makes gasless settlement possible ([Blockscout implementation](https://explorer.testnet.whitechain.io/api/v2/smart-contracts/0x26425662d126412128967ecf0a02ec48573026f7)). Honest limit: uniqueness binds a buyer and an invoice number, so a colluding buyer can still sign fake invoices.

### Tollgate: a gas relayer the judges can attack

**Promise.** Attack the relayer however you like: every request either pays its own gas in USDT inside the same transaction or never reaches the chain. If the relayer dies, the payer can broadcast the same signature themselves.

**20-second moment.** A judge runs a script that fires 1,000 hostile requests: bad signatures, reused nonces, and transfers built to revert. The relayer's WBT balance chart stays flat. Then a passkey payment settles in about a second.

**Why it is not done.** Whitechain Scale asks "how your relayer gets its WBT, and what stops someone from draining it". Paybot and router402 ran facilitators that pay gas, but neither page describes an adversarial test of its facilitator. Plan C's Whitechain review found its relayer "safe from theft but easy to take offline". The EF's payments domain asks that "Relayer- and paymaster-based flows need a user-broadcastable equivalent" ([payments domain](https://github.com/ethereum/access-layer-resources/blob/HEAD/domains/payments.md)).

**Prizes.** Whitechain Scale and Operate; EF Best CROPS App, leading on C.

**Difficulty: Medium.** The faucet gives only 0.5 WBT a day, so the refill path (USDT fees swapped into WBT) has to be real. Honest limit: a sequencer-level race on the same authorization can still make the relayer pay for a revert, bounded by per-payer rate limits. It is strongest as Plan C's relayer module.

### Bolo: the counter speaker that cannot be faked

**Promise.** The counter speaker announces a payment only after USDT has settled on Whitechain to this shop. The box checks this itself, so a fake "payment successful" screen or a replayed QR code never makes it speak.

**20-second moment.** A customer pays 5 USDT by fingerprint, and the box announces it aloud in Hindi and English. A "scammer" then shows a doctored success screen and rescans yesterday's QR. The box stays silent.

**Why it is not done.** Takarabako won Tokyo with local cash hardware and an invariant the kiosk enforces. QuickPay and ETHPark-QR put crypto behind local QR rails in 2024, but both built only the payer side ([QuickPay](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/quickpay-rxu7v); [ETHPark-QR](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/ethpark-qr-gvj0t)). No finalist built the merchant's counter device. The design borrows the payment speaker boxes common at Indian shop counters; that is general knowledge, not from the notes.

**Prizes.** Whitechain Operate, giving a merchant view without a dashboard and QR invoices bound to an order nonce, plus Scale, where the payer sees no crypto. No EF prize.

**Difficulty: Medium.** The box is a Raspberry Pi or ESP32 with a speaker, or an old Android phone as a fallback. Honest limit: the box trusts Whitechain's single sequencer and two RPC endpoints, not a light client. Technicality will be its weakest score.

### Quiet Port: Anon-RPC as an endpoint, not a library

**Promise.** Point MetaMask, Foundry's `cast` or a local AI agent at `localhost:8545`, and every read leaves through Tor or Nym with no code change. If the anonymity network dies, the endpoint returns errors rather than falling back to a direct connection.

**20-second moment.** `cast balance` and an agent's MCP tool call both go through the endpoint. An IP echo through the same path returns a Tor exit. The team stops the gateway, and the agent's next call fails closed on screen.

**Why it is not done.** The Node harness is "Not yet a conforming harness" and has no WebAssembly, so every real worker runs only in browsers ([node-harness README](https://github.com/ethereum/anon-rpc/blob/main/impl/node-harness/README.md)). Vitalik asked for "an internet search skill that wraps around Tor or other internet anonymization" for local agents ([secure LLMs](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2026/04/02/secure_llms.html)). The agent tools among the finalists (TARE, LPlens, OnchainRouter, Cordon) all call RPCs directly.

**Prizes.** EF Best Use of Anon-RPC, under its listed idea "new harness".

**Difficulty: Medium.** The work is sandboxing WASM workers in Deno or Electron. Worker boot takes 8 to 11 seconds. This is also the cleanest pivot for Plan A.

### Spendguard: refuse the spends that unmask stealth users

**Promise.** A stealth-address wallet that refuses the spending habits which let researchers trace 38.92% of withdrawn stealth addresses ([ephemeral.money](https://ephemeral.money/docs/research/method)).

**20-second moment.** The team spends from a stealth address the usual way, with gas topped up from the main wallet, and the published heuristic links the two addresses in one click. With Spendguard, the same spend is refused ("gas from a linked address"), and the gasless path leaves the heuristic empty.

**Why it is not done.** Kohaku's receive-side withdrawal rules H1 to H5 come with 18 conformance vectors, but no wallet implements them ([Kohaku issue #324](https://github.com/ethereum/kohaku/issues/324)). How Doxxed Am I? reported exposure without enforcing anything ([How Doxxed Am I?](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/how-doxxed-am-i-ftbux)). SQUIDL, POAPrivacy and GrimSwap create stealth outputs but leave spending hygiene to the user.

**Prizes.** EF Best CROPS App, leading on P.

**Difficulty: Low to medium.** Demo only on the team's own addresses: replaying the de-anonymization of real users on stage is an ethics problem. This also works as a strong feature inside Plan C.

### RuleDiff: fuzz the mempool against its own spec

**Promise.** RuleDiff turns the ERC-8421 draft into an executable rulebook, fuzzes the devnet's admission node against it, and attaches a reproducing transaction to every disagreement.

**20-second moment.** A divergence counter climbs. Clicking one entry replays a staked paymaster that was refused at 100,000 gas, although the draft gives staked entities 1M.

**Why it is not done.** The prize's third area asks for "DoS vectors and vulnerabilities, new rules, simulators and fuzzers". The devnet sidecar enforces a rule set its own workshop calls "not equivalent" to ERC-8421 ([workshop README](https://github.com/forshtat/mempool-workshop)), and the organizers' docs quote budgets of 250k and 2.5M where the draft says 100k and 1M ([docs: differences](https://github.com/forshtat/mempool-workshop-documentation/blob/HEAD/src/content/docs/project/differences.md)). None of the 320 finalists targeted mempool rules.

**Prizes.** EF alt mempool.

**Difficulty: Medium.** The jargon makes it a poor finalist bet, but its EF odds are high, and the team's simulated alt mempool reviewers said they would weigh a mergeable sidecar fix heavily.

### Blind Meter: pay home nodes without telling them who asked

**Promise.** A home node is paid for every read it serves, through anonymous prepaid credits. Its own log shows that it was paid, but not who asked.

**20-second moment.** A judge's phone reads through Anon-RPC from the team's laptop node. Earnings tick up on the operator screen, while the request log and the redeemed-credit log cannot be joined.

**Why it is not done.** The EF Reads team says operators "need a reason to serve requests" but "We do not design the incentive mechanism" ([Reads about](https://reads.ethereum.foundation/about)). zkAPI lists "Blockchain RPC" as a use case for anonymous prepaid credits ([zkAPI](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/10/01/introducing-zkapi)). router402 and OnchainRouter charge per call, but both tie the payer to the request ([OnchainRouter](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/onchainrouter-8r4jm)).

**Prizes.** EF Anon-RPC, as a new worker plus an app.

**Difficulty: High.** The notes never checked how mature zkAPI's tooling is, so this is a wildcard.

The portfolio logic is simple. A team that wants a finalist slot and an EF prize from one build should pick **Walkaway or Truthsign**. A team set on the alt mempool should pick **Lastgas**, which is legible, or **Tripwire**, which has a higher ceiling and higher risk. A team optimizing for Whitechain should take **Hundi**, or keep Plan C and absorb Tollgate and Spendguard as modules.

**E is the only plan shaped like the 2026 finalists.** It has one binary moment, tied to stakes every judge knows, and a demo the team controls. Its overlap with prior finalists is a matter of framing, not mechanism. Its stage line echoes VEIL VPN's "Open source lets you read the code" almost word for word, which any judge from Cannes will notice. And npmguard already reached the Cannes top 10 by attacking the same software-supply-chain problem from the package side ([npmguard](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/npmguard-aeihd)). The fixes are to write its own line, open and close on the tamper beat, and answer the debugger-permission question before a judge asks it.

**B is the most legible plan, but it sits in a genre that is already finalist-proven.** How Doxxed Am I? reached Taipei's finalists by reporting what a wallet reveals, and Proof of Scan reached New York's by turning scans into TLSNotary evidence ([Proof of Scan](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/proof-of-scan-trx38)). B's canary address is the genuinely new part. It still needs TARE's discipline: a pre-verified, surprising finding on a famous dapp, and every red row backed by an artifact anyone can replay.

**A, C and D are prize entries.** A's own panel ranks it as the EF Access Layer's favourite, and its best path to a finalist slot is to ship the drop-in provider as the product (Quiet Port is that pivot). C's mechanics are already finalist-proven by GrimSwap (browser Groth16 in about one second, ERC-5564 stealth outputs, gas paid by a relayer) and by SQUIDL (stealth payment links), so its novelty has to come from the accountant key and gasless stealth spending, and its demo has to drop to three beats ([GrimSwap](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/grimswap-ntdmp); [SQUIDL](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/squidl-psquk)). D faces two problems. Its alt mempool review found that the chain 1337 sidecar applies the flat 100,000-gas cap to staked payers too, so D must confirm before the event that some devnet enforces per-entity budgets. Its headline also repeats work that MacWha's privacy paymaster and Tokyo's Himitsu have already shown. If the gate fails, Lastgas or RuleDiff reuse D's paymaster skills on claims that need no budget above 100k.

Three other finalists set bars the plans should borrow. Carte Bleue reached Lisbon's top 10 with "no live demo by design", which shows that an honest constraint stated up front costs less than a fragile live beat. maki's "The model interprets intent but never moves funds" is the standard any signing-safety sentence must match. TARE shows that a measurement wins when every number is replayable to the wei, which is what D's projected fan-out numbers and B's findings both lack today.

## Conclusion

Three years of finalists show that novelty has a short half-life. ZK-proven fiat ramps went from the finalist round (zkP2M, 2023) to non-placement among seven lookalikes (New Delhi, 2025). x402 went from one Prague finalist to the core of 9 of 40 within six months. The most reliable source of originality is therefore timing. The blob finalists of March and April 2024 won by using a capability only weeks old. At Mumbai the equivalent capabilities are just as fresh: the alt mempool devnets with keyed nonces and recent roots, Anon-RPC spec 0.4.0, the EF's assertions request of 5 October, zkAPI from 1 October, and Clear Signing descriptors that nobody yet verifies. A team that builds on one of these and proves a single claim with it is competing against an empty field rather than against 2026's crowded agent lanes.

The second implication is organizational. With about half of recent finalists winning no partner prize, the finalist demo and the partner submission are two different products that can share one codebase. The demo is a 4-minute single beat for generalists. The submission is a README and a 2:00 to 2:59 video written in EF or Whitechain vocabulary. A DeFi and security team's edge in that split is the evidence style judges have rewarded most consistently in 2026: a fork, a counterfactual and a number anyone can rerun. Applied to the open lanes (exits, signing truth, collateral-funded gas, single-financing invoices), that style turns the team's audit instincts into the kind of claim judges remember.
