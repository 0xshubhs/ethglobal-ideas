Written 2026-10-31 for ETHGlobal Mumbai (5 to 7 Nov 2026). Team context: 3 people, DeFi and smart contract security, Classic track, fresh build only. The Arc Continuity track is out of scope and is mentioned only where it explains a pattern.

- Prize text comes from the embedded Next.js data of each sponsor prize page, fetched on 2026-10-31 with pauses between requests: `https://ethglobal.com/events/<slug>/prizes/arc` for HackMoney 2026, Cannes 2026, New York 2026 and ETHOnline 2026, and `https://ethglobal.com/events/buenosaires/prizes/circle` for Buenos Aires 2025 (Arc's first tracks ran under the Circle sponsor page). Projects seen: Buenos Aires 473, HackMoney 618, Cannes 2026 265, New York 2026 232, ETHOnline 2026 811.

### 4.2 What separates winners from these (Judgement, with the evidence)

1. **A contract on Arc that holds USDC and makes the decision.** Winners almost always have a named Arc contract that holds funds and enforces a rule: `ProofGatedEscrow` (OpenPop), `MarsEscrow` (Mars), ERC-8183 escrow with `SlaNotMet` (OpenBook), `LoanVault` plus `TranchePool` (Vouch), `GroupTreasury` (C.E.S.T.A), Onda's claim escrow, `PayrollVaultV2` (Manila). Entries that only pass payments through Arc, such as routers and paywalls (OnchainRouter, Xpack, Spigot), lost the agentic tracks to escrow-holding projects, even when they were finalists. An escrow is necessary but not sufficient: [Clawback](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/clawback-vpmw2) (a `ClawbackEscrow` USDC state machine funded by `receiveWithAuthorization`, with replay-safe deal ids), [arbiter](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/arbiter-qbw5w) (a non-upgradeable task escrow with challenge and timeout settlement) and [Verified Agent Rails](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/verified-agent-rails-2rgy5) (per-transaction and per-period caps enforced in a token's `_update` hook) all had careful contracts and won nothing from Arc. The differences I can see: they were generic primitives with no named counterparty or market (task escrow, agent escrow), Verified Agent Rails gated its own `GatedUSD` token rather than USDC itself, and none put a newly shipped Circle product at the centre. On the prediction-market side, [Prediction FX](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/prediction-fx-um0su) says its Chainlink feed is "mocked currently" and [BulwArc](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/bulwarc-9uoni) falls back to a manually updated oracle; a mocked core dependency looks like a loss factor.
2. **Several Circle products composed, with the new one at the centre.** arctan(x) used Wallets, Gateway, CCTP and Bridge Kit, and StableFX; The Wolf of Web3 used Wallets, CCTP, Bridge Kit and Gateway; Thurman used `depositForBurnWithHook` with the Forwarding Service. At Cannes, "4 of the 6 winning projects built their core value proposition around nanopayments", the product Circle had just launched ([Cannes recap](https://www.arc.io/blog/meet-the-arc-track-winners-from-ethglobal-cannes-hackathon-and-what-we-learned)). Sidekick said its design "was [not] buildable until gas-free batched nanopayments shipped": winning copy ties the idea to the new primitive.
3. **Finance-native problems told in institutional vocabulary.** FX stablepair perps, senior and junior tranches, delivery versus payment, invoice financing, secondary loan sales, PSP prefunding, ERC-3643 capital markets, transaction cost analysis. Arc's About text ("onchain lending, capital markets, FX, and payments") is the vocabulary judges reuse.
4. **A visible guarantee.** Cumulant's solvency fuzz invariants, OpenBook's revert, Manila's three-verdict policy engine, Mars's slash, VEIL's attestation. This matches the general finalist pattern in `00_season.md` section 3 (a refusal on stage, a replayable number).
5. **Stacking is normal.** 16 of the 30 winners also won at least one other partner prize (ETHGlobal finalist badges not counted); Chainlink CRE appears in five Arc winners (OpenPop, Vouch, PayMate, Sidekick, and Thurman on Continuity). Arc judges do not penalise multi-sponsor builds as long as Arc does real work.
6. **Social impact and non-crypto users earn a place.** Text-to-Chain (feature phones), ETHastic (blackouts), Onda (artists who are not in crypto), Omma Cash and cellfi before them. Arc's HackMoney recap quotes a user: "They don't care about blockchain."
7. **Fit to the bullets beats polish when fields are small.** Predict It! won 1st at Cannes as a self-described vibecoded solo project in a 69-team pool spread over 4 tracks. Buenos Aires winners OneTx and Coco were mostly Uniswap and 1inch builds that settled in USDC on Arc. In thin tracks the bar is "a working thing that matches the bullet"; in crowded tracks (agentic, 98% of Cannes entries) the bar is points 1 to 4.
8. **Generic crosschain is no longer enough.** "This infrastructure is becoming expected rather than differentiating" (HackMoney recap); the Liquidity Hub track is gone at Mumbai.

## 6. Mumbai product facts the ideas depend on (sourced)

- **Arc Studio** "builds and deploys to Arc testnet, so you can ship and iterate safely (it does not deploy to mainnet on your behalf)"; it can "Build on existing contracts", "Start from a template" and "Push to GitHub" ([Arc Studio docs](https://docs.arc.io/ai/arc-studio.md)). The **Arc Studio CLI** (`npm install -g @circle-fin/arc-studio-cli`) runs turns from a terminal or as a Claude Code subagent (`arc-studio skills install --tool claude-code`); "The sandbox is a Vite and Foundry app, so Solidity belongs under `contracts/` and web code under `src/`"; `--file a.sol:contracts/a.sol` round-trips your own file with real diffs; "a contract deploy can take 5 to 20 minutes"; `arc-studio pull` brings files back ([Arc Studio CLI docs](https://docs.arc.io/ai/arc-studio-cli.md)). Circle warns generated code can be wrong and must be reviewed ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/circle-launches-arc-studio-ai-agent-for-building-onchain-apps/)).
- **Discovery API**: `GET https://api.circle.com/v2/x402/discovery/resources`, "No API key, no auth, no Circle account"; filters `category`, `network`, `maxUsdPrice`, `supportsCircleGateway`, `type`, `query` ([Circle blog, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.circle.com/blog/discover-the-whole-agent-marketplace-in-one-call)). My calls on 2026-10-31 returned `total: 2839` listings; `network=eip155:5042` (Arc mainnet) 2185; `supportsCircleGateway=true` 2070; by category SOCIAL_INTELLIGENCE 1074, WEB_SEARCH_RESEARCH 627, FINANCIAL_ANALYSIS 457, PREDICTION_MARKETS 84, CREATIVE 82; `maxUsdPrice=0.001` 225; `type=mcp` 0; Arc testnet (`network=eip155:5042002`) 134; Arc mainnet with Gateway and `maxUsdPrice=0.01` 277 (for example Exa at 1000 atomic units, $0.001). Each listing carries `accepts[].payTo`, `amount`, `asset` and an input JSON schema. Arc mainnet is chain 5042 and testnet 5042002 ([Bitquery](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/blockchain/arc-mainnet/)).
- **Nanopayments**: buyers deposit into a Gateway Wallet once, then sign EIP-3009 authorizations offchain; Gateway settles in batches; supported on "Arc, Base, Polygon PoS", with the `upto` scheme on those chains. "Gateway Nanopayments and x402 batch settlement require EOA signatures and do not support ERC-1271." ([Circle Nanopayments docs](https://developers.circle.com/gateway/nanopayments.md)). Agent Wallet spending policies "apply to mainnet agent wallets only" ([Circle docs](https://developers.circle.com/agent-stack/agent-wallets/wallet-operations/custom-policies.md)).
- **Bridge Kit and CCTP for non-USDC**: "`bridge` and `estimate` are not limited to USDC ... passing a non-USDC symbol such as `cirBTC`, `wETH` or `EURC`, or a bytes32 `0x...` token id, bridges that token via CCTPx" ([Bridge Kit 1.17.0 README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@circle-fin/bridge-kit@1.17.0/README.md)). CCTPx routes take `feePayment` only as source and `useForwarder` only as true ([changelog](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@circle-fin/bridge-kit@1.17.0/CHANGELOG.md)). Four integration paths: a configured token, a custom token (issuer keeps "rate limits, denylist, pause, and upgrade controls"), an ownerless token, or a new `CrossChainToken` deployed by CREATE3 ([CCTP for non-USDC](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/expanded-assets.md)). Domains: Ethereum 0, Avalanche 1, OP 2, Arbitrum 3, Base 6, Polygon 7, Arc Testnet 26; EVM only; trust must be configured on both ends ([supported chains](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/expanded-assets/concepts/supported-chains-and-domains.md)). Hooks run "in the same transaction as token delivery. If the hook reverts, the entire transfer reverts"; fast (1000) finality hooks must tolerate rollback; "Iris does not relay transfers that carry hook data", so hook transfers need self-relay ([Hooks](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/expanded-assets/concepts/hooks.md)). Setup traps the docs name: the `TokenManager` needs the minter role, "`rateLimit: 0` blocks all outbound transfers", configuring an ownerless token on more than one chain "will lock supply", and the per-token denylist provider "replaces, it does not chain" ([custom token](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/expanded-assets/quickstarts/configure-custom-token.md), [ownerless token](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/expanded-assets/quickstarts/configure-ownerless-token.md), [contract reference](https://developers.circle.com/cctp/expanded-assets/references/contract-reference.md)). The sandbox registry (`iris-api-sandbox.circle.com/v2/cctpx/tokens`) already lists 150+ test tokens, many named "Arc BTC".
- **Arc EVM differences**: USDC is native gas with an 18-decimal native interface and a 6-decimal ERC-20 interface sharing one balance; `PREVRANDAO` returns 0; "Transfers to the zero address are forbidden"; "The blocklist is enforced at runtime. A value transfer to or from a blocklisted address reverts"; "Sending native value to a contract isn't guaranteed to succeed"; `SELFDESTRUCT` moves a contract's USDC out ([EVM differences](https://docs.arc.io/arc/references/evm-differences.md)).

## 7. What Arc's judges reward now (Judgement)

1. Compliance first: Arc Studio project URL, Studio visible in the video, frontend plus backend, an architecture diagram, detailed docs, and the bounty named. Past texts said judging was "alignment with bounty requirements, quality of solution, depth of technical implementation" (Cannes recap); with one winner per track, a missed requirement is fatal.
2. A USDC- or EURC-holding contract on Arc that makes a decision you can watch.
3. The newest Circle primitive at the centre, framed as "only possible now".
4. Finance-native framing and real users (businesses, non-crypto consumers, social impact).
5. Depth over breadth, but composing 2 to 4 Circle products is the norm among winners.
6. Feedback on Circle tooling was weighted at HackMoney and is plausibly valued again because Studio is new; a short, specific Studio feedback section is cheap insurance.

## 8. Lessons for the Mumbai ideas (Judgement)

- Use Arc Studio for real work (contracts, tests and the UI scaffold) and keep the prompts and diffs in the repo; that meets both Arc's rule and ETHGlobal's AI attribution rule.
- Pick a track where the team's Solidity and fuzzing depth shows: Advanced Stablecoin Logic (escrows, splitters, reusable contracts) and Bridge Kit non-USDC (a new, thin field with documented misconfiguration traps).
- In the consumer agent track the field will be crowded; the winning entry will be the one whose limits are enforced by something the agent cannot override, shown on a phone.
- Avoid pure routers, paywalls and generic crosschain UX.
